Our mission

Make trust part of the decision

A convincing website is not enough to choose a vendor. Buyers also need to understand who is behind the company, what its product actually does and what evidence supports its claims. Spam, exaggerated promises and providers that are not relevant to a project make that decision harder.

Our mission is to help people look beyond the pitch. Through vendor reviews, comparisons and research, FluidRWA helps buyers ask better questions, assess evidence and understand whether a provider fits their needs.

For vendors, the Vetted badge is a way to show that a FluidRWA review has been completed and approved. Appearing in the directory alone does not mean a company has been vetted.

Our vision

One place to bridge the trust gap

We want FluidRWA to become a trusted starting point for the Web3 and AI industry: one place where buyers can discover relevant vendors, examine the evidence behind them and make a more informed choice about who to work with.

Trust is the reason we are building FluidRWA, not an extra feature. Our aim is to make credible information easier to find and to give companies with substance a clearer way to demonstrate it.

Vendor review standard

Six areas behind a more informed review

A credible review starts with the service a company actually provides. Our review considers six areas, with the evidence requested matched to the vendor's product, business model and claims. Not every provider operates a blockchain, holds client money or requires a financial-services licence.

  1. Product and operating track record

    Review the actual service, documentation and evidence of delivery. For blockchain products, ask for relevant production deployments and transaction evidence; for AI and other services, use appropriate operating examples.

  2. Client work and references

    Examine relevant case studies and supporting references where available. Distinguish a named client engagement from an integration, ecosystem membership or partner logo.

  3. Regulatory claims and service scope

    Check claimed registrations or permissions against the named entity, activity and jurisdiction where applicable. A software vendor does not need the same permissions as a custodian or payment operator.

  4. Leadership and company identity

    Review the people behind the business, its corporate identity and the entity responsible for the proposed service. Separate a group brand from the company entering the contract.

  5. Website, documentation and ecosystem presence

    Check whether the public product description, documentation, company channels and supporting materials are consistent and relevant to the service being reviewed.

  6. Feedback and reputation

    Consider client feedback, references and material reputation concerns in context. Testimonials and a lack of public complaints are not guarantees of service quality or future performance.
