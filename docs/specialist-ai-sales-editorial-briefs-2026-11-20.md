## Material findings before writing

- **AiSDR:** its current public pricing states $1.50 per person prospected for pay-as-you-go, with subscription arrangements also offered. Do not reuse an old flat-monthly-plan comparison without checking the actual offer. A synthetic 100-person example would be $150 at that unit price, before any separately agreed scope; it says nothing about meetings or leads produced.
- **Topo:** the public Team offer lists $129 per seat per month, from three seats, and 1,600 credits per seat per month. Three seats gives a $387 monthly base calculation, not an all-in negotiated quote. The page places Salesforce in the Enterprise section; do not assume it is included in Team.
- **Unify:** the pricing page lists Free, Base and Pro options, with Pro at $60 per seat per month and 2,400 credits. Pro's HubSpot/Salesforce sync is described as read-only; read-write is listed under Business. This distinction materially changes an automation comparison.
- **Octave:** the public model combines a flat annual platform subscription and usage credits, with unlimited users listed. No numeric base price was established in this review. Do not turn this into a per-seat price or call it free.
- **Persana:** the company's [March 30, 2026 announcement](https://persana.ai/blogs/exciting-news-our-next-chapter) says its team is joining Rox. Hold a standalone Persana comparison until the current product, contracting and support arrangement is confirmed. This is a stronger reason to defer than simply preferring a newer logo.
- **Firmable:** its homepage advertises a Data API waitlist alongside other access methods. Confirm availability product by product; a visible API menu item is not evidence that a production API contract is available today.

## First article

**AiSDR vs Topo vs Agent Frank: Who Owns the Outbound Workflow?**

Audience: a small technical B2B sales team considering delegated prospecting, not an enterprise CRM replacement. Minimum 3,000 substantive words. Open with a reply arriving from a prospect while nobody knows whether the agent or a person should respond.

Required sections:

1. A direct answer by operating model, without declaring a universal winner.
2. A product-scope table separating research, sequencing, sending, reply handling, CRM writes and human approval.
3. The documented offers and plan boundaries above, with a fresh pricing check before publication.
4. A synthetic matched account sample: same ICP, region, exclusions and desired offer. No real outreach is sent during research.
5. The handoff demonstration: positive reply, unclear reply, opt-out, wrong-company result and unsupported factual claim.
6. Cost model covering the platform, seats, contacts, credits, mail infrastructure and operator time.
7. Privacy, permissions, suppression, audit records and exit exports.
8. A 30-day pilot scorecard, practical buyer questions, visible FAQs and primary sources.

Original value: a responsibility matrix and a downloadable evaluation sheet. Distinguish proposed test criteria from observed product results. Do not label this a hands-on review without actually running authorized trials.

## Second article

**Unify vs Octave: Sales Execution or GTM Context?**

Audience: a RevOps owner deciding whether their bottleneck is acting on prospects or producing reliable account context. Minimum 3,000 substantive words. These are not assumed interchangeable products.

Develop a fictional account change from discovery through context update, task creation, outreach approval, reply handling and CRM reconciliation. Identify which product or existing system owns every step. Use the current CRM tier boundary and the platform-plus-usage distinction. Cover integration scope, context corrections, access permissions, stale claims, source attribution, total costs, implementation and exit. Include a section explaining when buying both would add complexity without solving the team's actual bottleneck.

## Third article

**AI SDR Pricing: Cost per Accepted Opportunity, Not Emails Sent**

Audience: a founder comparing several incompatible billing units. Minimum 3,000 substantive words. Use clearly synthetic scenarios and current public offers, not invented conversion benchmarks.

Compare per-seat, per-contact, credit and platform-plus-usage models. Show the denominator ladder: researched accounts, contacted people, delivered messages, replies, booked meetings, held meetings, sales-accepted opportunities and qualified enquiries. Keep each measure separate. Include a worksheet with editable assumptions, operator time, recurring infrastructure, low-volume months, expansion, cancellation and data export. A holdout group helps assess incrementality; attribution alone does not establish it.

## Pilot acceptance and publication gate

Keep a frozen prospect/account sample and a list of prohibited claims. Check suppression before outreach, require human approval for the initial pilot, and assign a single owner for replies. Track qualified outcomes and false personalization separately. Review applicable privacy and marketing requirements with the responsible team; this brief does not authorize mass sending or a legal conclusion.
