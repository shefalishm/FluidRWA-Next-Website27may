# Citation recovery release

## What This Release Does

Updates 17 existing blog articles and the cost-basis operating use case. It does not create competing duplicate URLs, fabricate product tests, purchase placement or promise citation recovery. The exact changed URLs and 14 frozen retest prompts are in data/citation-recovery-release-2026-11-19.json.

The five first priorities are cost-basis automation, development firms, on-ramps, institutional staking, and custody/settlement. Related query topics and the highly cited Talos, Alloy, fund-platform and smart-contract comparisons receive tailored workflow additions too.

## Full Export Inventory

docs/citation-page-inventory-2026-11-19.json inventories all 182 URLs in the supplied November 16 Bing page export, including 127 blog URLs. All 182 resolve to a local source, a built production route or a configured redirect. This is not proof that they are indexed, live-cited or returning 200 now.

Before this release, 61 of the blog sources were below 1,000 approximate source words. Length is a triage flag, not a quality verdict. The report preserves current and baseline counts, source-link counts, local FAQ detection and unknown loss deltas. It does not label every historically cited page a confirmed casualty.

The CSV has no selected-period metadata or daily page rows. Its totals cannot establish which individual pages lost citations on November 3. Paired before/after page exports are required for that conclusion; no cumulative-total subtraction or rate estimate was used.
