# FluidRWA SEO and AEO execution review

Date: September 16, 2026

## Executive verdict

FluidRWA does not primarily have an indexing problem. It has a click-through, differentiation and commercial-page authority problem.

The site already earns meaningful visibility, including page-one positions for comparison content. However, too many result titles are long and generic, too many articles share templated procurement language, and the strongest-ranking editorial pages do not move buyers into the corresponding directories quickly enough.

The attached teardown is directionally useful, but it overstates what the data proves. Search Console cannot establish that malformed or long-tail queries were generated by AI systems. Those queries may reflect automated searches, query fan-out, rank tracking, browser tooling or other non-human behavior. Treat them as suspicious traffic, not proven AI demand.

## What the exports actually show

- Search Console overview: 40,405 impressions, 80 clicks, 0.20% CTR and average position 15.6 for September 7-13.
- Page export: 235 of 266 URLs received no clicks.
- Query export: 480 reported queries ranked at position 10 or better with zero clicks, representing 7,212 impressions.
- Desktop CTR was 0.15%, compared with 0.72% on mobile.
- `/blog/top-tokenization-companies-2026` produced 25 clicks from 3,609 impressions at position 6.68.
- `/vendors/crypto-custody-providers` produced one click from 4,531 impressions at position 18.93.
- `/vendors/tokenization-platforms` produced three clicks from 3,671 impressions at position 31.53.
- The fiat-ramp comparison produced one click from 2,488 impressions at position 8.71. Its largest reported query is unusually formatted and should not be treated as normal buyer demand without further validation.
- The query export contains only the top 1,000 rows and excludes anonymized query data. Its 16 attributed clicks are not the site's total clicks.
- The Network Explorer preview generated 110 GA4 views from only two active users. That is internal or test activity, not customer demand.

## Brutal assessment

1. Publishing more articles in the same template would make the problem worse. Twenty-five comparison articles reused the same long procurement framework, and 75 vendor sections repeated the same verification paragraph.
2. Schema volume was not the constraint. The site already had Article, FAQ, ItemList, Organization and breadcrumb data. FAQ rich results are not a realistic growth lever for this type of site.
3. Several titles were too long to communicate a reason to click before truncation. A brand suffix also consumed space without adding a decision signal.
4. Directory pages have commercial intent but weaker rankings than editorial comparisons. Articles need to pass readers directly into a relevant directory, not a generic ecosystem page.
5. The strongest opportunity is to make FluidRWA's comparison data more distinctive: explicit best fit, avoid-if conditions, counts, review ownership, source links and verified update dates.
6. The GA4 export is contaminated by preview use and near-all-new-user behavior. Conversion and returning-user trends should not be interpreted until internal and preview activity is filtered.

## Fixes implemented

- Rewrote titles and descriptions for 11 high-impression comparison articles and five commercial directories.
- Kept result titles between 42 and 57 characters and descriptions between 121 and 144 characters.
- Moved the short answer above the article image on every comparison article.
- Added a visible editorial review strip with FluidRWA Research Team, primary sources where available and a direct link to the relevant vendor directory.
- Added FluidRWA Research Team as the reviewer organization in Article structured data, retaining the separate author details.
- Added primary-source citation URLs to the structured data for priority articles that previously lacked them.
- Removed repeated procurement boilerplate from the 25 templated comparison pages while preserving unique comparison tables, vendor analysis, best-fit scenarios, sources and FAQs.
- Replaced the removed boilerplate with a concise five-step shortlist validation checklist.
- Removed stale table-of-contents links after the duplicate sections were removed.
- Updated directory result titles to use the actual on-page counts: 11 tokenization platforms, 28 custody providers, 30 blockchain development companies, 29 fiat ramps and 30 stablecoin providers.
- Updated directory modification dates and sitemap dates to September 16, 2026.
- Added `noindex, nofollow` to both Network Explorer preview routes.

## Institutional website benchmark

The editorial and visual recommendations were checked against the live websites of [Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/), [Ripple](https://ripple.com/), [Tokeny](https://tokeny.com/), [Ondo](https://ondo.finance/), [Fireblocks](https://www.fireblocks.com/) and [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/) on September 16, 2026.

The useful pattern is not a particular font, color or animation. It is a disciplined information hierarchy:

- Coinbase uses a short category statement, one plain-language promise and one primary action.
- Ripple leads with the institutional outcome, then explains the complete capability set and supports it with operating metrics and customer evidence.
- Tokeny combines a direct commercial benefit with specific scale proof before asking for a demo.
- Ondo uses a memorable category position and recognizable ecosystem evidence instead of a keyword-heavy hero.
- Fireblocks uses an outcome-led headline and product imagery rather than explaining every capability above the fold.
- Chainalysis states the category it owns, follows with platform value and reinforces authority with customer scale.

FluidRWA should adopt the same discipline without imitating those brands or presenting unverified claims. On-page article headlines are now shorter and more human; the detailed search language remains in the title tag, introduction, comparison table and metadata. Editorial review and sources are presented as a quiet evidence row rather than another promotional card.

The resulting FluidRWA writing model is:

1. State the buyer decision or outcome in one short headline.
2. Answer the decision immediately in plain language.
3. Show methodology, reviewer and sources.
4. Present the comparison evidence.
5. Offer one relevant directory action before secondary conversion paths.

## What not to do next

- Do not publish another large batch of lightly differentiated comparison pages.
- Do not add schema that is not represented in visible page content.
- Do not rewrite pages around malformed Search Console queries until they are confirmed as human demand.
- Do not promise rankings, “verified” capabilities or current pricing unless the evidence is visible and dated.
- Do not evaluate progress from impressions alone; clicks into directories and submitted requirements are the commercial outcomes.

## Seven-day measurement plan

Track these pages as a fixed cohort in Search Console:

- the 11 updated comparison articles
- the five updated commercial directories

Compare:

- CTR by page and device
- non-brand clicks
- queries at positions 1-10 with zero clicks
- article-to-directory clicks
- directory-to-requirement starts
- requirement submissions

Do not call the test after one or two days. Title and snippet changes require recrawling, and Google may rewrite either one. Review directional movement after seven days and make a stronger decision after 14-21 days.

## Next content standard

Every new comparison should include original, visible decision value:

- a one-paragraph answer
- a compact comparison table
- best fit and avoid-if guidance
- a dated methodology
- primary-source links
- at least one limitation per provider
- one relevant directory CTA
- one project-intake CTA

If a proposed article cannot add information beyond existing vendor pages and provider marketing copy, do not publish it.
