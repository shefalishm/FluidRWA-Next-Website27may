# The Companies Behind a Tokenized Fund Are Not All Doing the Same Job

When a tokenized fund is announced, the tokenization platform often gets the headline. But a fund can involve several other organizations, each responsible for a different piece of the operating model.

There may be an asset manager, a fund administrator, a custodian, a transfer agent and a technology provider. Some roles may sit with the same company. Others are delegated. The important point for anyone planning a similar product is to understand what each appointment actually covers.

## Start with the asset and the record

Ask where the fund’s underlying assets are held and who is responsible for them. Then ask where the official ownership record lives. A blockchain record can play different roles depending on the product’s structure and documents; seeing a token in a wallet does not answer every legal and operational question about the fund.

That distinction also affects reconciliation. If a transfer is recorded onchain, how does the official register reflect it? Who resolves a mismatch? Who can correct an error? These are questions for the fund’s legal and operating teams, not decisions that should be inferred from a token standard alone.

## Custody is not a single role

A fund asset custodian and an investor wallet provider may be involved at different points. They should not automatically be treated as the same service. Similarly, an administrator that calculates or records fund information is not necessarily the transfer agent maintaining an investor register.

For procurement, a useful exercise is to create a responsibility table with four columns: the task, the named party, the evidence for that appointment and what the project team still needs to confirm. If no public source names a provider, mark it as unknown rather than assuming there is none.

## Treat announcements as dated evidence

A launch announcement can tell us who was involved at launch. It does not automatically confirm that the arrangement remains unchanged today. Prospectuses, filings and provider pages may offer more context, but they can describe different entities or levels of a fund structure.

That is why FluidRWA’s [research into the firms behind tokenized funds](https://www.fluidrwa.com/network-explorer/research/tokenized-fund-infrastructure-october-2026) identifies the source and scope for each observed role and keeps gaps visible.

## What a project team should ask

Before choosing a platform, ask which responsibilities it owns and which require separate providers. Request a diagram of the investor onboarding process, the share or token record, transfer restrictions, reporting and redemption. Then ask what happens when one of those steps fails.

The answer will look different for each fund. A good operating model makes those differences explicit, so that the technology, legal structure and service providers can work together after launch.
