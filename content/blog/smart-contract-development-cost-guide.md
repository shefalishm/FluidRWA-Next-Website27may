## Why pricing looks opaque

Smart contract pricing looks opaque from the outside, but the logic underneath is straightforward once you know what moves the number.

A simple token contract, a regulated tokenized fund and a DeFi lending protocol should not be priced the same. They do not carry the same complexity, risk or review burden.

The goal is not to find the cheapest quote. It is to understand whether the quote reflects the real work.

## What drives the cost

### Complexity

Complexity is the biggest lever. A standard token following a known pattern is a known quantity. Custom logic, novel economic mechanics, cross-chain behavior or anything that has not been built many times before takes more design, testing and review.

Complexity rises with:

- custom token logic
- permissioned transfers
- investor allowlists
- DeFi mechanics
- staking
- lending
- liquidation
- redemption logic
- upgradeable contracts
- multiple roles
- oracle dependencies
- cross-chain messages
- automated lifecycle events

### Value at risk

Security requirements scale with the value your contract will hold or control.

Higher stakes mean:

- more senior review
- deeper tests
- independent audit
- remediation time
- monitoring
- incident planning
- bug bounty consideration

That is not padding. It is the cost of not becoming a cautionary tale.

### Integrations

A contract that stands alone is cheaper than one that must work with oracles, bridges, custody systems, KYC providers, payment rails or offchain databases.

Every connection adds failure modes. That means more code, more testing and more coordination.

For tokenization projects, integrations can include:

- KYC status
- wallet allowlists
- custody controls
- transfer restrictions
- payments
- distributions
- redemptions
- reporting
- legal document references

### Team seniority

Senior specialists charge more per hour. They often cost less per outcome because they make fewer expensive mistakes.

Smart contract development is a bad place to optimize purely for hourly rate. The real cost of weak work appears in audits, delays, rewrites and security risk.

## The line items people forget

The contract code is often only one part of the bill.

Budget explicitly for:

- specification
- architecture
- contract development
- tests
- internal review
- independent audit
- remediation after audit
- deployment
- documentation
- monitoring
- post-launch support

Quotes that only cover writing code are not cheaper. They are incomplete.

## How to compare quotes fairly

Ask each provider to break the estimate into the same phases:

1. Discovery and specification
2. Architecture
3. Development
4. Testing
5. Audit coordination
6. Remediation
7. Deployment
8. Support

This makes low quotes easier to understand. A provider may be efficient. Or it may have excluded audit, tests, documentation or support.

## Where to spend and where not to

Do not skimp on:

- senior review
- testing
- audit
- access-control design
- admin-key controls
- documentation
- remediation

Do question scope. The cheapest contract is the one you did not need to build.

Good discovery helps remove features that do not earn their complexity. That usually saves more than a small rate discount.

## Budgeting for audit findings

Useful audits find issues. That is the point.

Budget time and money for:

- fixing findings
- retesting fixes
- updating documentation
- redeploying if needed
- communicating unresolved risks

If the launch plan assumes a clean audit and no remediation, the plan is fragile.

## Cost questions to ask every provider

Ask:

- What is included?
- What is excluded?
- Is audit included or separate?
- Is remediation included?
- How many review rounds are included?
- What testing is included?
- What support is included after deployment?
- What triggers a change request?
- Who pays for delays caused by external vendors?
- What happens if the audit requires major changes?

Good providers answer these clearly.
