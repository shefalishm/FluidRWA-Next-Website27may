## The real question is what you need to carry with you

A fund operator can replace a login screen in a week and still spend months disentangling the records behind it. Investor identities live in one system, approved wallets in another, and the administrator keeps a third view of ownership. When somebody asks for a Tokeny alternative, the useful response is not immediately another product name. First establish which records, permissions and operating relationships must survive the change.

This guide is for issuers and platform teams comparing a new software arrangement or planning a controlled transition. The focus is continuity of ownership, investor servicing and administrative authority. It is not a recommendation to move an existing issuance, a judgment about Tokeny's performance or a claim that every alternative supplies the same legal services.

Tokeny's platform documentation describes issuer servicing and investor applications for issuing and managing tokens. That is the incumbent software baseline. A replacement proposal must explain the surrounding operating responsibilities rather than treating a different interface as a complete replacement for the business process.

## Four alternatives, in alphabetical order

| Alternative and primary source | Product role to investigate | Choose to evaluate when | Boundary to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|
| DigiShares | Issuer tokenization software | Your priority is a branded issuer and investor workflow | Which operators and servicing responsibilities remain outside the software |
| InvestaX | Consulting and tokenization-platform engagements | You want to compare a different operating arrangement alongside the technology | Platform services versus access to investment products |
| [Securitize](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2094496/000162828026073937/a424b3-securitizecorpq32026.htm) | Issuance and servicing within a wider service stack | You are reviewing appointed services as well as issuer tooling | Exact entities, mandates and contractual scope |
| Zoniqx | z360 issuer workflows and zConnect distribution | Issuance and distribution need separately defined workstreams | What the proposed engagement actually includes |

Alphabetical order makes the list easier to scan. It is not a performance score. An alternative can be useful without replacing every incumbent function. Equally, a broader service arrangement can create new dependencies that a software-only purchase would not. Require each candidate to identify the purchase being proposed before comparing features or asking for a migration estimate.

## Decide whether this is selection, replacement or extension

A greenfield issuer can choose its initial operating model before investor records exist. An established issuer has a different task: it must preserve records and open obligations while changing part of the stack. A third team may only need an additional distribution interface. These projects can mention the same vendors while requiring very different contracts and acceptance tests.

For a new issuance, concentrate on who creates the authoritative investor record and which events produce ownership. For a replacement, add reconciliation, historical corrections, access removal and successor usability. For an extension, specify which system remains authoritative and prevent the new interface from becoming an accidental second register. The scope document should identify one of these projects explicitly.

Avoid using a software evaluation to make an unstated decision about legal appointments. The administrator, register operator, custodian and distributor may have separate agreements and responsibilities. A proposed technical connection does not amend those appointments. Bring the responsible business owners and advisers into the selection before a product team promises that a replacement will be operationally invisible.

## A ten-criterion evidence scorecard

Use this as a procurement worksheet. The cells describe evidence to request, not capabilities we have established for every vendor. Mark an item accepted only when the accountable team has reviewed a relevant artifact or witnessed the agreed exercise.

| Criterion | Evidence to request | Acceptance exercise |
|---|---|---|
| Register authority | Approved ownership-record hierarchy | Resolve a disagreement between the register and token balance |
| Identity mapping | Stable investor identifiers and history | Associate an existing investor without creating a duplicate |
| Wallet association | Address approval and change rules | Replace a compromised destination under independent approval |
| Token administration | Role inventory and authority boundaries | Revoke an operator without losing required functions |
| Eligibility rules | Offering-specific decisions and versions | Reproduce a past restriction decision from retained evidence |
| Servicing | Distribution and corporate-action records | Rebuild one investor statement from source events |
| Integrations | API versions and event contracts | Recover a missed event without processing it twice |
| Security and recovery | Scope-specific assessments and runbooks | Restore access with unavailable personnel |
| Commercial model | Itemized implementation and ongoing charges | Price normal activity, exceptions and transition overlap |
| Exit | Structured exports and successor rights | Operate a sample close without the incumbent interface |

A scorecard should make disagreement useful. If investor relations accepts a statement format but finance cannot reconcile it, record the two findings separately. Do not average them into a reassuring number. A critical ownership or recovery failure should remain a release condition even when the candidate performs well on less consequential usability preferences.

## What to investigate with each alternative

### DigiShares: define the issuer experience and the operating team

DigiShares presents Launch, Standard and Advanced software tiers. Use that product structure as a starting point for a scoped conversation, not evidence that every operational requirement belongs in every tier.

Ask the candidate to demonstrate the journey from an incomplete application to an approved subscription and a serviced investor. The important handoff is between the person approving an application and the system recording ownership. Require a visible explanation of rejected, withdrawn and corrected cases. A familiar investor portal is helpful, but the business still needs to know which decisions create lasting obligations.

DigiShares is an investigation path when the main purchase is issuer tooling. If the real gap is finding an appointed administrator or a distribution mandate, place those requirements in separate workstreams. Request a clear boundary between implementation assistance and ongoing responsibility. Confirm how the proposed package changes when an existing book, rather than a new offering, is introduced.

### InvestaX: keep platform and investment-product decisions separate

InvestaX presents consulting, a tokenization-platform route and investment-product access. Those are distinct entry points. A team looking to issue its own asset should request an issuance engagement rather than infer its scope from an investment opportunity shown on the same website.

Evaluate the proposed legal entities, operating partners and contractual deliverables together. Ask who accepts the asset, who reviews investors, where records are maintained and which relationships the issuer must arrange independently. Do not assume the answer for one product or jurisdiction carries across to another engagement with the same brand.

This option belongs in the comparison when the team is willing to reconsider the wider arrangement, not only the software interface. That broader review may be appropriate, but it should be intentional. Write down which incumbent appointments would remain, which would change and which require the counterparties' agreement before discussing an implementation date.

### Securitize: compare service ownership, not just software features

The [Securitize filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2094496/000162828026073937/a424b3-securitizecorpq32026.htm) describes issuance and servicing within a wider service structure. It is a useful reference for understanding why a software comparison and a coordinated-service comparison are different purchases. It does not establish approval for the reader's offering.

Ask for an entity-by-entity proposal. A sales conversation should lead to a responsibility map covering the register, investor decisions, servicing and any distribution arrangement. Identify what depends on separate admission, contracts or professional review. Compare that proposal with the fully staffed cost of your software-led arrangement, rather than comparing one software subscription with a broader commercial package.

A coordinated service proposal is worth investigating when the issuer wants to revisit several appointments at once. It is a poor basis for a rushed software migration if the team has not agreed which responsibilities it intends to retain. Use [Tokeny vs Securitize](/blog/tokeny-vs-securitize) for the direct comparison and the [Securitize alternatives guide](/blog/securitize-alternatives-2026) for the service-stack purchasing question.

### Zoniqx: separate issuer operations from distribution

Zoniqx distinguishes z360 issuer workflows from zConnect distribution. Preserve that distinction in the request for proposal. Product names do not make issuance administration and investor acquisition the same responsibility.

Ask which records move between those workstreams, who approves access and how a rejected or changed instruction returns to the authoritative record. A distribution connection should carry a defined mandate and admission process. Do not turn the existence of infrastructure into an assumption about investor demand, secondary-market liquidity or automatic acceptance of a particular asset.

Zoniqx belongs on the shortlist when the issuer wants to evaluate those connected but separate roles. Give each role its own acceptance criteria. A successful issuance demonstration does not prove a distribution arrangement, and a distribution discussion does not demonstrate ownership-record continuity. The same separation helps procurement identify the right commercial owners.

## Build an export inventory before asking about migration

Begin with the information your team needs to operate, not only the files a supplier happens to export. List investor identities, entity relationships, approved wallet associations, ownership events, documents, restrictions, distributions, open cases, administrative roles and historical changes. For each category, record the authoritative owner, available format, identifier and permitted successor use.

Keep identifiers distinct from display names. Two investors can have similar names, and one organization can change its legal name without becoming a new investor. An export that preserves only labels may be readable while losing the relationships needed for servicing. Request stable references and a description of how records relate, including merged or corrected records.

Do not export more personal information than the transition requires. Agree access, retention and transfer arrangements with the responsible privacy and legal teams. Some evidence may need controlled retrieval rather than unrestricted copying. A usable migration plan can identify where an artifact remains and how authorized operators obtain it; it need not distribute every document to every system.

## An illustrative register-reconciliation exercise

Consider a fictional offering with four investors and an approved ownership total of 1,000 units. One investor holds through two approved wallets. A fifth application is incomplete, and its payment is held outside the ownership register. The purpose of the exercise is to distinguish approved ownership, wallet distribution and unresolved money, not to reproduce a particular vendor's implementation.

Export the ownership record and relevant events at an agreed cutoff. Have the successor independently reconstruct the 1,000 units by investor and explain the split-wallet relationship. The incomplete applicant must not appear as an owner simply because a payment reference exists. Ask both teams to identify which records support those conclusions.

Now introduce a delayed event dated before the cutoff but received afterward. The operating team should decide whether it belongs in the frozen migration set or a separately approved adjustment. Preserve that decision and compare the revised totals. A reconciliation process that silently changes the baseline makes later differences much harder to explain.

Finally, produce a statement for one investor from the reconstructed history. Compare holdings, distributions and document references, not only the final token quantity. The exercise passes when the business owners can explain the result and outstanding exceptions. It does not pass merely because an import completes without an error message.

## Token administration requires a separate transition plan

The software application, the ownership record and token-contract authority are related but separate control surfaces. A transition might change an interface while leaving contract administration unchanged, or require a broader approved change. Do not assume that importing investor data also transfers the authority to pause, issue, restrict or recover tokens.

Inventory each administrative function and who may exercise it. Include ordinary roles, emergency roles, integration credentials and any provider dependency. Obtain an explanation of which actions are reversible and which can affect investors immediately. Responsible technical and legal teams should agree the intended control model before anyone schedules a production change.

Use a low-risk rehearsal that demonstrates removal of an unavailable operator and continuity of required functions. Check whether alerts, approval records and downstream systems reflect the change. A new administrator should not automatically inherit unrestricted privileges merely to make migration convenient. The objective is a documented authority transition, not temporary bypasses that become permanent.

## Investor servicing is where portability becomes visible

Investors experience a transition through access, communications and completed requests. They may not care which system maintains the register until a distribution is missing or a wallet change remains unresolved. Plan servicing continuity with the same care as technical import. Identify who answers questions before, during and after the agreed transition window.

Create an investor-facing exception list without exposing unnecessary personal data. Track interrupted applications, pending distributions, undelivered documents, wallet changes and unresolved account access. Assign each case to a named operating role. A vendor transition should not make a case disappear from the dashboard while the underlying obligation remains open.

Test a request that starts before the cutoff and finishes after it. Require a durable business reference connecting both stages. Ask which operator communicates the outcome, where the final evidence is retained and how the predecessor and successor avoid taking conflicting action. This is often more revealing than testing an entirely new investor after migration.

## Integrations need a replay contract

An integration can deliver the same event twice or deliver a delayed event after a newer status. Design the consumer to recognize the business event and its version rather than treat arrival order as truth. Ask candidates to show how identifiers, timestamps, status retrieval and reconciliation support the exact workflow being proposed.

During a rehearsal, suppress one notification and replay another. The receiving system should detect the gap or retrieve authoritative state without creating a duplicate distribution, ownership entry or investor case. Retain the input, processing result and recovery evidence. A successful API response alone does not demonstrate that the downstream business state is correct.

Document who owns the recovery job. A connector maintained by a supplier, an issuer integration team and an administrator's export may each have different support boundaries. State who investigates a disagreement and how the parties exchange enough evidence to resolve it. Avoid a design that requires an investor to diagnose which supplier lost an event.

## Compare costs using the same workload

Build a common activity model before requesting proposals. Include the number of offerings and investor records, historical depth, expected onboarding, servicing events, integrations, administrators, support coverage and exceptions. These are inputs to a quote, not claims that every supplier uses the same billing model. Request the units and exclusions that actually apply.

Separate implementation from the continuing operating cost. Data cleanup, mapping, historical reconciliation and integration testing can require work outside a recurring subscription. A lower application fee may leave more responsibility with the issuer, which can be acceptable if that work is staffed and costed. The comparison should expose that tradeoff rather than hide it.

Add a transition-overlap scenario and an exit scenario. Ask who pays for retained access, later corrections and successor assistance. Avoid unsupported assumptions about how long the overlap will last. Tie it to acceptance milestones and outstanding obligations. A contract should explain what happens when the project needs more review, not rely on an optimistic calendar.

## Security evidence must follow the proposed configuration

Request the scope, period and relevant customer responsibilities for any assurance material. A logo or general security statement does not tell the buyer which application, integration, operating entity or administrative process was assessed. Ask how the proposed deployment and successor access fit within that evidence, including any material differences.

Check ordinary access removal as well as emergency recovery. A departing employee should not retain a privileged API credential or an overlooked route into investor records. Require a complete inventory and a rehearsal of revocation. Recovery should restore authorized operations without giving one person unrestricted authority over the entire transition.

Plan incident communication across the issuer, administrator and suppliers. The runbook should say who may pause activity, which records are preserved and how unresolved investor requests are handled. Reopening a working login is not enough if the restored system cannot explain activity that occurred during the interruption.

## When retaining the current arrangement is a valid result

An alternatives exercise can conclude that the present arrangement should remain while a narrower issue is corrected. For example, a reporting gap might be addressed through an approved export or an integration change rather than a platform replacement. Require a concrete description of the problem, its impact and the proposed remedy before treating migration as the default answer.

Compare the risk of change with the risk of staying. Include outstanding investor requests, the quality of available history and the team's capacity to operate a transition. If the replacement cannot demonstrate a usable register or a safe authority change, a lower quoted subscription does not resolve those concerns. Document the conditions under which the decision would be reconsidered. That produces an accountable procurement result without manufacturing urgency or presenting every incumbent relationship as something that must be replaced.

## Ten questions for a Tokeny alternatives decision

1. Are we selecting new issuer software, replacing a live workflow or changing the wider operating arrangement?
2. Which record is authoritative for ownership, and who is responsible for maintaining it?
3. Can the export preserve stable investor references, wallet associations and correction history?
4. Which open investor requests and financial obligations must remain with the existing operator?
5. What contract-administration authority changes, and who approves those changes?
6. How will the successor reconstruct a servicing event without the incumbent interface?
7. Can our integration recover missed, repeated and out-of-order events safely?
8. What security evidence applies to the proposed access and recovery model?
9. Which implementation, overlap, support and exit charges sit outside recurring software fees?
10. What evidence must be accepted before the old access and credentials can be retired?

Turn the answers into release conditions with an owner and evidence reference. A decision memo should explain why the selected arrangement fits the business and why other routes were rejected. Avoid a conclusion that merely repeats a supplier's feature list. The approval is for a specific operating model, not for a brand in the abstract.
