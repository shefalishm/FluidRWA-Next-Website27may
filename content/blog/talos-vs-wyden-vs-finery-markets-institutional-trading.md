## Vendor profiles

### Talos

Talos presents an institutional platform covering connectivity, smart order routing, execution algorithms, RFQ, portfolio and risk management, treasury, settlement and post-trade analytics. Its breadth makes it relevant to both buy-side and sell-side institutions.

**Good fit:** Institutions seeking an integrated digital-asset trading stack with broad venue connectivity and capital-markets workflow depth.

**Potential limitation:** Buyers should avoid assuming every module or provider connection is included. Confirm instrument, venue, jurisdiction, custody and deployment scope in writing.

### Wyden

Wyden positions its platform as a unified operating layer for banks and brokers. Public materials emphasize liquidity aggregation, best execution, custody and core-banking integration, settlement, reconciliation, accounting and institutional security controls.

**Good fit:** Regulated banks and brokers that need crypto trading to fit existing operational, accounting, risk and outsourcing frameworks.

**Scope to clarify:** Wyden's September 3, 2024 Infinity launch announcement also describes Enterprise for funds and asset managers. Request the current product and deployment, not an assumption that all Wyden implementations require the same banking stack.

### Finery Markets

Finery Markets describes FM Marketplace, FM Liquidity Match and FM White Label for electronic liquidity workflows. Its RFQ page distinguishes firm-book execution from quotes requiring liquidity-provider confirmation. Its current footer describes Finery Markets BVI Limited as a SaaS supplier, with institutional operators responsible for bilateral trading and settlement relationships. Separate product terminology from the identity and duties of the contracted entity.

**Good fit:** OTC desks, brokers and institutions that prioritize ECN market structure, liquidity distribution and branded trading services.

**Potential limitation:** Confirm how bilateral counterparties, credit, pre-funding, settlement, custody and legal venue access work for the buyer's entities.

## Reconcile one order across systems

Before the demo, assign an owner to each record: order intent, fills, settlement instructions and the final custody or ledger entry. Ask each vendor to export those records for a partial fill followed by a cancel. The test is whether your operations team can reconstruct what happened without relying on a vendor dashboard.

If execution and settlement are owned by separate teams, pair the [trading platform directory](/vendors/institutional-digital-asset-trading-platforms) with the [custody comparison](/blog/crypto-custody-providers-comparison-2026). [Share your trading requirements](/submit-requirement?source=trading-article-buyer) if you need help identifying which roles to shortlist.

Take an order from approval through execution, allocation, settlement and the end-of-day record. Add a partial fill and a venue outage. Ask where the official order state lives, which party can amend it and how your operations team resolves a mismatch with the custodian or prime broker. The demo should include the audit trail, not just a trading screen.

## Worked example: Reconcile a partially filled order

Use a fictional order for 10 units. The first venue fills six units and the remaining four are canceled. Ask the provider to export the original instruction, accepted order, fill and cancellation as separate records. Your operations team should be able to start with a client order ID, find the venue IDs and reconcile six units rather than ten. Populate fees using the test venue's records, not the quote shown before execution.

| Boundary | What to show | What counts as a failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order to execution | Client reference, route or counterparty, fills and canceled remainder | A canceled remainder appears as executed exposure |
| Execution to settlement | Which party owes which asset and when, with a settlement reference | A fill is shown as settled when the settlement record is missing |
| Settlement to close | Position and cash records, fee breakdown and exception owner | Finance cannot link a variance to the trade or correct it without overwriting the history |

Use this test to distinguish roles. Ask Talos how a routed trade returns to your own records. Ask Wyden how the institution's downstream systems receive the reports and exceptions. Ask Finery Markets which evidence comes from its trading workflow and which must be supplied by counterparties and settlement providers. The same test can reveal very different integration work; the article does not claim these platforms were tested.

## Institutional trading in 2026: prove the route from order to settled position

A dealer can show a competitive execution price while finance is still unable to explain the final position. That is the difference between a trading demonstration and an operating-model demonstration. Ask each shortlisted provider to connect the order, fills, fees, counterparty obligation, settlement instruction and final balances.

Talos describes trading and related operating products; Wyden describes a trade-lifecycle platform; Finery Markets distinguishes marketplace and trading-software products. Confirm the purchased module and service entity rather than treating each group as one identical execution venue.

| Buyer | Decision to test first | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset manager | Order control and execution across approved counterparties | Decision rules, timestamps, fills and post-trade allocation |
| Bank or broker | Integration with the existing client, treasury and accounting stack | Account mapping, approvals, positions and settlement exceptions |
| OTC desk or liquidity business | Dealer connectivity and the applicable marketplace or software model | Counterparty relationship, quote terms, credit and settlement duties |

These are buyer scenarios, not a performance ranking.

### The difficult-order exercise

Send a fictional order that receives two partial fills, followed by a cancellation request. Then simulate a delayed acknowledgment from one connected service. Ask how the system distinguishes requested cancellation from confirmed cancellation, and how it prevents an uncertain state from creating a duplicate order.

Reconcile the result by quantity, price, fee currency and account. Include the execution reference and settlement reference separately. A position should not become "settled" simply because a trade has filled.

Next disable one venue or counterparty. Check which orders can continue, whether policy changes are recorded and how your team explains the new route. Repeat after removing an operator's access.

### Define the same scope

Compare the same venues, accounts, users, instruments, integration work and service level. Separate software access, connectivity, market data, implementation and support responsibilities. Confirm whether the provider, a connected venue or another counterparty handles the asset and cash obligations.

For jurisdiction, name the buyer entity, instrument and counterparties. A broad list of integrations does not settle your permissions or eliminate counterparty exposure.

Use the [RFQ, order-book and OTC execution guide](/blog/rfq-vs-order-book-vs-otc-crypto-execution) to define the model, then [submit the trading workflow](/submit-requirement?source=blog-shortlist&topic=institutional-trading&article=talos-vs-wyden-vs-finery-markets-institutional-trading) with execution and settlement requirements stated separately.

## Compare the control plane, not just the trading screen

The awkward moment in a trading demo is often after the trade. A portfolio manager sees a fill, operations sees a pending movement and finance sees no settled balance. A useful platform evaluation follows that disagreement until each system agrees on what actually happened.

The following exercises are buyer evaluation methods, not comparative execution results. Request records from the product and deployment being proposed, rather than relying on a group-level feature list.

| Criterion | Ask all three providers | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Order ownership | Where does an instruction become authoritative? | Client and venue identifiers |
| RFQ terms | Is a quote firm and when does it expire? | Quote, expiry and acceptance record |
| Credit | What limits apply before routing? | Limit decision and exposure record |
| Funding | Which assets must be prefunded? | Account and available-balance evidence |
| Partial fills | How are fills and residuals allocated? | Fill-by-fill audit trail |
| Custody | Who controls the settlement assets? | Custody agreement and integration map |
| Settlement | Which event closes the obligation? | Confirmation and reconciliation state |
| Outage recovery | What prevents a duplicate order? | Retry and replay procedure |
| Reporting | Can independent systems reproduce the trade? | Export with fees and timestamps |
| Commercial scope | What is excluded from implementation? | Itemized statement of work |

### Choose by the hardest unresolved dependency

Start with Talos when the investigation centres on a multi-venue execution and trading technology stack. Start with Wyden when bank or broker operations must connect trading, settlement and accounting. Start with Finery Markets when the central problem is electronic liquidity interaction, including RFQ. These are investigation paths, not recommendations to skip legal, risk or operational approval.

If the biggest gap is asset safeguarding, procurement should first resolve the [custody model](/blog/crypto-custody-providers-comparison-2026). If the team cannot distinguish a quote from an executable order, use the [RFQ, order-book and OTC guide](/blog/rfq-vs-order-book-vs-otc-crypto-execution) before commissioning an integration.

### Ten questions for an operational sign-off

1. Who can submit an instruction and change a limit?
2. What distinguishes a rejected order from an uncertain order?
3. Which counterparties are available to our contracting entity?
4. How are quote expiry and price movement recorded?
5. What prevents routing beyond available credit or funds?
6. How are fees assigned across partial fills?
7. Who owns an unsettled obligation overnight?
8. Can operations recover after a missed event?
9. Can an independent reviewer reconcile the final balances?
10. What complete history do we receive when leaving?

## Product scope: what exactly are you buying?

A platform name is not a specification. One proposal may cover a trader's terminal, another a client brokerage ledger, and another a branded environment through which a broker distributes liquidity. All can show an order ticket. The operating responsibilities behind that ticket are different.

Keep the established comparison above as your starting point, then attach a product schedule to the shortlist. Record the contracted entity, module, deployment, interfaces, eligible instruments, approved counterparties and authoritative records. Mark dependencies that require another agreement. This is more useful than awarding a point for each logo on a connectivity page.

### Talos Trading, White Label and RFQ are distinct discussions

Talos Trading describes routing that considers funding and fees, with in-flight and post-trade transaction analysis. Talos White Label separately describes client-facing interfaces, internal transaction records and configurable hedging. Its documentation describes hedging before customer booking under the stated model. Ask how an unsuccessful hedge affects the customer instruction; do not translate a product description into a promise of risk-free execution.

The separate Talos RFQ Platform page is marked coming soon and qualifies availability for some features and clients. That is different from saying Talos has no RFQ functionality: the Trading page also describes multi-dealer RFQ. Request a written product and instrument scope, including current availability and eligibility, rather than combining every page into one feature checklist.

Talos' software disclosure also separates bilateral liquidity-provider arrangements from software access and distinguishes separately contracted regulated affiliates. Ask which entity supplies each service. A group affiliate's permissions do not automatically become part of a software subscription.

### Wyden Infinity is not every Wyden deployment

Wyden's September 2024 Infinity announcement describes bank and broker orchestration, a double-entry record of internal and external activity, and agency/principal operating models. It also identifies Enterprise for funds and asset managers and describes both multi-tenant and dedicated deployment options. Confirm today's proposed configuration; do not rule out Wyden for a fund based only on Infinity's bank-focused positioning.

The useful question is which ledger owns the institution's final record. Ask how omnibus positions reconcile to client subledgers and how a correction reaches core banking, custody and reporting. Require named owners for breaks between systems instead of treating a double-entry ledger as evidence that every external balance already reconciles.

### Finery Markets: identify the operator and settlement chain

FM Liquidity Match documentation describes a master-account/subaccount model: the master settles separately with liquidity providers while clients settle with the master. It also describes bilateral limits and onboarding prerequisites. Confirm that this is the proposed arrangement before assuming the client has a direct relationship with every underlying provider.

For any branded environment, draw the legal and operational chain. Identify the client-facing operator, software supplier, liquidity counterparty and settlement account owner. Finery Markets' software disclaimer should be read alongside product marketing, not ignored because a feature page uses marketplace terminology. This is a contracting question for the parties and their advisers, not a conclusion about anyone's regulatory status.

## RFQ versus firm book: test the commitment point

An attractive quote is not the same record as an accepted trade. Ask when a request becomes binding, what expires, who can reject it and what the client sees while confirmation is pending. Preserve the quote identifier, response, acceptance, provider acknowledgment and final state separately.

Finery Markets' RFQ documentation distinguishes continuous firm-book liquidity from an RFQ path sent to the liquidity provider for final confirmation. Ask for the actual message sequence and rejection states for your setup. Do not assume a firm-book description applies unchanged to every RFQ. Similarly, do not infer settlement finality from a successful execution response.

Use a controlled exercise: request a quote, let it expire, and attempt acceptance. Then accept a fresh quote while delaying the provider response. The client should receive an explicit state, not a misleading completed trade. Ask whether retrying the same client reference can produce another obligation and how operations resolves uncertainty without placing a replacement order blindly.

For streaming liquidity, disconnect the price feed while keeping the order connection alive. Check whether stale quotes are detected, whether new orders are blocked or rerouted under policy, and what operators see. Record timestamp sources and clock synchronization. A visible price is not sufficient evidence that the associated liquidity remains usable.

Test the emergency controls as well. In a controlled environment, trigger an agreed price or size limit, stop an algorithm and request cancellation of outstanding orders. Ask which routes the stop covers, what remains uncertain during a disconnected session, and who can authorize a restart. A stop request is not proof that every external venue has canceled every child order; reconcile acknowledgments before reopening the flow.

## Funding, credit and hedging: follow the exposure

The best displayed price may be on a venue where the institution has no available funds or remaining credit. Define eligible routes using actual permissions and resources. Test a better-priced but unfunded destination against a funded alternative. The expected result should follow your approved policy, with the routing decision retained for review.

Separate three constraints: available balances, bilateral counterparty credit and client trading limits. A client can be within its own limit while the broker has exhausted a provider limit. Model reservations for outstanding orders and unsettled trades, not just completed positions. Demonstrate simultaneous orders competing for the same available resource.

In an agency workflow, identify the client's execution relationship and how allocations are communicated. In a principal workflow, identify when the institution becomes obligated to the client and when its hedge becomes final. If those moments differ, ask who owns the residual exposure and which controls limit it. Product labels alone do not answer that question.

Test the difficult case: the client transaction is acknowledged but its hedge receives only a partial fill. Ask whether remaining exposure is warehoused, retried, rerouted or escalated. Record the policy, decision maker and client-facing state. Do not assume that an automated hedge always eliminates market risk or that using several providers eliminates counterparty concentration.

## Settlement is a separate acceptance gate

Map what is owed after execution, who owes it and which evidence closes that obligation. Distinguish a settlement instruction, a broadcast transaction, a provider acknowledgment and the final record accepted by your reconciliation process. State the conditions your own operations team uses rather than relying on a single green status in a trading interface.

The settlement map should include custody and cash dependencies, cut-off times, asset/network support and handling of incorrect instructions. Ask which movements require independent approval and how changed settlement details are authenticated. A trading integration should not silently grant broad withdrawal permissions merely because an execution connection exists.

For a master-account structure, reconcile client obligations and provider obligations independently. A net position can conceal a failed leg. Demonstrate one client settling while a provider leg remains pending, then the reverse. Operations should be able to identify the outstanding exposure without treating unrelated client assets as an automatic solution.

Include weekends and bank holidays in the operating model. Digital-asset markets can trade while a cash or custody dependency is unavailable. Define escalation, limits, communication and recovery, including who is staffed to approve an exception. Continuous price availability does not prove continuous settlement availability.

## A measurable institutional trading pilot

Freeze a representative test population and define the acceptance evidence before connecting production accounts. Include instruments, order sizes, order types, eligible counterparties, account structures and operating hours. A sandbox pass proves only the behavior tested in that environment; repeat critical integration checks under an agreed controlled launch process.

| Pilot exercise | Evidence to collect | Acceptance question |
|---|---|---|
| Partial fill then cancel | Parent order, child orders, acknowledgments and residual quantity | Can the team distinguish requested from confirmed cancellation? |
| Better price on an unavailable route | Funding/credit snapshot and route decision | Was the route consistent with approved constraints? |
| Quote expires or awaits confirmation | Quote, expiry, acceptance and final response | Is the obligation state explicit at every step? |
| Connection drops during execution | Last checkpoint, replay and duplicate controls | Can recovery establish state without double execution? |
| Settlement leg fails | Instruction, obligation and exception record | Is execution kept distinct from settled balances? |
| Client or operator access changes | Permission change and blocked action | Are permissions enforced across interfaces, not just the screen? |
| Historical correction | Original and amended record linked together | Can finance explain the change without losing history? |
| Vendor exit | Orders, fills, allocations, fees and settlement exports | Can another system reconstruct the complete lifecycle? |

Measure execution and operations separately. For execution, define the benchmark, sampling window, timestamps and treatment of partial fills before comparing outcomes. For operations, count unresolved breaks, manual interventions, recovery time and time to reconcile. Do not present a routing-latency claim as end-to-end order latency or compare unlike workloads.

For algorithmic execution, distinguish a time-based schedule such as TWAP from a volume-based benchmark such as VWAP. Ask how the chosen strategy reacts to missing market data, illiquid intervals and an early stop. For pooled orders, define allocations before execution and test rounding and partial fills across accounts. Neither a benchmark label nor a combined order ticket establishes fair allocations or a satisfactory outcome on its own.

Retain unsuccessful and canceled orders in the assessment. Looking only at filled trades can hide rejects, missed opportunities and unresolved instructions. Record why an order failed and whether the cause was credit, permissions, stale data, venue behavior or software handling. The team needs an explanation it can act on, not just an aggregate success figure.

## API, security and deployment diligence

An interface name such as FIX or REST does not establish message compatibility. Request supported versions, authentication, rate limits, sequence handling, replay behavior and event ordering. Test reconnects with missed messages and duplicate deliveries. Agree how your own client reference survives routing and how external identifiers are returned.

Review permissions for trading, account administration and asset movement separately. Apply least privilege and independent approval to sensitive changes. Ask whether a user removed from the interface can still act through an API credential, and how revocation is recorded. Use test credentials and controlled accounts for demonstrations, not unrestricted production secrets.

For hosted or dedicated deployment, document data location, tenant isolation, backup, recovery targets, maintenance and incident responsibilities. Request relevant assurance evidence with its scope and period; a certification logo does not describe every module or subcontractor. Confirm which controls belong to the provider and which remain with your institution.

Build-versus-buy is not a choice between all custom software and no integration work. Even a ready-made trading layer must connect to your permissions, accounts, limits and records. Identify the smallest working launch scope and the conditions for expanding it. Avoid postponing reconciliation and exit planning until after the first client trade.

## Make the shortlist decision explicit

Retain Talos when multi-venue execution is the central problem, investigate the relevant Wyden product when trading must join the institution's wider operating records, and examine the proposed Finery Markets product when electronic liquidity distribution is central. These are starting points, not a universal winner or evidence of comparative performance.

For the final decision, ask each shortlisted team to demonstrate the same difficult workflow and deliver the same evidence package. List unresolved items, accountable owners and go-live gates. A provider should advance because it fits the institution's actual duties and dependencies, not because its marketing page contains the longest feature list.
