## Compare the cost to operate, not just to issue

Ask each provider to price the same asset and operating scenario. A setup quote can look low because onboarding, custody, administration and transaction costs sit in other contracts. Identify which costs the issuer pays, which are charged to investors and which require internal staff time.

| Cost area | What to request | What can change the bill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design and implementation | Integration hours, configuration scope, data migration and test environment | Legacy systems, multiple jurisdictions or nonstandard rules |
| Investor onboarding | Price basis for identity and business checks, repeat screening and manual reviews | Rejected applications, referrals and document resubmission |
| Custody and cash movement | Minimums, balance charges, network fees and settlement charges | Segregation, currencies, networks and where cash is held |
| Ongoing servicing | Administration, reporting, distributions, transfer reviews and exception handling | Reporting frequency, investor count and complexity of entitlements |
| Switching or closing | Data export, record migration, contract termination and transition support | Export format, minimum terms and required third-party work |

## Model an ordinary month and a bad month

Use your own estimates rather than a generic per-token fee. For example, put 100 investor applications, 20 manual reviews, one distribution and two payment exceptions into a worksheet. Multiply each volume by the relevant quoted charge and add fixed monthly fees and internal effort. Then repeat the model after doubling the manual review volume or adding a new market. Ask the provider to price both scenarios on the same assumptions.

## Read the terms behind the line items

Check how the quote defines an active account, a completed identity check and a billable transaction. Ask whether unsuccessful checks, sandbox activity or network fees are included. For services billed by a different entity, request that entity's written terms instead of relying on one vendor's estimate.

Before signing, request a sample invoice and trace each line back to the event that creates it. That exercise helps operations verify the first bill and gives procurement a common way to compare alternatives. This is a cost model, not a fee benchmark or legal estimate.
