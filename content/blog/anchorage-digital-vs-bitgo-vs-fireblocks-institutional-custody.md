## Short answer

- **Anchorage Digital** is the natural starting point when a US institution wants regulated custody combined with platform APIs, staking, governance and documented account workflows.
- **BitGo** is the natural starting point when qualified custody, cold-storage operations, wallet technology and integrated trading or settlement services need to be compared together.
- **Fireblocks** is the natural starting point when the buyer wants to operate MPC-based wallet infrastructure, policy automation and network connectivity directly or through a custody-service design.

The first decision is whether the buyer needs a regulated custodian, custody technology or both. Comparing features before making that distinction creates false equivalence.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Decision factor | Anchorage Digital | BitGo | Fireblocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary orientation | Regulated institutional custody and digital asset financial platform | Qualified custody, cold storage and integrated institutional services | Direct-custody technology, MPC wallets, policy engine and connectivity |
| Natural buyer | Institution seeking regulated custody with institutional access and governance | Asset manager, exchange, treasury or institution comparing qualified custody | Institution operating high-volume wallets, transfers, treasury or embedded asset workflows |
| Control model question | Which Anchorage entity holds assets and which activities are separately contracted? | Which custody entity, wallet model and service boundary applies by jurisdiction? | Does the buyer use direct custody, a connected regulated custodian or another operating model? |
| Operational emphasis | Institutional governance, custody, staking, trading and settlement access | Cold storage, multi-signature or MPC options, custody and prime services | Policy automation, transaction operations, recovery and network connectivity |
| Evidence to request | Custody agreement, entity map, key governance, segregation, recovery and service evidence | Trust or custody terms, wallet architecture, cold-storage operations, insurance scope and recovery tests | MPC and policy architecture, administrator controls, key recovery, transaction evidence and dependency map |
| Do not assume | A regulated custodian removes every smart-contract, asset or counterparty risk | Insurance covers every loss event or customer error | Direct custody means the buyer has no operational dependence on software, devices or recovery processes |

## Evidence buyers should demand

| Evidence | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Contracting-entity and regulator map | Brand-level claims do not establish which entity serves the mandate |
| Wallet and ledger segregation diagram | Legal language must align with actual books, accounts and address operations |
| Signing and approval architecture | Shows where compromise, collusion or unavailable personnel can stop or redirect assets |
| Production asset and network list | Marketing-level coverage may not apply to a specific entity, token contract or service |
| Statements and machine-readable exports | Custody records must reconcile to internal books and survive provider exit |
| Recovery drill and continuity evidence | A design is not recoverable merely because it uses MPC, multisig or cold storage |
| Insurance schedule and exclusions | Headline limits rarely equal available recovery for a particular incident |

## Vendor profiles

### Anchorage Digital

Anchorage Digital presents institutional digital asset services centered on regulated custody, with related access to staking, trading and settlement capabilities. Its public custody materials emphasize institutional governance and regulated structures in the United States and Singapore.

**Good fit:** Institutions that want to evaluate regulated custody as part of a broader digital asset operating relationship rather than purchasing only wallet software.

**Potential limitation:** Confirm the contracting entity, qualified-custodian status for the intended assets and jurisdiction, legal segregation, supported assets, governance workflow, staking treatment, counterparty exposure, recovery process and service-level commitments.

**What to verify:** Demonstrate account creation, permission assignment, a prohibited withdrawal, an approved withdrawal, statements, API records and recovery. Confirm which Anchorage entity and product supplies each step.

### BitGo

BitGo presents institutional custody through regulated entities, with cold-storage custody wallets and configurable key-management approaches that include multi-signature and MPC. Its broader platform also includes trading, staking, financing and settlement-related services.

**Good fit:** Institutions comparing qualified custody with long-standing cold-storage operations and the option to connect custody to broader institutional activity.

**Potential limitation:** Review which entity holds assets, jurisdictional coverage, wallet design, withdrawal governance, insurance terms, sub-custodian exposure, service boundaries, supported assets, transaction timing and recovery evidence.

**What to verify:** Demonstrate the proposed cold or warm wallet flow, policy approval, asset movement, trading or settlement boundary, reconciliation and recovery. Obtain the current insurance schedule rather than relying on a headline figure.

### Fireblocks

Fireblocks presents a direct-custody platform in which customers remain owners and controllers of assets while using MPC-based technology, policy controls, transaction operations and network connectivity. It also connects customers to regulated custodians and other counterparties where a different custody model is required.

**Good fit:** Institutions that want to control wallets and transaction policy while operating transfers, treasury, payments or embedded digital asset products at scale.

**Potential limitation:** Separate software and direct-custody responsibilities from any regulated custody relationship. Test policy administration, device compromise, quorum changes, break-glass recovery, privileged access, network dependencies and the export or migration path.

**What to verify:** Demonstrate vault and user administration, policy changes, MPC recovery, blocked and approved transactions, webhook failure, audit export and migration. State separately which regulated custodian, if any, holds assets.

## What buyers should understand before shortlisting

### Custodian versus custody technology

A regulated custodian legally holds assets under a custody agreement. Custody technology can help a customer create, protect and operate its own wallets. Some platforms support both models through different entities or partners. The RFP must state which party holds title, controls keys, approves transfers and bears responsibility in each workflow.

### The withdrawal-governance test

Demonstrate a high-value withdrawal from request through approval, screening, signing, broadcast, confirmation and reconciliation. Then test a prohibited address, unavailable approver, compromised administrator and vendor outage. Evidence should show who could change the rule and whether the buyer can independently detect the change.

### Recovery is a governance ceremony

Recovery should cover technology failure and organizational failure. Ask what happens when devices are lost, approvers leave, a region fails, the vendor becomes unavailable or a regulated entity enters distress. Run the ceremony with named roles and retain evidence rather than accepting a diagram.

## Best fit by buyer scenario

| Buyer scenario | Likely starting point | Reason to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Fund requiring a regulated institutional custodian | Anchorage Digital or BitGo after entity and asset validation | The legal agreement, asset segregation, jurisdiction and operational withdrawal process should drive the shortlist. |
| Corporate treasury managing active transfers | Fireblocks after policy and recovery testing | Direct wallet operations may fit when the company has the staff and governance to own key and transaction processes. |
| Institution combining custody and trading access | Compare Anchorage Digital and BitGo service boundaries | The buyer should identify when assets leave custody, which counterparties are involved and how settlement is evidenced. |
| Embedded wallet or payment product | Fireblocks plus a separately selected regulated custody model where needed | Technology, legal custody and customer-asset safeguarding can sit in different layers and must be mapped explicitly. |
| Tokenized asset issuer | Custody design for issuer, treasury and investor roles | Token issuance does not make one custody model suitable for every participant or lifecycle event. |

## Test the approval path

Give each provider the same withdrawal scenario: a new destination address, a large transfer and one unavailable approver. Ask who can add an address, who can approve the payment, how the policy change is logged and how the institution regains access after a key or device is lost. Request the actual evidence your operations team would receive, not just a demonstration of the signing screen.

## Conclusion

Anchorage Digital is a natural starting point for institutions seeking regulated custody within a broader institutional digital asset platform; BitGo for buyers comparing regulated qualified custody, cold-storage operations and integrated trading or settlement services; and Fireblocks for institutions prioritizing direct-custody technology, MPC-based wallet operations, policy automation and network connectivity. Buyers must first separate regulated custody, custody technology, wallet operations and counterparty services because the legal and operational responsibilities differ.
