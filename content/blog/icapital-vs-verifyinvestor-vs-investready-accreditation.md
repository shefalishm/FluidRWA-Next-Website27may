## Which accreditation workflow fits a tokenized securities offering?

Start with the offering's exemption and investor population, not a provider logo. The [SEC's Rule 506(c) guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/assessing-accredited-investors-under-regulation-d) says issuers must take reasonable steps to verify accredited-investor status. The rule is a US securities-law context; this article is a workflow comparison, not a determination that a specific token or offering qualifies.

The cohort spans two accreditation specialists and one broader identity/onboarding service. It is **not** a like-for-like ranking or an exhaustive market list. iCapital's current public page documents identity and AML/KYC services; its current 506(c) accreditation scope was **not verified** in this review. Official product pages establish what each company says it offers; they do not independently validate a customer's legal compliance or conversion rate.

## iCapital Identity Solutions: test reuse without losing context

[iCapital announced its acquisition of Parallel Markets](https://icapital.com/newsroom/press-releases/icapital-to-acquire-parallel-markets-to-streamline-digital-identity-investment-onboarding-and-compliance-verification/) in January 2025. Its [current Identity Solutions page](https://icapital.com/identity-solutions/) describes reusable investor identity records and AML/KYC review. The older Parallel Markets accreditation URL now redirects to that broader page, so this review does **not** treat the historic accreditation description as proof of a currently contracted 506(c) service. Ask iCapital directly for the legal entity, verification method and deliverable offered today.

For a platform offering more than one product, the practical question is whether a prior verification can be used in the new offering, with what date and evidence, and who approves an exception. Reuse can reduce duplicated collection, but it cannot substitute for a current, offering-specific compliance judgment. Request the record format and test an investor whose status changes after the original verification.

## VerifyInvestor: test the verification-to-issuer hand-off

[VerifyInvestor](https://www.verifyinvestor.com/) describes accredited-investor verification and other investor-status services; its [API materials](https://www.verifyinvestor.com/api/) describe an integration path. Pilot a real review sequence with synthetic data: invite, document collection, result, issuer notification, exception and renewal. Check whether the proposed contract includes exactly the evidence and qualified-purchaser or identity checks your workflow needs. Their coexistence on a marketing page is not proof that every check is bundled.

## InvestReady: test completion and evidence retrieval

[InvestReady](https://investready.com/) describes an investor accreditation flow. Its [consulting materials](https://consulting.investready.com/) also discuss tokenization-related requirements. The buyer should test the applicant's path on mobile, resubmission after an incomplete file and how staff retrieve a result at subscription time. Do not infer that a verification provider is your transfer agent, broker-dealer or securities counsel.

## Run the same pilot for all three

Use a synthetic accredited individual and an entity with layered beneficial ownership. Record what the investor sees, who requests documents, how a reviewer rejects or requests more, and exactly what artifact reaches the issuer. Then simulate a material change before a second offering and ask whether the platform can identify stale evidence.

| Decision | Evidence to request |
|---|---|
| Offering scope | Written description of supported investor types and verification method |
| Data handling | Retention, access, deletion and cross-border processing terms |
| Operational exceptions | Manual review, failed checks, appeals and support ownership |
| Result use | Date, validity conditions, downloadable evidence and audit trail |
| Integration | API/webhook behavior, retries and reconciliation identifiers |

This is narrower than our [general KYC and AML provider comparison](/blog/sumsub-vs-persona-vs-chainalysis-kyc-aml-providers): accreditation asks whether a person or entity meets an offering's investor-status conditions, while identity and financial-crime controls answer other questions. Last checked **October 15, 2026**. Legal interpretation and provider suitability require direct review of the offering and contract.
