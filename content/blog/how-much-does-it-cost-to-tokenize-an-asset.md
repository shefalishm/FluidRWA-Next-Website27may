## Start by checking what a quote includes

Two proposals can have the same headline price and cover completely different work. A software quote may get you to a working token. Launching an investment product may also require legal documents, investor eligibility checks, custody arrangements, money movement and people who will service the asset after launch.

| Budget line | Ask who is paying and for what |
|---|---|
| Legal and product setup | Structuring, documents, jurisdictions and updates after launch |
| Platform and implementation | Configuration, smart contracts, integrations, testing and security review |
| Investor onboarding | Identity and eligibility checks, exceptions and support |
| Custody and operations | Keys or assets held, approvals, recovery and statements |
| Payments and servicing | Subscriptions, income, redemption, reconciliation and reporting |
| Ongoing change | New investors, new markets, product upgrades, audits and exit |

Ask each provider to price the same asset, expected investor count, transaction volume and launch geography. Separate one-time fees from annual and per-transaction charges. Show the costs of a small launch and a larger operating year. A cheap minting quote is not a complete project budget.

## Work out whether the project is worth it

The [BIS summary of tokenization](https://www.bis.org/publications/fsi-summary-financial-stability-implications-tokenisation-executive-summary) discusses possible efficiency and cost benefits, but they depend on implementation. Write down the manual work your team hopes to remove and the new work it will take on. For example, a shared ownership record may simplify some reconciliation while adding wallet support, key controls and exception handling.

Compare the proposed costs with your current way of issuing and servicing the asset. If the new structure improves access, speed or recordkeeping, explain how that benefit will be measured. If the business case depends on secondary-market liquidity, ask who will provide it and under what rules. No vendor can responsibly give a universal price or savings percentage without your project scope.
