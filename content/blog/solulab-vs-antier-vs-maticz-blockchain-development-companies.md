## The short answer

SoluLab, Antier and Maticz all market blockchain development services, but the buyer is purchasing a delivery team, not a list of website capabilities. SoluLab combines blockchain with broader software and AI engineering. Antier presents an extensive blockchain-focused service portfolio. Maticz positions itself as a specialist Web3 development company across exchanges, tokens, DeFi and related products.

The decisive questions are who will actually work on the project, what comparable system they have operated in production, how security is evidenced and whether the buyer can maintain or replace the system after launch.

## SoluLab

SoluLab publicly positions itself across blockchain development, AI and custom software engineering. That breadth can help where smart contracts are one component of a larger product containing APIs, data, mobile or web interfaces and enterprise integrations.

**Good fit:** Organizations seeking one engineering partner across blockchain and conventional application layers.

**Verify:** Named architects and developers, direct experience with the selected chain and token standard, security process, subcontractors, product management, quality assurance, DevOps, documentation and post-launch support.

## Antier

Antier publishes a broad portfolio of blockchain development services, including tokenization and Web3 product categories. It may suit buyers that want a blockchain-focused firm able to cover several technical workstreams.

**Good fit:** Programmes where blockchain architecture and product delivery are central and the buyer wants a specialist services organization.

**Verify:** References for the precise use case, assigned team, reusable components, license terms, key and admin ownership, security testing, change control, deployment access, support and exit.

## Maticz

Maticz presents development services across exchanges, tokens, DeFi, wallets, NFT and other Web3 applications. It can be relevant for teams seeking a Web3-native delivery shop.

**Good fit:** Startup and growth teams building a defined onchain product and prepared to retain strong buyer-side product and security ownership.

**Verify:** Production evidence beyond demos, code-review standards, testing, independent audit readiness, backend and infrastructure capability, incident support, IP ownership, documentation and maintainability.

## A procurement process that reveals delivery risk

Send the same concise requirements pack to every firm. Include one critical workflow, users, assets, jurisdictions, integrations, expected volume, security constraints and operating responsibilities. Ask each bidder to state assumptions and exclusions instead of filling gaps silently.

Require the proposed team by role and seniority. Interview the people who will make architecture and security decisions. A polished sales team and a long portfolio do not show who will write, review and operate the production code.

Use paid discovery when uncertainty is material. The output should include architecture, threat model, responsibility matrix, delivery plan, acceptance tests, cost range and key risks. It should remain usable if the buyer does not proceed with that firm.

## Security and handover requirements

The development company should provide tests, code review, dependency controls, secure deployment and remediation. Material smart contracts should still receive independent security review. Production keys, domains, repositories, cloud accounts and package ownership should be under buyer-controlled accounts with least-privilege vendor access.

Define source-code ownership, third-party licenses, documentation, deployment scripts, monitoring, runbooks and knowledge transfer in the contract. Tie final acceptance to a usable handover, not only feature completion.

## Avoiding cannibalisation

This comparison adds a new vendor trio to the high-intent blockchain development cluster. It complements the existing LeewayHertz vs PixelPlex vs Unicsoft guide rather than repeating those companies or the general how-to-choose article.

## Buyer checklist

1. Compare named people, not agency headcount.
2. Ask for directly relevant production references.
3. Define acceptance and security evidence before build.
4. Keep production accounts and credentials buyer-controlled.
5. Use independent review for material smart contracts.
6. Price support, remediation and change requests.
7. Contract for documentation and an exit-ready handover.
