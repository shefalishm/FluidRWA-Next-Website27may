## Find the record that survives the platform

A vendor can stop operating while a contract remains accessible. That does not tell you who can administer the asset, who retains investor records or how transfers are approved. Start with the instrument and service agreements to identify which record determines ownership and which entity can amend it. Work with counsel and the administrator before attempting to replace the platform.

| Dependency | What to retain before an outage | What to rehearse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership and investor records | Current holdings, dated history, investor identifiers and applicable access controls | Reconcile export totals to the authoritative record |
| Contract control | Deployed addresses, roles, transaction authority and emergency procedures | Confirm the authorized parties can act without the vendor interface |
| Payments and servicing | Distribution, settlement and reconciliation records | Match an unresolved payment to the correct investor during cutover |
| Compliance and permissions | Eligibility decisions, restrictions, access logs and retention duties | Apply the same rules on a replacement platform |
| Vendor agreements | Export rights, transition support, dependencies and termination provisions | Establish who can retrieve data if the service is unavailable |

## Test a migration without moving customer funds

Use a synthetic register and a recent representative history. Have an administrator reconstruct balances and identify a restricted transfer without calling the old vendor API. Compare holdings, pending instructions and corrections at a documented cutover point. If the replacement cannot match the list, stop and investigate rather than accepting plausible-looking totals.

For a real incident, restrict changes where authorized, preserve records, communicate with the relevant parties and bring in counsel and service owners. Do not assume the right remedy is to redeploy a contract or to ask investors to send tokens to a new address. A platform outage, a custody failure and a formal insolvency may require different responses.

## Ask for exit evidence before purchase

Request a sample export and document what the vendor cannot export. Ask who holds the credentials, which other services the workflow depends on and whether access to critical operations continues while a migration is under way. Put named owners and a test date in the runbook. A documented drill is stronger evidence than a general portability promise.
