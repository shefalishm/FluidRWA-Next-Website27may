A provider's website says it is regulated. Your proposal arrives under a different company name. Is that a routine group arrangement, or are you about to buy a service from an entity that cannot perform the work you need?

The answer starts with the name on the contract, not the logo on the homepage. This guide shows how to connect that entity to its actual activity and the relevant regulator, so your next conversation is about the right business rather than a reassuring badge.

## Start with the contract, not the brand

Record the legal name and identifier of the entity that will serve you. Ask which activities it performs directly and which are performed by affiliates or third parties. Match that answer to the official record, including restrictions, status and any relevant warnings. Do not infer a subsidiary's permission from a parent's licence.

This guide explains a research process, not whether a particular offering is lawful. Have qualified counsel assess your asset, investor eligibility and cross-border service model.

## Choose the register for the activity

| Region | Official starting point | Scope to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | [FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/) | Broker-dealer and broker records; not every type of financial permission |
| United States | [SEC Investment Adviser Public Disclosure](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/) | Investment adviser records, not a substitute for broker-dealer registration |
| United Kingdom | [FCA Financial Services Register](https://register.fca.org.uk/s/) | Named entity, status, permissions and restrictions |
| Singapore | [MAS Financial Institutions Directory](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/fid) | The institution and the specific licence or regulated activity |
| Switzerland | [FINMA authorised institutions](https://www.finma.ch/en/finma-public/authorised-institutions-individuals-and-products/) | Relevant institution or product category and its status |
| Spain | [CNMV company search](https://www.cnmv.es/portal/consultas/busquedaporentidad?lang=en) | Investment-service entity records and relevant register categories |

Search using the legal name on your proposed contract. If a register requires JavaScript or will not load, use the regulator's alternative search or contact route.

## Keep a scoped record of what you found

Retain the register URL, legal name, identifier, activity, restrictions, effective or reporting date, access date and reviewer. Compare the contract's website and contact details with the regulator's record. If the names differ, request an explanation and supporting documentation.

The [FINMA page](https://www.finma.ch/en/finma-public/authorised-institutions-individuals-and-products/) cautions that list updates can lag changes in status. Use the regulator's contact route where a material discrepancy cannot be resolved.

## Avoid three common conclusions

Registration is not an endorsement of investment quality. A securities registration or filing is not blanket approval of the issuer's technology. A permission in one jurisdiction does not establish the right to serve every investor in another.

Likewise, MiCA crypto-asset permissions should not be treated as interchangeable with securities market-infrastructure permissions. Review the [EU DLT Pilot Regime guide](/blog/eu-dlt-pilot-regime-tokenized-securities-guide) and the relevant legal classification with counsel.

## Put the result into procurement

Link each service to its responsible entity and supporting record. Mark what is confirmed, what is outside scope and what remains unknown. Recheck before contracting and when the provider changes its entity, product or service location.
