## The Short Answer

RWA.xyz, Artemis and Allium sit at different layers of the digital-asset data stack.

**RWA.xyz** is the strongest fit for tokenized real-world asset market intelligence: asset-level classification, issuers, protocols, networks, market metrics and RWA-specific dashboards and APIs.

**Artemis** is the strongest fit for institutional analysis of crypto, stablecoins and fintech fundamentals through standardized metrics, a terminal, spreadsheet integrations, research, REST API and Snowflake delivery.

**Allium** is the strongest fit when a company needs the underlying multi-chain data infrastructure: raw and enriched datasets, SQL analysis, data shares, production REST endpoints and real-time streams.

These products are not direct substitutes. RWA.xyz answers **what exists in tokenized assets**. Artemis answers **how digital-asset networks and businesses are performing**. Allium helps teams **build their own analysis or product on standardized blockchain data**.

## Why “Blockchain Data” Is Too Broad a Category

A strategy team researching tokenized Treasuries does not need the same product as an engineer calculating wallet balances in real time. A compliance team reconciling stablecoin flows does not need the same interface as an analyst building a comparable-company model.

Separate requirements into four layers:

1. **Market intelligence**: assets, issuers, products, categories and market size
2. **Standardized metrics**: activity, revenue, fees, stablecoin use and fundamentals
3. **Analytical data**: queryable decoded transactions and historical tables
4. **Production data**: low-latency APIs and streams embedded in customer products

RWA.xyz, Artemis and Allium occupy different combinations of these layers. Selecting one by brand recognition alone creates expensive gaps later.

## Provider Profiles

### RWA.xyz

RWA.xyz is purpose-built for tokenized real-world assets. Its documentation describes a data model connecting assets, tokens, protocols, networks and platforms, supported by classification frameworks and methodology. Its API exposes asset records, filtering, aggregation and time-series metrics.

That specialization is valuable because RWA data is difficult to infer from contract events alone. A token address does not reliably explain the legal asset, issuer, asset class, jurisdiction, represented value, maturity or tokenization structure. RWA.xyz adds a curated semantic layer.

RWA.xyz is a strong fit when:

- investors need a map of tokenized asset markets
- issuers benchmark products and networks
- analysts compare asset classes and protocols
- a media, research or policy team needs a recognized RWA dataset
- an application needs asset-level RWA metadata through an API
- the business tracks tokenized Treasuries, credit, commodities, real estate or stablecoins

Its main limitation is scope by design. It is not intended to replace a general multi-chain warehouse for arbitrary wallet, transaction or protocol analysis. Buyers should also understand the methodology for classifications and how issuer-supplied data is validated.

### Artemis

Artemis standardizes financial and operating metrics across crypto, stablecoins, fintech and selected equities. Its product set includes a terminal, stablecoin dashboard, Sheets integration, research, REST API and Snowflake data share.

Artemis is strongest when analysts want comparable metrics without first building blockchain pipelines and metric definitions. Examples include daily active addresses, transactions, fees, revenue, TVL, developer activity, stablecoin transfer volumes, supply and user behavior.

Artemis is a strong fit when:

- an investment team needs standardized chain and protocol fundamentals
- stablecoin adoption and payment activity are strategic questions
- analysts work in Excel, Google Sheets or a terminal
- management needs repeatable comparable metrics
- a quant or research team needs API or Snowflake access
- the business wants curated research alongside data

Its main limitation is flexibility compared with a raw data platform. A standardized metric is useful only if its definition fits the question. Teams requiring custom entity labels, transaction-level logic or a production application may need a deeper data layer.

### Allium

Allium provides raw and enriched multi-chain data through databases, data shares, APIs, SQL tooling and real-time streams. Its documentation distinguishes realtime REST APIs, Explorer APIs for saved analytical queries and streaming products that can route filtered blockchain events.

This makes Allium an infrastructure platform rather than merely a research dashboard. A customer can retrieve balances, holdings, transactions, token data and prices, build custom endpoints from SQL, receive data in a warehouse or stream events into an application.

Allium is a strong fit when:

- the product needs low-latency blockchain data
- data engineers need normalized schemas across chains
- finance or operations needs reconciliation-grade datasets
- analysts require custom SQL and decoded data
- developers need wallet, token, price or DeFi position APIs
- enterprise teams want data shares or event streams

Its main limitation is that raw flexibility creates ownership. The customer must define business metrics, RWA classifications and decision logic. Allium can supply the data layer, but it does not automatically provide the curated tokenized-asset taxonomy of RWA.xyz or every ready-made metric from Artemis.

## Fit by Business Question

### “How large is the tokenized Treasury market, and who issues the assets?”

Start with RWA.xyz. Its asset model and RWA taxonomy are designed for this question. Validate important totals against issuer disclosures and contract data before publishing or making investment decisions.

### “Which networks and applications are gaining economically meaningful activity?”

Start with Artemis. Its standardized chain, protocol and financial metrics reduce the work required to compare activity, fees, revenue and developer behavior.

### “We need wallet balances and stablecoin transfers inside our product in near real time”

Start with Allium. This is a production data requirement, not only an analytics requirement. Test endpoint coverage, freshness, latency, reorg handling, quotas and failure behavior.

### “We need a weekly stablecoin market report”

Compare Artemis and RWA.xyz for ready-made metrics and market framing. Add Allium when the report needs bespoke cohorts, entity labels, wallet flows or custom transaction definitions.

### “We need to reconcile issued token supply with operational records”

Allium is the closest infrastructure fit for custom onchain reconciliation, but the application must define the authoritative contract set, mint/burn logic, bridge treatment, decimals and offchain source of truth. RWA.xyz can provide useful reference metadata; it should not replace the issuer’s books.

## Data Methodology Questions

Every data buyer should ask:

1. What is the primary source for each field?
2. Which values are calculated, curated or issuer-submitted?
3. How are contracts mapped to protocols and assets?
4. How are bridged assets and wrappers treated?
5. How are blockchain reorganizations handled?
6. When are historical values restated?
7. Which timestamps and time zones define daily metrics?
8. Are transfers filtered for self-sends, bots or contracts?
9. How are entities and wallets labeled?
10. Can a user reproduce the metric from documented inputs?

The most dangerous data error is not a missing row. It is a number that looks precise but uses a definition the decision-maker does not understand.

## RWA-Specific Data Challenges

### Legal asset vs token

One legal instrument can have several token representations, and one token can sit inside wrappers or pools. Market totals must avoid double counting.

### Outstanding value vs trading volume

The onchain token balance is not always the economic value outstanding. Rebasing, price changes, accrued income, redemptions and offchain settlement can alter interpretation.

### Public chain vs private ledger

Institutional products may use permissioned systems or maintain important state offchain. Public blockchain data alone can understate activity.

### Classification

Tokenized credit, funds, Treasuries and stablecoins can overlap. The taxonomy should be explicit enough that a reader understands what is included.

### Issuer verification

Issuer-provided data adds context but requires validation and timestamps. The platform should distinguish claimed information from independently derived onchain metrics.

## Analytics Data Is Not Automatically Oracle Data

These platforms may inform research, dashboards and products. That does not mean their data should directly control smart contracts.

An oracle design must define:

- signed data provenance
- update cadence and freshness
- manipulation resistance
- onchain verification
- fail-safe behavior
- dispute and correction process
- economic security and availability

A REST API used by an analyst can tolerate a retry. A NAV or collateral value used to mint assets requires a much stricter delivery and failure model.

## Proof-of-Concept Design

Choose one question that all three can inform, such as monthly stablecoin supply and transfer activity across five chains.

For each platform:

- record the exact metric definition
- retrieve the same time range
- compare chain and asset coverage
- inspect missing and revised values
- trace a sample to underlying contracts
- measure API or export usability
- document time spent cleaning and reconciling
- test how quickly a known recent event appears

Then add one provider-specific task:

- RWA.xyz: map five tokenized assets to issuer and asset-class metadata
- Artemis: build a comparable stablecoin activity table in Sheets or API
- Allium: create a custom transaction-level query or production endpoint

This avoids scoring each provider against a job it was not built to perform.

## Procurement and Operating Checklist

- Coverage of required chains, assets and protocols
- Data latency and service levels
- API, export, Sheets and warehouse delivery
- Historical depth and backfill policy
- Revisions and versioning
- Entity labels and custom taxonomies
- Usage, redistribution and derived-data rights
- Rate limits and expected total cost
- Security and access controls
- Support for methodology questions
- Exit plan and exportability

Data licensing deserves special attention. The right to view a dashboard does not necessarily include the right to redistribute data in a commercial product or public report.

## Final Verdict

- Choose **RWA.xyz** for curated tokenized-asset market intelligence and an RWA-specific data model.
- Choose **Artemis** for standardized institutional metrics, stablecoin analytics and analyst-friendly workflows.
- Choose **Allium** for custom, raw, enriched and real-time blockchain data used in analytics and production products.

Many serious teams will use two layers: RWA.xyz or Artemis for fast market understanding, and Allium or another data platform for custom verification and production. The correct stack separates convenient metrics from decision-grade evidence.
