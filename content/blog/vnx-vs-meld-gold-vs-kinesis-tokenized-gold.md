Tokenized gold is often discussed as one category, but a treasury team, bullion dealer and issuer may be buying three different things. One may need technology to create a branded token against metal it controls. Another may want to accept an existing gold-backed token. Another may need a distribution and redemption network. This article compares **operating models**, not expected returns or token prices.

## Short Answer

Consider VNX for a B2B white-label tokenization project that needs token generation and lifecycle tooling around allocated metal. Consider Meld Gold when integration with a gold-and-silver token ecosystem and bullion partners is the main requirement. Consider Kinesis when the use case concerns existing KAU/KAG currencies, business acceptance or physical redemption under its rules. These are not substitutes for one another in every procurement.

## Comparison at a Glance

| Decision area | VNX | Meld Gold | Kinesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Published model | B2B white-label tokenization plus own metals case study | Metal tokens and bullion-partner ecosystem | Existing gold and silver currencies plus business integrations |

## The Three Models

[VNX](https://vnx.li/) says its tokenization framework can support a partner's own asset, brand and commercial setup, with precious metals as its established case. Its [tokenization description](https://vnx.li/tokenization) covers minting and burning against asset data, programmable controls and reporting. An issuer should ask precisely which functions VNX provides, who holds legal title to metal and who acts as custodian.

[Meld Gold](https://www.meld.gold/) describes gold and silver tokens designed for investors and businesses, as well as an integrated bullion supply chain. Its [conversion page](https://www.meld.gold/convert) identifies participating bullion partners. A buyer should establish whether it is integrating existing tokens, using a bullion partner or seeking custom issuance; those are different contracts.

[Kinesis](https://kinesis.money/business/) markets business acceptance and integration for its gold and silver currencies, KAU and KAG. Its [redemption guidance](https://support.kinesis.money/hc/en-gb/articles/12355371107613-Can-I-redeem-my-gold-or-silver-from-Kinesis) states that bullion redemption is subject to minimum withdrawal requirements and describes periodic independent audits. The relevant procurement question is whether the existing currency and its terms fit the business, not whether Kinesis will build a new branded gold token.

## Five Tests That Matter More Than Token Branding

1. **Legal claim:** Does the holder own metal, a contractual claim against an issuer, or another right? Obtain the operative terms.
2. **Inventory:** Can token supply be reconciled to specific bars, vault records and any outstanding redemption instructions?
3. **Redemption:** What amount, country, fee, notice and identity checks apply to physical delivery or cash settlement?
4. **Control:** Who can mint, burn, pause, freeze or upgrade the token and under what governance process?
5. **Distribution:** Where can the token be held or traded, and what happens when an integration or market-maker is unavailable?

For an institutional buyer, add an adverse scenario: a vault discrepancy, a delayed audit, a redemption queue and a chain outage on the same day. Ask which party owes the holder performance under each event.
