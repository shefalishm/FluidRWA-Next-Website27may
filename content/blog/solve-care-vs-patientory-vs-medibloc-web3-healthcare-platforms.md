## Side-by-side comparison

| Decision factor | Solve.Care | Patientory | MediBloc |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary orientation | Healthcare network coordination, benefits, transactions and payments | Patient-facing health wallet, records, coaching and consented data marketplace | Patient-centered health-data ecosystem and credential-oriented interoperability |
| Publicly described model | Care platform with wallet, protocol, data vault and network components | Encrypted records, health coaching, consumer services and health-data sharing model | Panacea blockchain with DID, credentials and health-data services that keep sensitive records off-chain |
| Likely user | Payers, administrators, providers, employers and care networks | Patients and consumer health-programme participants | Patients, providers, institutions, research and health-data ecosystem participants |
| Strongest starting point | Coordination of network services and administrative workflows | Consumer health engagement and portable patient experience | Health-data provenance, credentialing and patient data-control architecture |
| Data principle to verify | Which data is stored, where, and which party can access it | Security, consent, marketplace use and data-sharing terms | Public statement that sensitive health data is not stored directly on-chain |

## Vendor profiles

### Solve.Care

Solve.Care describes a healthcare platform designed for networks that coordinate care, benefits, transactions and payments. Its public materials describe Care.Wallet, Care.Protocol, data and consent components, and a model that can be used by insurers, administrators, government agencies, clinical organisations and employers.

**Good fit:** Organisations exploring a coordinated healthcare-network model rather than a standalone consumer record application.

**Potential limitation:** Healthcare workflow, privacy and payment obligations are specific to the programme and jurisdiction. Verify the operating entity, data-processing roles, EHR integration, consent mechanics and clinical responsibility before deployment.

### Patientory

Patientory positions its product as a patient-facing digital health wallet with records, an AI health coach, health services and a consented data marketplace. Its website states that the product is secured by its PTOYMatrix blockchain and presents a consumer engagement and rewards model.

**Good fit:** Consumer health programmes where the participant experience, data access and engagement model are central to the product.

**Potential limitation:** Data sharing and token incentives require heightened scrutiny in healthcare. Confirm how consent is obtained and revoked, how information is secured, what clinical claims are made and whether incentives create inappropriate pressure or disclosure risk.

### MediBloc

MediBloc describes a patient-centered health-data ecosystem based on its Panacea blockchain. Its materials emphasize DID and credential verification, while explicitly stating that sensitive health data is not recorded directly on the public blockchain and is instead stored and managed at the user's endpoint with a separate encrypted channel for sharing.

**Good fit:** Health-data initiatives focused on patient agency, data provenance and verifiable sharing architecture.

**Potential limitation:** A sound high-level data architecture still requires secure endpoint design, key recovery, EHR interoperability, provider adoption, data-quality governance and jurisdiction-specific privacy analysis.

## Best fit by buyer scenario

| Buyer scenario | Likely starting point | Reason to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Payer or employer care-coordination network | Solve.Care | It is publicly positioned around care, benefit and payment coordination across network participants. |
| Consumer health-engagement application | Patientory | Its product centres on a patient wallet, health services and engagement experience. |
| Patient-controlled data and credential project | MediBloc | Its public architecture focuses on data sovereignty, DID and off-chain handling of sensitive records. |
| Hospital sharing actual clinical records | Standards-first architecture | Evaluate FHIR integration, record stewardship, consent, audit, breach response and clinical governance before selecting a Web3 layer. |
| Clinical trial recruitment and data sharing | Any only after ethics and privacy review | Participant consent, IRB/ethics controls, dataset governance and de-identification must lead the design. |

## Test consent and correction with a real workflow

Map one patient's record from creation to access by a clinician and later correction. Ask what the patient can see, which organization remains responsible for the record and how access is revoked. Use synthetic data in any demonstration. A claim that data is patient-controlled needs to be tested against the provider's actual permissions and the healthcare rules that apply to your service.

## Conclusion

Choose Solve.Care when the core use case is healthcare-network coordination across benefits, care and payments; Patientory when a patient-facing health wallet, data marketplace and consumer engagement are central; and MediBloc when the programme is focused on a patient-controlled health-data ecosystem, verifiable credentials and integration with its Panacea-based services.
