## Vendor profiles

### DLx Law

DLx Law describes a boutique practice at the intersection of digital assets, payments, financial markets and related infrastructure. Its published scope includes token regulation, tokenized real-world assets, custody, stablecoins, AML and commercial-law architecture.

**Good fit:** US-focused projects requiring close partner access, crypto-native issue spotting and legal design that follows the technical product.

**Potential limitation:** A specialist boutique may need local or subject-matter co-counsel for a large multijurisdictional transaction. Confirm staffing, admissions, conflicts and coordination before appointment.

### DLA Piper

DLA Piper publishes global blockchain and digital-asset capabilities and a tokenization offering called Scintilla. Its wider platform can connect regulatory work with technology, intellectual property, commercial, tax and transactional advice.

**Good fit:** Companies needing one coordinating firm across multiple workstreams, offices and legal disciplines.

**Potential limitation:** Global brand coverage is not the same as identical partner depth in every jurisdiction. Identify named practitioners, deliverables and local-law assumptions in the engagement scope.

### Clifford Chance

Clifford Chance has a substantial institutional finance and capital-markets platform and publishes frequently on digital assets, tokenization and financial regulation. That orientation can be useful where tokenization is part of a regulated transaction rather than a standalone technology launch.

**Good fit:** Banks, funds, issuers and market-infrastructure projects involving complex financing, securities, funds or cross-border regulatory questions.

**Potential limitation:** Large-firm economics and conflicts can be material. Confirm that the proposed team includes both transaction specialists and lawyers who understand the relevant ledger, custody and smart-contract design.

## Best fit by buyer scenario

| Buyer scenario | Likely starting point | Reason to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| US protocol or stablecoin product | DLx Law | Specialist US digital-asset, payments and product-regulatory work may be the clearest starting point. |
| Global enterprise tokenization programme | DLA Piper | A broad office and practice network may simplify multidisciplinary coordination. |
| Tokenized fund or capital-markets transaction | Clifford Chance | Institutional finance, funds and capital-markets depth is likely to carry more weight. |
| Multi-country retail product | DLA Piper or coordinated local counsel | Consumer, payments, privacy, licensing and marketing rules must be mapped country by country. |
| Novel onchain ownership structure | Specialist plus transaction counsel | Combining crypto-native architecture with asset, insolvency and enforcement expertise may be more defensible than relying on one label. |

## Ask for a scoped legal work plan

Give each firm the same asset, jurisdictions, investor types and proposed token rights. Ask which legal questions they will answer, which local counsel or specialists they would involve, what documents they expect to deliver and which assumptions your team must resolve first. Compare scope and responsibility, not only a partner biography. The legal work should map to the product you can actually launch.

## Conclusion

Choose DLx Law when a partner-led, crypto-native US practice and detailed product or regulatory architecture are central; DLA Piper when the mandate needs a broad global platform, technology transactions and tokenization delivery capabilities; and Clifford Chance when complex cross-border finance, capital markets and institutional tokenization are the dominant requirements.
