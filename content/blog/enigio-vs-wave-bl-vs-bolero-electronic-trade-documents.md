## Vendor profiles

### Enigio

Enigio's trace:original product is designed around digital originals that preserve the key functions of paper documents while supporting a human-readable representation and structured data. Enigio states that its technology is relevant to trade finance and transport, and that only the issuer needs to be a customer for a recipient to receive, hold or forward a document.

**Good fit:** Trade programmes digitising several document types or requiring portability beyond a closed participant network.

**Potential limitation:** Legal recognition depends on the document, governing law and each relevant jurisdiction. Require transaction counsel to test possession, control, endorsement, presentation and enforceability for the live trade route.

### Wave BL

Wave BL presents a platform focused on electronic bills of lading for shipping workflows, including master and house bills. Its public materials describe an approach that allows eBL actions through secure email connection and supports on-behalf workflows for shippers or consignees.

**Good fit:** Carriers, forwarders and logistics participants for whom eBL handling is the immediate operational bottleneck.

**Potential limitation:** An eBL is only valuable when each party in the shipment and finance chain can process it. Confirm carrier service, ports, banks, insurers, counterparties, document type and release process on the exact route.

### Bolero

Bolero's Galileo platform brings electronic bills of lading together with digital trade-finance document workflows. It describes connectivity for banks, carriers, forwarders, beneficial cargo owners and corporate systems, with a title registry used in its eBL model.

**Good fit:** Organizations needing document lifecycle management to sit alongside multi-bank and trade-finance operations.

**Potential limitation:** The buyer must understand the contractual rulebook, title and possession mechanics, integration responsibility and availability of the relevant bank, carrier and corridor before relying on a platform for production transactions.

## Best fit by buyer scenario

| Buyer scenario | Likely starting point | Reason to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Exporter digitising a full trade-document set | Enigio | Its product is positioned around digital originals across trade and transport documents. |
| Carrier rolling out master and house eBLs | Wave BL | Its public positioning is focused on eBL workflow operations for shipping. |
| Corporate working with several banks and trade partners | Bolero | The Galileo offering is designed around connected trade-finance and document workflows. |
| New MLETR-enabled trade corridor | All three after legal validation | Choose based on document type, counterparties, standards, governing law and operational integration. |
| Tokenizing trade receivables | Do not start with the token | First establish the enforceable document, title, assignment, financing, disputes and servicing workflows. |

## Test the full document hand-off

Use one shipment and name every party that must receive, hold, endorse or surrender the bill of lading. Ask each provider to show what happens when a counterparty is outside its usual network, a document is corrected or a bank rejects a presentation. Have transaction counsel check whether the proposed electronic record will work under the governing law and contract used on that route.

## Conclusion

Choose Enigio when the priority is creating transferable digital originals that can work across document types and existing processes; Wave BL when carrier and forwarder electronic-bill-of-lading workflows, including master and house bills, are central; and Bolero when eBL lifecycle management must connect closely with broader bank, corporate and trade-finance workflows.
