## What makes these firms different?

### LeewayHertz: enterprise application scope

LeewayHertz's published [case study](https://www.leewayhertz.com/wp-content/case-study/TrustExplorer-CaseStudy.pdf) identifies a San Francisco headquarters and describes blockchain-platform development. That makes it a candidate when a buyer needs an architecture discussion spanning on-chain logic and enterprise systems. Ask for a current, relevant reference and identify which components the proposed team will build versus integrate. The case study alone does not establish suitability for a regulated securities workflow.

### PixelPlex: Web3 product and tokenization scope

PixelPlex describes [New York delivery work](https://pixelplex.io/services/web-development-new-york/) involving smart contracts, Web3 applications and tokenization. A buyer should distinguish a demonstration or proof of concept from a production product with monitoring, account recovery and upgrade controls. Request named technical leads, code samples where disclosure permits, and a production-support plan.

### Intellectsoft: financial-software integration scope

Intellectsoft states a [New York headquarters](https://www.intellectsoft.net/services/nearshore-software-development) and describes [smart contracts and blockchain ledgers](https://www.intellectsoft.net/finance) within a broader financial-software practice. This may suit a project where the ledger is one component of a larger application. The buyer should ask which engineers write the contract code, where that work is delivered, and how security review is separated from implementation.

## What should a US buyer put in the RFP?

1. **Define the system of record.** Specify which rights and balances exist on-chain and which stay in internal databases or custodial systems.
2. **Map regulated steps.** For tokenization, show who owns investor eligibility, identity checks, transfer restrictions and redemptions. Engineering claims are not legal opinions.
3. **Demand a security boundary diagram.** Identify signing keys, admin roles, upgrade authority, incident response and independent review.
4. **Specify proof, not adjectives.** Ask for a relevant production reference, architecture excerpt, test strategy and post-launch service levels.
5. **Clarify the US relationship.** Confirm contracting entity, engineering locations, subcontractors, data access and time-zone coverage.
6. **Protect the handoff.** Set repository ownership, documentation, deployment credentials, acceptance tests and exit assistance in the contract.
