## Vendor profiles

### Immutable

Immutable describes a gaming stack that combines its chain infrastructure with Passport identity and wallets, asset contracts, marketplace and commerce primitives, indexing, and game-growth tooling. Its public material highlights Unity and Unreal SDKs and a model intended to make blockchain interactions less visible to players.

**Good fit:** Game teams that want an integrated infrastructure, asset and player-onboarding stack rather than assembling each component separately.

**Potential limitation:** Validate the exact chain environment, product maturity, pricing, wallet and account controls, content-platform policy considerations, and which services are mandatory versus optional for the planned game.

### Ronin

Ronin's documentation describes a gaming-oriented developer environment with contract deployment, a Developer Console, wallet and market tools, a Ronin Store, game-development resources and a builder reward programme. It is closely associated with the Sky Mavis and Axie ecosystem.

**Good fit:** Studios that value a gaming-native chain and ecosystem distribution, particularly where the market, store and community fit the planned title.

**Potential limitation:** The team should assess chain security, bridge exposure, key and wallet recovery, marketplace terms, game economy design and whether the ecosystem's audience matches the genre and geographic strategy.

### Oasys

Oasys describes a gaming-oriented public blockchain protocol that uses a Hub Layer and Verse Layer architecture. Its public developer materials emphasize EVM compatibility, high-throughput gaming use cases and gas-free player interactions.

**Good fit:** Studios seeking EVM compatibility together with a gaming-first chain architecture that separates broader network and game-layer considerations.

**Potential limitation:** Understand the operator, governance, validator, deployment, data-availability and exit model for the selected Verse. A game-specific chain configuration is an operational responsibility, not merely a deployment choice.

## Best fit by buyer scenario

| Buyer scenario | Likely starting point | Reason to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Studio wanting an integrated wallet, assets and growth stack | Immutable | Its published products extend from chain and assets to onboarding and growth. |
| Title designed for the Ronin commerce and player ecosystem | Ronin | Its tools include developer, wallet, market and store paths within one gaming ecosystem. |
| EVM game requiring a dedicated gaming layer | Oasys | Its Verse architecture is designed around gaming-specific scale and gas UX. |
| Traditional studio adding collectible cosmetics | Start with a narrow proof of value | Test whether ownership adds player value before committing to a full token economy or marketplace. |
| Competitive multiplayer economy | Any after security and game-economy review | Asset custody, anti-cheat, sanctions, age gating, fraud, recovery and live-ops policies will matter as much as chain throughput. |

## Put a player through the whole experience

Test sign-up, wallet creation, an in-game purchase, a failed transaction and support recovery with the same game build. Record which parts of the experience depend on the network, the wallet, the marketplace or your own team. Ask how assets and player records can be accessed if you later change distribution or infrastructure partners.

## Conclusion

Choose Immutable when a studio wants a broad gaming stack that combines chain infrastructure with player onboarding, assets, commerce and growth tooling; Ronin when access to a gaming-native chain and Sky Mavis ecosystem distribution is central; and Oasys when a studio wants an EVM-compatible, multi-layer gaming chain architecture designed around gas-free player interactions.
