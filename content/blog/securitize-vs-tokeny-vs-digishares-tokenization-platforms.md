## Side-by-side comparison

| Decision factor | Securitize | Tokeny | DigiShares |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary orientation | Regulated, integrated tokenized-securities and fund infrastructure | Permissioned-token and onchain-finance platform built around ERC-3643 | Configurable issuer, investor-management and tokenization platform |
| Best starting point | Institutional programme needing to evaluate a regulated operating stack | Institution or platform operator prioritising compliant EVM token controls and orchestration | Issuer or marketplace seeking configurable tokenization and investor workflows |
| Lifecycle lens | Issuance, trading, servicing and regulated operating capabilities | Issue, manage and distribute permissioned tokenized securities | Onboarding, token issuance, cap table, distributions and related workflow configuration |
| Architecture question | Which regulated entity performs each role and in which jurisdiction? | How do ERC-3643 controls, identity and external integrations map to the product? | How do configurable workflows map to the issuer's legal, investor and servicing model? |
| Main diligence point | Licences, entity scope, custody, transfer-agent, broker-dealer and jurisdiction availability | Standard implementation, identity and compliance integration, distribution and chain design | Issuer responsibility, integrations, launch model, trading representation and legal workflow |
| Do not assume | A regulated affiliate can provide every regulated service in every country | A token standard alone resolves securities, distribution or investor-protection obligations | A platform configuration gives an asset liquidity, legal enforceability or a secondary market by itself |

## Vendor profiles

### Securitize

Securitize publicly presents a platform for tokenized real-world assets and describes regulated infrastructure for issuance, trading and servicing through its operating affiliates. It may be relevant where the buyer's first question is how technology maps to a regulated operating model.

**Good fit:** Asset managers and institutions evaluating a tokenized-securities programme that requires careful consideration of regulated roles, investor onboarding, lifecycle administration and distribution.

**Potential limitation:** Scope varies by entity, product and jurisdiction. The buyer must obtain current legal and commercial confirmation of which service, licence, investor type, geography and asset workflow is actually available.

### Tokeny

Tokeny publicly presents an onchain-finance platform centred on ERC-3643, with white-label and API-oriented products designed to issue, manage and distribute permissioned tokenized securities.

**Good fit:** Financial institutions and platform operators focused on embedded transfer controls, onchain identity and an EVM-oriented tokenization architecture.

**Potential limitation:** ERC-3643 and identity controls are infrastructure components, not legal conclusions. Validate the issuer, agent, investor, custody, payment, distribution and recordkeeping arrangements in the target market.

### DigiShares

DigiShares publicly presents configurable tokenization products for issuers, including investor onboarding, token and cap-table management, distributions and related marketplace or white-label workflows.

**Good fit:** Asset owners, funds and platform operators that need to evaluate a configurable issuer-facing system and wish to separate product configuration from bespoke development where possible.

**Potential limitation:** The platform does not become the issuer, regulated intermediary or legal adviser. Define asset rights, offering terms, KYC/AML responsibility, payment flows, custody, tax, servicing and secondary-transfer reality before launch.

## Best fit by buyer scenario

| Buyer scenario | Likely starting point | Reason to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional tokenized-fund programme | Securitize after jurisdiction and regulated-role mapping | The buyer should first establish the issuer, investor, transfer, custody and servicing model. |
| Permissioned-token infrastructure for a financial institution | Tokeny after architecture and identity design | The focus is on how compliance controls, token standards and connected systems enforce the chosen workflow. |
| Real-estate or private-market issuer portal | DigiShares after legal and servicing design | A configurable issuer platform can be useful once the actual asset, investor and distribution model is clear. |
| Tokenized public-equity initiative | Specialist securities counsel and transfer-agent design first | Public-company ownership, shareholder records, settlement and market infrastructure require a specific regulated model. |
| Secondary-market promise | Validate market access independently | Issuance technology does not guarantee counterparties, permissions, liquidity, market-maker support or an executable transfer path. |

## Demonstrate one investor's full lifecycle

Ask each platform to show an eligible investor subscribing, receiving the asset, changing wallet, collecting income and redeeming or transferring under your proposed restrictions. Request the ownership record at each step and identify any transfer agent, custodian or broker that sits outside the platform. This makes the implementation boundaries visible before you choose a vendor.

## Conclusion

Choose Securitize when a programme needs to investigate a regulated, vertically integrated tokenized-securities operating model; Tokeny when permissioned-token standards, embedded compliance and an EVM-oriented white-label or API approach are central; and DigiShares when an issuer needs to evaluate a configurable platform for tokenization, investor management and related marketplace workflows. The first decision, however, is not the software vendor: it is the asset, legal structure, jurisdictions, regulated roles and servicing model that the platform must support.
