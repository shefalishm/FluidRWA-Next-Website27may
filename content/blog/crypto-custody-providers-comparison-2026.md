## Short Answer

Crypto custody is not one category. It includes qualified custodians, MPC wallet platforms, institutional trading custody, cold storage, embedded wallets, DeFi access controls and tokenized asset custody workflows.

For institutions, common providers to compare include Anchorage Digital, Coinbase Prime, BitGo, Gemini Custody, Fireblocks, Copper, Taurus, Fordefi and Utila. The right choice depends on whether the buyer needs regulated custody, wallet operations, DeFi access, settlement infrastructure or tokenized asset support.

## Custody Models Buyers Should Understand

### Qualified Custody

Qualified custody is relevant when client assets must be held with a regulated custodian. This matters for asset managers, funds, broker-dealers, advisers and institutional investors.

Best for: regulated institutions, fund custody, asset managers, tokenized securities and high-governance workflows.

Watch-out: Qualified custody can be less flexible for fast-moving product or DeFi workflows.

### MPC Wallet Infrastructure

Multi-party computation custody splits signing responsibility across multiple parties or systems. MPC vendors often provide policy engines, approvals, whitelists, transaction simulation, role controls and API workflows.

Best for: treasury operations, exchanges, fintechs, tokenization platforms, stablecoin businesses and DeFi operations.

Watch-out: MPC is technology infrastructure, not always a legal custody solution.

### Cold Storage

Cold storage keeps signing material offline or in highly restricted environments.

Best for: long-term asset holding and lower-frequency institutional storage.

Watch-out: It can slow down operations, settlement and active treasury management.

### Embedded Wallets

Embedded wallets help applications create user-friendly wallet experiences without forcing users to manage seed phrases.

Best for: consumer apps, fintech onboarding, marketplaces and tokenization products with non-crypto-native users.

Watch-out: Embedded wallet design must be reviewed carefully for recovery, control and compliance.

## How RWA Teams Should Evaluate Custody

Tokenized asset projects should ask:

- Who legally controls the asset?
- Who controls investor wallets?
- Who controls issuer treasury wallets?
- Can assets be frozen, redeemed or transferred under policy?
- Does the custodian support the target token standard?
- Are reporting and audit records exportable?
- What happens if an investor loses access?
- How are admin keys and upgrade permissions handled?

Custody decisions affect investor trust, regulatory posture and operational risk. They should be made before launch, not after tokens are already issued.

## Questions To Ask About Tokenized Assets

Tokenized assets create custody questions that standard crypto custody checklists miss:

- Can the provider custody restricted or permissioned tokens?
- Can it support transfer restrictions and allowlisted wallets?
- Can it hold both reserve assets and issued tokens?
- Can it support redemptions, subscriptions and distributions?
- Does it integrate with transfer agents or tokenization platforms?
- Can it generate reports suitable for issuers, auditors and institutional investors?
- Can it support multiple investor wallet models?

If the answer is unclear, buyers should run a custody workflow workshop before signing.

## Common Buyer Mistakes

### Confusing Wallet Infrastructure With Legal Custody

MPC wallet software may provide strong transaction controls, but it does not automatically mean the provider is legally acting as custodian. Buyers should confirm the legal role.

### Choosing Only For Asset Support

Supporting a token is not enough. The provider also needs reporting, transaction controls, user permissions, recovery process and policy enforcement.

### Ignoring Operational Latency

Cold storage can be secure but slower. Hot wallets can be fast but higher-risk. MPC can balance speed and control, but setup quality matters.

### Not Checking Exit Paths

Ask how assets, wallet policies, records and reporting data can be migrated if the provider no longer fits.

## Digital asset custody and settlement: choose three responsibilities, not one brand

An institution can have secure signing technology and still be unclear about who owes it the assets. It can have a custody agreement and still depend on an exchange for execution or a bank for the cash leg. The first procurement task is to separate these responsibilities before comparing logos.

Draw three boxes: legal safekeeping, transaction control and settlement. A single group may supply more than one box, but the entities, agreements, eligibility and failure procedures may differ. Buying a wallet platform is not automatically appointing a custodian, and appointing a custodian does not automatically complete your settlement design.

### Put settlement through a failure drill

Consider a fictional fund buying a tokenized asset. Its custodian approves the movement, the asset transaction is broadcast and the cash payment is delayed. Which part of the trade is complete? Who may unwind it? Which party carries the exposure while operations investigate?

Ask every candidate to show the same sequence:

1. Approve the counterparty and beneficiary details.
2. Reserve or identify the assets and cash required.
3. Authorize the transaction under the correct policy.
4. Track asset and cash instructions as separate states.
5. Establish the evidence used to mark settlement final.
6. Reconcile both legs to the trade and custody records.
7. Escalate an incomplete leg without blindly resubmitting it.

Do not label two unrelated transfers as delivery-versus-payment merely because they happen close together. Ask the provider to explain the exact mechanism, legal effect and failure boundary. This guide proposes the exercise; it does not certify a provider's settlement design.

### A custody pricing comparison needs an inventory of charges

Request a written quote for your legal entity, asset list, balances, activity and support expectations. Separate onboarding, minimum commitments, asset-based safekeeping charges, withdrawals, network fees, API/platform access, settlement services and any staking or trading relationship.

Include the cost of exceptions: new asset onboarding, urgent withdrawals, beneficiary review, historical statements and contract termination. Confirm whether a public price applies to a wallet product, personal account or institutional custody service before putting it into your procurement model.

The useful unit is the total cost of the operating arrangement under the same workload assumptions, not an isolated basis-point number copied from another customer segment.

### Reporting, insurance and recovery need different evidence

For reporting, ask for a sample inventory that connects balances, movements, fees and the authoritative account or wallet identifiers. For insurance, request the relevant coverage, insured party, limits, exclusions and claim conditions. For recovery, request an exercise describing who can restore service and which dependencies must remain available.

None substitutes for the others. A statement does not establish insurance coverage. Insurance does not establish the legal treatment of client assets. A recovery tool does not prove that your organization has retained the necessary material or trained approvers.

For tokenized funds, also map the fund administrator, transfer agent and register. The token balance may be only one record in the operating model. ## An institutional custody decision starts with one withdrawal

Imagine the finance team has authorized a payment, one approver is unavailable and the destination has just changed. A custody proposal becomes meaningful when it explains who may act, what evidence is required and how the instruction reaches a reconciled final state. A list of chains or signing features cannot answer those questions alone.

Current service references include [Anchorage Digital](https://www.anchorage.com/), [BitGo](https://www.bitgo.com/), [Copper](https://copper.co/en-us), [Fidelity Digital Assets](https://www.fidelitydigitalassets.com/), [Gemini custody](https://www.gemini.com/institutions/custody) and [Fireblocks Trust Company](https://www.fireblocks.com/trustco). Use these to establish product scope, then request the agreements and security evidence for your actual entity.

| Criterion | Evidence to request | Test or decision |
|---|---|---|
| Contracting entity | Service agreement and applicable permissions | Confirm which entity accepts the customer |
| Asset ownership | Segregation and recordkeeping terms | Match account ownership to statements |
| Signing authority | Initiator, approver and recovery roles | Attempt a prohibited instruction |
| Destination control | Address approval and change process | Independently validate a changed destination |
| Recovery | Credential and key recovery procedure | Remove an unavailable operator safely |
| Asset support | Exact network, token contract and activity | Check a deposit and withdrawal on the intended network |
| Settlement | Counterparty, timing and release conditions | Handle an obligation that does not complete |
| Reporting | Statements, fees and transaction identifiers | Reconcile to the finance ledger |
| Insurance | Policy scope, exclusions and claims handling | Document uncovered scenarios |
| Exit | Export, transfer and account-closure support | Complete a limited migration rehearsal |

### Avoid solving a service problem with the wrong product

An institution seeking safekeeping under a custody agreement should not select wallet software solely because its signing controls look sophisticated. Conversely, a team that needs application-level transaction governance should not assume a custody account replaces every software integration.

Fireblocks' separate Trust Company page is an important reason to name the product, not just the brand. Gemini's current custody page describes MPC; do not carry forward an assumption that its architecture is defined only by historical cold-storage descriptions. Neither observation establishes how a proposed agreement meets your institution's legal obligations.

### Ten questions for the risk committee

1. Which entity will hold or control the assets?
2. What happens to ownership records during insolvency or an operational incident?
3. Who can initiate, approve and recover a withdrawal?
4. Can one administrator change policy and then approve their own transaction?
5. Which assets and activities are excluded from the service?
6. How do staking or tokenized-asset restrictions affect exit?
7. What evidence establishes settlement rather than only broadcast?
8. Which insurance exclusions matter to our use case?
9. How are balances reconciled independently?
10. How do we migrate if access or service is interrupted?
