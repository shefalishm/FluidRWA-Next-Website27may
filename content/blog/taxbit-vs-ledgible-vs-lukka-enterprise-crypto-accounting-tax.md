## Start with the report you cannot produce today

A finance team closing its books has a different problem from an exchange preparing customer tax forms. Both need clean transaction data, but they are not buying the same finished output.

Write down the missing deliverable before requesting demos: a reconciled ledger, a journal export, a valuation input or a filing workflow. Ask each provider to show the route from your records to that output.

## TaxBit: separate the accounting package from the tax package

TaxBit's Accounting Suite help page describes transaction standardization, lot-level calculations and journal mappings to ERP systems. Its current reporting platform separately describes form generation and filing after customer approval. Do not assume the quote for one includes the other, or that the older Accounting Suite documentation establishes today's commercial availability for your entity.

The [cost-basis product](https://www.taxbit.com/platform/cost-basis) describes acquisition lots, account-level methods and flags for incomplete history. Ask the demo team to trace a disposal back to its acquisition and fee records. Your adviser should approve the applicable method; a list of software methods does not establish which one you may use.

For [information reporting](https://www.taxbit.com/platform/reporting), name the required forms, jurisdiction, recipient delivery, approval and correction steps. Confirm who submits each output and who owns rejected filings. No accuracy or audit-outcome guarantee follows from these product descriptions.

Pricing, contracted coverage and implementation effort were not established in this review.

## Ledgible: put reconciliation at the centre of the demo

Ledgible's accounting product describes a digital-asset subledger, gain/loss reporting and OFX exports for ledger import. Its [accounting help guide](https://support.ledgible.io/hc/en-us/articles/360059213851-Ledgible-Accounting-Overview) also describes ledger connections and mappings; confirm the current connector and sync direction rather than treating a file export as a live integration.

Its separate [Tax Information Reporting solution](https://ledgible.io/tir/) describes aggregation, taxpayer mapping and form generation, including connection to existing reporting systems. That differs from a firm's own accounting close and from the [professional tax product](https://ledgible.io/professional/). Ask where cost-basis calculation ends and form preparation, filing, delivery and corrections begin.

The [reconciliation guide](https://support.ledgible.io/hc/en-us/articles/25651516537357-Using-the-Reconciliation-Tab) compares calculated and source balances by asset. Bring a history with a known difference and ask the team to explain it. Check whether corrections remain visible to the reviewer. This is a proposed buyer test, not a result FluidRWA obtained.

Confirm the exact source and history supported, what happens when credentials expire and what export your accountant receives. A named integration does not establish completeness for every transaction type.

## Lukka: decide whether you need a broader data foundation

Lukka's enterprise materials describe data management alongside tax and business reporting. Its [reporting page](https://lukka.tech/tax-and-business-reporting/) separately lists financial statements, tax forms, NAV reporting and cash/crypto reconciliation services. Confirm which are software outputs and which require a service engagement.

For example, a fund administrator may need consistent inputs for accounting, valuation review and reporting rather than a standalone tax output. This is a buyer scenario, not a claim that Lukka has implemented your particular workflow.

Ask which fields arrive from each source, how they are normalized, how changes are recorded and how outputs reach your systems. Verify which valuation data and licences are included; public descriptions do not settle commercial entitlements.

## Cost basis and reporting need different evidence

A complete balance is not proof that acquisition history is complete. Include a transfer with missing basis, a fee paid in another asset and a later correction in the trial. Ask the provider to retain the source identifier, acquisition lot, selected method, valuation timestamp and reviewer decision behind the result.

For regulatory reporting, add a sample reportable account and a rejected or corrected output. Have your advisers define the expected form, jurisdiction and treatment. Ask who handles onboarding documentation, taxpayer matching, approval, filing acknowledgements and recipient delivery. Do not assume every listed regime is included or that software determines your legal obligations.

## A close-period trial that produces a useful answer

Give each provider the same permissioned sample and expected outputs. Have your finance and tax advisers define the expected treatment.

| Test record | What the reviewer should be able to explain |
| --- | --- |
| Transfer between company wallets | How both sides are matched under the agreed accounting policy |
| Network fee paid in another token | Where the asset movement and fee appear |
| Duplicate import | How duplication is detected and resolved |
| Missing transaction | How the balance difference becomes visible |
| Correction after close | What changes, who approves it and whether the original output remains traceable |
| Ledger export | Whether another reviewer can reproduce the output from retained records |

Software output alone does not establish the right accounting policy or legal reporting obligation.

## What to include in the proposal request

- Entities, sources, assets and historical periods in scope.
- Finished outputs and formats your team needs.
- Responsibility for unresolved records and manual adjustments.
- Implementation, support and charges for extra history or sources.
- Permissions, retention and usable exports if you leave.

Keep the final choice tied to the trial results. No enterprise price comparison, performance ranking or certification assessment was verified for this article.

## Which cost-basis software fits your buyer type in 2026?

Imagine that your closing balance matches the custodian, but the gain on one disposal looks wrong. The missing fact may be a purchase made before a wallet migration. A faster calculation engine will not recreate that evidence. The useful purchase is a system that shows where the history ends, preserves the correction and carries the reviewed result into the next report.

That is why cost-basis automation should be evaluated by buyer and output, rather than by the largest advertised integration count.

| Buyer | Starting shortlist or product category | Deliverable to request | Commercial comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise finance team | Ledgible accounting, TaxBit's applicable accounting/basis package, Lukka enterprise data | Reconciled source records, reviewed lots and a journal-ready export | Separate implementation, monthly usage, historical backfill and ledger connections |
| Exchange or broker | TaxBit cost basis/reporting, Ledgible tax information reporting; consider GainsKeeper for Digital Assets as an adjacent option | Account-level lots, reportability inputs, approved forms and corrections | Quote the entire reporting workflow, not only the calculation API |
| Tax practice | Ledgible professional tax or a tax-practice workflow compatible with client records | Per-client evidence, review access and accountant-ready output | Confirm who pays per client and which transaction tiers apply |
| Individual investor | A personal tax product such as CoinTracker rather than an enterprise subledger | Personal acquisition history and the required local tax reports | Check tax year, transaction cap and whether downloads are included |

The first three providers remain the editorial comparison on this URL. The additional products above illustrate adjacent buying routes, not results from a head-to-head product test.

[Ledgible's cost-basis page](https://ledgible.io/cost-basis/) describes source-connected calculation for on-chain and off-chain activity. Its [pricing page](https://ledgible.io/pricing/) distinguishes professional tax pricing from custom enterprise accounting pricing; the latter varies with users, wallets, exchange connections, volume and ledger integrations. Do not apply a professional-tax starting price to an enterprise implementation.

[TaxBit's cost-basis product](https://www.taxbit.com/platform/cost-basis) describes lot tracking across wallets, exchanges and custodians. [Lukka's enterprise data management](https://lukka.tech/enterprise-data-management/) addresses the data foundation behind downstream outputs. These are different starting points: compare the exact data and reporting package in the proposal.

[GainsKeeper for Digital Assets](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/gainskeeper/gainskeeper-for-digital-assets) is aimed at digital-asset reporting workflows, including tax lots and Form 1099-DA. It must not be confused with GainsKeeper for Individual Investors: the [official discontinuation notice](https://www.gkis.net/closing.html) says that individual product's final supported tax year was 2024 and expressly separates it from other Wolters Kluwer products.

[CoinTracker's pricing page](https://www.cointracker.com/pricing) offers personal tax plans differentiated by transaction allowance and services. Its free portfolio tier does not include downloadable tax forms. A portfolio tracker is therefore not a free substitute for every reporting output.

### A reproducible cost-basis acceptance test

Use a fictional, permissioned test dataset before exposing customer records. For an illustrative arithmetic check, suppose a company acquires two units for a total of $200, transfers both to another controlled wallet, and later sells one for $150. Ignore fees for this simplified calculation. If the approved policy carries the original $100 unit basis through that internal transfer, the gain on the sale is $50, not $150. This is a software test assumption, not a determination of tax treatment.

Now remove the acquisition record and run the same import. A useful exception workflow shows the missing evidence rather than presenting a confident zero-basis result. Restore the acquisition, approve the correction, rerun the disposal and compare the old and new export. A reviewer should be able to explain the change without reconstructing it from screenshots.

| Test case | Pass condition to agree before the demo |
| --- | --- |
| Historical acquisition missing | Record is surfaced as an exception with an owner and evidence request |
| Transfer across controlled accounts | Both movements are linked under the approved policy without losing acquisition history |
| Same symbol on two chains | Network and contract identity are retained, not merged by ticker alone |
| Fee paid in another asset | Separate movement and valuation inputs remain inspectable |
| Duplicate API and CSV import | Duplicate detection preserves the original source identifiers |
| Backdated correction | Prior output remains available and the changed calculation is traceable |
| Source disconnects | The stale period is visible and backfill does not silently duplicate records |
| Accountant leaves the organization | Access is removed without losing reviewer attribution |
| Vendor contract ends | Raw inputs, mappings, lots, decisions and usable outputs can be exported |

These are proposed acceptance tests. FluidRWA has not run this dataset through the providers.

### Form 1099-DA: ask the right period-specific question

The [IRS's 2026 Form 1099-DA instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da) distinguish covered from noncovered digital assets and describe different basis-reporting treatment, including optional reporting methods for certain transactions. Do not assume that every broker statement supplies complete acquisition history or that one rule applies to every token and account. Have a qualified adviser define your entity's requirements, then ask the vendor to demonstrate those requirements using the correct reporting year.

Keep calculation, filing and recipient delivery as separate acceptance gates. A correct lot calculation does not prove that a form was submitted, accepted, delivered or corrected. Retain the relevant account, approval, submission and correction identifiers alongside the reported amount.

### Security evidence belongs in the proposal, not a logo comparison

Ask for the actual security report or trust-center materials covering the purchased service, examination period, relevant exceptions, subprocessors and customer responsibilities. Request read-only source permissions where feasible, retention controls, a deletion process and a recovery exercise. A SOC badge alone does not establish that your connector, entity or implementation is within scope.

## A repeatable close is a better test than a polished tax report

The difficult reporting case is not the trade with a complete purchase history. It is the disposal after an internal transfer, an expired exchange connection and a correction approved two weeks after close. Give each provider that same story and ask for a reproducible evidence trail.

Current product starting points are [TaxBit](https://www.taxbit.com/), [Ledgible](https://ledgible.io/) and [Lukka](https://lukka.tech/). Their accounting, data and tax-reporting offerings are not one interchangeable package. This matrix is a buyer test plan, not a vendor certification.

| Criterion | Required evidence | Acceptance exercise |
|---|---|---|
| Source completeness | Accounts, periods and import status | Identify a missing historical interval |
| Asset identity | Network and contract mapping | Separate similarly named tokens |
| Ownership | Controlled-account inventory | Match an internal transfer without a disposal assumption |
| Acquisition lots | Source-linked opening positions | Flag a missing acquisition rather than conceal it |
| Method governance | Adviser-approved policy and version | Reproduce a calculation under the selected method |
| Fees and valuation | Timestamped inputs and treatment | Trace a fee paid in another asset |
| Reconciliation | Quantity and value breaks | Explain a balance difference from source evidence |
| Reporting scope | Entity, year, forms and deliverables | Demonstrate the required output and approval |
| Corrections | Prior and replacement versions | Retain the original while producing a corrected export |
| Exit and audit | Exportable raw and derived records | Rebuild an approved result outside the dashboard |

### Separate calculation approval from filing approval

A calculation can be internally consistent while still being the wrong report for the entity or period. Have one acceptance step for source and lot reconstruction, then another for the reporting output. Name the reviewer responsible for taxpayer information, exceptions, recipient delivery and corrections.

The [IRS instructions for Form 1099-DA for 2026](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da) distinguish covered and noncovered digital assets. They are a specific US reporting reference, not a global tax rule or a reason to assume every source statement contains acquisition history. A qualified adviser should decide the requirements for the relevant entity and activity.

### Ten questions before committing the finance team

1. Which source periods and opening positions are included?
2. How are controlled-account transfers linked?
3. What identifies a token across networks?
4. How are missing acquisitions exposed?
5. Who approves classification and method changes?
6. Can a reviewer trace every output to raw evidence?
7. Which ERP integration is included and who owns rejected postings?
8. How are recipient delivery and corrections supported?
9. What charges apply to backfill, entities and exception review?
10. What complete evidence package can we export?
