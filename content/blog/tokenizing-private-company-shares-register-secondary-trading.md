## Choose the representation first

In its January 2026 [statement on tokenized securities](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/corp-fin-statement-tokenized-securities-012826-statement-tokenized-securities), SEC staff distinguish issuer-sponsored and third-party-sponsored models. This is staff guidance, not an approval or a legal determination for your offering.

| Design | What to establish before coding |
| --- | --- |
| Issuer-backed share representation | Which record is authoritative, how the share class is identified and how an approved transfer changes ownership |
| Intermediated holding or entitlement | Who legally holds the shares, what claim the holder receives and what happens if the intermediary exits |
| Synthetic exposure | Whether holders receive only a contractual return linked to the shares; do not describe it as direct ownership |

## Walk one share through five checkpoints

1. **Issue:** Match the authorizing documents, class and number of shares to the initial register entry. Keep the original subscription record.
2. **Onboard:** Verify investor identity and applicable restrictions under the chosen offering. Keep wallet permission separate from legal eligibility.
3. **Transfer:** Test consent, resale restrictions and a blocked transaction. Check whether the register updates from chain activity automatically or after a manual approval.
4. **Service:** Demonstrate a split, distribution or voting record date. Compare investor notices and corporate actions against the legal register.
5. **Exit:** Ask for a signed record export and a migration procedure. Test what happens if the technology vendor, wallet provider or trading venue becomes unavailable.

## Venue diligence: what the public register can and cannot tell you

Use the [SEC alternative trading system list](https://www.sec.gov/foia/frequently-requested-documents/alternative-trading-system-ats-list) to verify the operator name and list date for an applicable US venue. Then ask the venue to confirm the actual share class, offering structure, investor restrictions, settlement path and admission requirements in writing. An ATS listing is not proof that your shares are accepted, that they are legally transferable to a particular buyer, or that a buyer exists.

For vendor context use the [INX, tZERO and Texture Capital comparison](/blog/inx-vs-tzero-vs-texture-capital-digital-securities). For a worked procurement exercise, ask each candidate to show a blocked transfer, a corrected ownership record, and the finalized investor record for the same synthetic transaction.

## Which platform should a private company evaluate in 2026?

The useful shortlist starts with the ownership model, not with the ability to create a token. A company issuing its own shares needs a different arrangement from an intermediary offering exposure to somebody else's shares. Ask counsel to describe the holder's right in one paragraph before the technology team chooses infrastructure.

The [SEC staff statement dated January 28, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/corp-fin-statement-tokenized-securities-012826-statement-tokenized-securities), revisited November 19 for this section, distinguishes tokenization models. It is staff material, not a legal conclusion about your offering. Its practical procurement value is the question it forces: whose security or obligation does the token represent?

| Buyer situation | First workflow to evaluate | Do not promise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company issuing its own shares | Issuer and shareholder register integrated with subscription and transfer approvals | That a token standard settles corporate authority or investor eligibility |
| Existing shareholder seeking a transfer | Consent, restrictions, approved purchaser and register update | That technical wallet transfer creates an unrestricted resale market |
| Platform offering an entitlement | Holding structure, intermediary obligations and servicing | That indirect exposure is identical to direct registered ownership |
| Broker or venue considering admission | Instrument review, permitted investors and settlement | That a general venue registration admits this particular security |

For a software starting point, [Tokeny's platform documentation](https://docs.tokeny.com/docs/t-rex-platform) describes issuer and lifecycle tools. Use the [broader tokenization shortlist](/blog/top-tokenization-companies-2026) to compare product roles, then obtain confirmation for your exact instrument. This is not an assertion that any platform or venue accepts your company.

### Make a corrected register entry the decisive demo

Suppose a wallet is replaced after onboarding. The company must still know who holds the shares, which wallet may act and how the change became effective. Ask the operator to show the authorization, old and new record, technical update and investor notice together.

Then test a transfer that is technically valid but not approved under the applicable restrictions. The system should expose the controlling decision rather than allowing a token movement to become an unexplained ownership discrepancy.

Finally request a complete exit pack: holder and share-class records, transaction history, approvals, corporate actions and a migration process. A platform that is easy to enter but difficult to leave may become an expensive recordkeeping dependency.

## A practical acceptance rule

Approve the workflow only when legal records, administrator statements and technical events agree on holder, share class, quantity and effective time. Escalate a discrepancy before settling or reporting. This article is a workflow guide, not legal advice or an assertion of any provider's current regulatory status.
