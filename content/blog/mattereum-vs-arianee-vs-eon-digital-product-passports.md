## Short Answer

Mattereum, Arianee and EON all connect physical products to digital records, but they begin from different operating problems.

Mattereum's asset-passport model emphasizes the legal, contractual and evidentiary information needed to make claims about an asset credible. Arianee offers digital product passport infrastructure and consumer-facing access to specifications, certificates, maintenance and ownership experiences. EON's Product Cloud and digital IDs are oriented toward enterprise product data and circular services such as resale, repair and recycling.

The first procurement decision is therefore not vendor selection. It is whether the passport exists primarily to support enforceable asset rights, regulatory product data, customer engagement, authentication, ownership transfer or circular commerce.

## Comparison at a Glance

| Decision area | Mattereum | Arianee | EON |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clearest orientation | Legal and evidentiary asset passports | Open digital product passports and ownership experiences | Enterprise digital IDs and product-cloud services |
| Strong use case | High-value physical assets, warranties and transferable claims | Brand products requiring accessible lifecycle records and engagement | Large catalogs connecting item identity to resale, repair and recycling |
| Buyer must validate | Legal enforceability and evidence governance | Data model, ownership experience and ecosystem integration | Catalog integration, identifier binding and downstream service adoption |
| Main failure risk | Treating a digital record as enforceable without sound contracts | Launching a passport that lacks authoritative, maintained data | Creating IDs without useful lifecycle transactions or partner participation |

## Mattereum

Mattereum describes asset passports as containers for information and claims about real-world assets, including warranties, certifications, provenance and contractual rights. This orientation is important for assets where a buyer needs more than a marketing page: the record must explain who stands behind a claim and what remedy follows if it is wrong.

**Strong fit:** Valuable goods, collectibles, equipment or other assets where legal rights, condition evidence, warranties and transfer need to travel together.

**Verify:** The legal wrapper, governing jurisdiction, identity of claim issuers, evidence standards, inspection process, dispute pathway, warranty backing, transfer mechanics, privacy, data survival and what remains enforceable if the technology provider changes.

## Arianee

Arianee positions an open digital product passport infrastructure that allows people to access product information through mechanisms such as QR codes. Its Passport Portal highlights specifications, certificates, documents, safety information, maintenance history, ownership transfer and role-based access. This makes the product relevant to brands building persistent product relationships and compliance-ready records.

**Strong fit:** Consumer and durable-goods brands that need accessible product information, ownership experiences and lifecycle communication across a large installed base.

**Verify:** Identifier security, product activation, access roles, wallet assumptions, data interoperability, supplier participation, ownership transfer, offline and legacy-product support, analytics, consent and export of the full product history.

## EON

EON presents a Product Cloud and digital-ID system designed to connect products with lifecycle data and services. Public materials emphasize traceability from production through resale and recycling, together with authentication, compliance, repair and circular-commerce workflows.

**Strong fit:** Enterprises seeking to connect existing product catalogs and operational systems to resale, repair, recycling and customer services at scale.

**Verify:** ERP and PLM integration, item-level versus product-level identity, data onboarding, channel and marketplace integrations, physical tags, service-partner participation, event updates, consumer access and the economics of maintaining records after the first sale.

## The Passport Data Model

| Layer | Minimum question |
|---|---|
| Product identity | What uniquely identifies the model, batch or individual item? |
| Physical binding | Why should a verifier trust that the code or tag belongs to this object? |
| Provenance | Which parties created each claim and what evidence supports it? |
| Compliance | Which mandatory data must be available, to whom and for how long? |
| Ownership | Is ownership recorded, transferable and legally meaningful? |
| Lifecycle | Who can add repairs, inspections, resale, recall and recycling events? |
| Continuity | Can the complete record survive a vendor, brand or service-provider change? |

Blockchain can help with shared state and tamper evidence, but it cannot make bad source data true. The governance of issuers, inspectors, repairers, owners and recyclers matters more than the presence of a token.

## Pilot Design

1. Select one product line with clear economic and compliance value.
2. Define every required field and its authoritative owner.
3. Decide which information is public, owner-only, partner-only or regulator-only.
4. Bind a sample of physical items to identifiers and attempt deliberate swaps.
5. Test first sale, ownership transfer, repair, resale, recall and end-of-life events.
6. Export the data and reconstruct the record outside the platform.
7. Interview consumers and service partners about whether the passport changes behavior.
8. Calculate per-item and ongoing maintenance costs, not only launch cost.

## Procurement Recommendation

Shortlist Mattereum when enforceable claims, warranties and legal accountability are central. Shortlist Arianee when open passport access, ownership experience and brand-to-customer lifecycle communication are central. Shortlist EON when enterprise catalog integration and circular services across resale, repair and recycling are central.

Some programs may combine these concerns, but the buyer should appoint one system of record and define how evidence, ownership and lifecycle events move between systems.
