## Quick Answer: What to Compare First

Start with a single offering and follow it from investor application through redemption. For a private fund, a useful comparison must show who approves a subscription, confirms cash, authorizes minting, maintains the ownership record and resolves a failed redemption. A token-creation demo leaves most of that purchase decision unanswered.

Use the matrix below to decide which responsibilities you want to buy and which your team will retain. For provider discovery, use the [platform shortlist](/blog/top-tokenization-companies-2026) or [tokenization platform directory](/vendors/tokenization-platforms/). Once the scope is clear, send the existing [tokenization platform RFP](/blog/rwa-tokenization-platform-rfp-template) to shortlisted providers.

## Procurement Decision Matrix

These operating models can be combined. The columns describe responsibilities to evaluate, not interchangeable products or a ranking.

| Control | Managed services | Modular software | Network controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | Named entities and service roles | Issuer tools and partner handoffs | Identity and asset rules |
| Evidence | Offering and service agreements | API, deployment and export scope | Runtime and rule configuration |
| Pilot | Subscription through redemption | Integration failures and recovery | Rejected and privileged transfers |

Managed services can reduce separate handoffs, but only for the entities and activities included in the contract. Modular software gives your team more choice over portals and partners while leaving more integration ownership with you. Network-level controls still need applications, operating staff and a legal offering around them.

Tokeny's [March 12, 2025 service terms](https://tokeny.com/general-terms-of-service) identify Tokeny S.à r.l., registered in Luxembourg, and describe technology services for professional clients. That establishes an entity and service context, not eligibility for every asset or investor jurisdiction. Ask counsel to map each proposed service to the exact legal entity and offering documents.

### Test minting and privileged actions separately

Ordinary transfer rules do not necessarily govern every administrative action. The [ERC-3643 specification](https://ercs.ethereum.org/ERCS/erc-3643) distinguishes ordinary transfers from minting and forced transfers: the latter check the receiving identity but bypass the compliance rules; burning bypasses eligibility checks. Test the actual implementation, agent permissions and approval record for each path.

Polymesh documentation likewise says an asset with no compliance requirements permits transfers by default, and compliance can be paused. Its transfer-restriction documentation describes exemptions. Ask who may change those settings and how an auditor would detect the change. A permissioned standard or network does not by itself guarantee a restrictive configuration.

## What an Asset Tokenization Platform Actually Does

An asset tokenization platform helps an issuer represent an asset, security, fund interest, debt instrument, real estate interest, carbon credit or other real-world asset as a digital token.

But the token is only one part of the system.

A serious platform may also support:

- Issuer onboarding
- Offering setup
- Investor onboarding
- KYB and KYC workflows
- Wallet allowlisting
- Transfer restrictions
- Cap table or holder registry
- Primary issuance
- Secondary transfer controls
- Reporting
- Investor dashboards
- Custody integration
- Stablecoin or fiat settlement
- Corporate actions
- Redemption workflows
- Audit records

The question is not only "can this platform create a token?" The better question is "can this platform run the asset after issuance?"

## Main Types of Tokenization Platforms

### Securities and fund tokenization platforms

These platforms are designed for regulated financial products.

Good for:

- Tokenized funds
- Private credit
- Tokenized treasuries
- Digital bonds
- Private equity
- Real estate funds
- structured products

What to check:

- Transfer restrictions
- Investor eligibility
- fund administrator integrations
- qualified custody options
- distribution controls
- reporting
- legal-document workflow

Securitize's [infrastructure overview](https://own.securitize.io/) describes its BUIDL tokenization work. Treat that as a disclosed implementation example, not evidence that a different offering will be admitted on the same terms.

Tokeny positions its T-REX platform as a turnkey tokenization platform for financial institutions, with issuer servicing, investor applications, compliance, cap table and corporate action workflows.

### White-label marketplace platforms

These platforms help institutions launch branded marketplaces or investor portals.

Good for:

- Asset managers
- investment platforms
- wealth platforms
- private market marketplaces
- broker or distribution networks
- regional tokenized asset platforms

What to check:

- Investor portal quality
- order flow
- transfer rules
- KYC workflow
- branding control
- secondary transfer model
- wallet support
- reporting

### Compliance-native token frameworks

Some platforms are built around permissioned tokens and identity-based compliance.

Good for:

- regulated securities
- jurisdiction-restricted assets
- accredited investor workflows
- controlled transfer environments
- tokenized assets that need built-in compliance logic

What to check:

- What compliance logic is onchain
- Which identity providers are supported
- How claims expire or update
- Whether rules can be changed
- What happens when an investor becomes ineligible

Tokeny's ERC-3643 documentation describes permissioned tokens with onchain identity and compliance conditions. Polymesh documentation describes compliance rules based on identity claims, KYC, accreditation and jurisdiction.

### Enterprise asset digitization platforms

These platforms may focus less on securities distribution and more on operational efficiency.

Good for:

- internal asset registries
- supply chain assets
- carbon or climate assets
- enterprise receivables
- data sharing
- settlement workflows

What to check:

- Integration with existing systems
- permissioning
- privacy
- reporting
- API design
- governance

### API-first tokenization infrastructure

API-first vendors are useful when the buyer wants to build its own interface and keep tokenization logic in the background.

Good for:

- fintech products
- embedded finance
- custom investor portals
- neobanks
- exchanges
- institutions with internal product teams

What to check:

- API documentation
- sandbox quality
- webhooks
- developer support
- token lifecycle APIs
- compliance APIs
- wallet and custody integrations

## The Tokenization Platform Comparison Checklist

### Asset type fit

Ask which assets the platform has supported in production.

Examples:

- Fund interests
- Treasuries
- Bonds
- Real estate
- private credit
- commodities
- carbon credits
- invoices
- equities
- stablecoins
- tokenized deposits

Do not assume a platform that works for one asset type works for another.

### Legal structure support

The platform should support the legal structure, not invent it.

Ask:

- Does the platform support your jurisdiction?
- Can it map tokens to legal ownership or contractual rights?
- Can it support investor disclosures?
- Can it support transfer restrictions required by counsel?
- Can it support redemption, distributions or income events?

### Compliance controls

For regulated assets, compliance controls are central.

Ask:

- Can investors be KYC'd or KYB'd?
- Can wallets be allowlisted?
- Can transfers be blocked when a user is ineligible?
- Can rules be updated?
- Can compliance status expire?
- Can jurisdiction and accreditation status be reflected?
- What audit trail exists?

### Custody and wallet workflow

Custody should be solved before launch.

Ask:

- Which custody providers are integrated?
- Can investors use external wallets?
- Is wallet recovery supported?
- Are issuer wallets controlled by policy?
- Can qualified custody be used?
- Are stablecoins and cash handled?

### Distribution and liquidity

An issuance capability does not establish investor demand or a secondary market for your asset. Trading access depends on the instrument, eligible counterparties, venue admission and service agreements; liquidity is not guaranteed.

Ask:

- Can the platform support primary issuance?
- Can it support secondary transfers?
- Are there marketplace or ATS connections?
- Can distributors participate?
- How are transfer restrictions enforced after distribution?

### Reporting and lifecycle management

The asset still needs to operate after issuance.

Ask:

- Can the issuer see holders?
- Are tax reports supported?
- Are distributions supported?
- Can corporate actions be handled?
- Can documents be updated?
- Can redemption workflows be run?
- Are audit exports available?

### Integration depth

Ask whether the platform integrates with:

- KYC and AML providers
- custodians
- payment providers
- fund administrators
- transfer agents
- investor portals
- exchanges or ATSs
- oracles
- data providers
- legal workflow tools

## Platform Fit by Buyer Type

Different buyers should prioritize different operating requirements.

### Asset managers and fund issuers

Prioritize:

- investor eligibility and subscription workflow
- transfer restrictions
- fund administration and reporting support
- custody integration
- redemption and distribution workflow
- institutional references

Watch for:

- platform demos that stop at issuance
- weak support for fund lifecycle events
- no clear role for the transfer agent, administrator or custodian

### Real estate sponsors

Prioritize:

- SPV or fund workflow support
- investor portal usability
- subscription documents
- cap table or holder records
- distribution and tax reporting process
- transfer restrictions and approval flows

Watch for:

- claims of instant liquidity without a real marketplace path
- unclear securities compliance
- no support for ongoing investor servicing

### Private credit platforms

Prioritize:

- payment schedules and loan servicing events
- investor eligibility and transfer restrictions
- reporting for cash flows, collateral and borrower updates
- integration with administrators, agents or servicing systems
- redemption, repayment and default handling

Watch for:

- generic token issuance without credit lifecycle logic
- no plan for offchain collateral or borrower data
- weak reporting for investors and auditors

### Banks and financial institutions

Prioritize:

- regulatory and governance evidence
- custody and policy controls
- audit logs and access controls
- integration with internal systems
- role-based permissions
- vendor resilience and support

Watch for:

- crypto-native workflow assumptions that do not fit internal controls
- limited private-network, reporting or permissioning options
- unclear contractual responsibility

### Web3 startups and fintech builders

Prioritize:

- API quality
- speed to prototype
- wallet and payment integrations
- pricing flexibility
- developer support
- modular architecture

Watch for:

- enterprise-heavy onboarding that slows testing
- limited customization
- vendor lock-in before product-market fit

## Platform vs Adjacent Vendor Stack

A tokenization platform is rarely the only vendor in the project.

Common adjacent vendors include:

- legal and regulatory counsel to define the asset and offering
- KYC and AML providers to verify investors and counterparties
- custody providers to safeguard assets and wallets
- smart contract developers for custom logic or integrations
- security auditors for independent review
- payment or stablecoin providers for subscription and redemption flows
- fund administrators, transfer agents or reporting providers for operational records

If a platform claims to handle everything, ask what is native, what is partner-led and what remains the issuer's responsibility.

## Common Mistakes When Comparing Platforms

### Treating all tokenization platforms as the same

Some platforms are issuer portals. Some are infrastructure APIs. Some are marketplaces. Some are compliance token frameworks. Some are regulated operators. Compare the actual workflow.

### Ignoring the legal wrapper

The token does not automatically define investor rights. Legal documents, issuer structure and jurisdiction matter.

### Underestimating custody

The platform may issue the token, but custody still needs a decision for issuer assets, investor wallets and reserves.

### Not checking transfer restrictions

For regulated assets, unrestricted transferability can create compliance problems.

### Building without distribution

Tokenization without investor access, market access or operational use can become a technical demo instead of a business.

## Evidence to Request Before a Pilot

Keep a decision record for each mandatory requirement: **documented**, **demonstrated in your pilot**, **contracted**, or **unresolved**. These are different kinds of evidence, not points to add into a vendor score. An unresolved legal or operational requirement should remain visible even when the demo looks good.

- **Offering and responsibility map:** legal issuer, asset rights, permitted investor classes and jurisdictions, ownership register of record, and the party accountable for each regulated activity. Request the proposed agreements and applicable register entries; a brand-level license badge is insufficient.
- **Deployment record:** network and environment, token or asset identifier, contract/runtime version, enabled rules, upgrade rights, owner/agent keys and approval policy. Separate technical network support from agreed production support.
- **Integration record:** exact KYC, custody and administrator product/entity, API version, data fields, reconciliation owner, error handling and support boundary. A partner logo alone does not prove an operational integration.
- **Operations and exit pack:** sample holder and transaction exports, document access, redemptions, corporate actions, incident escalation, data retention and migration rights. Request an export your replacement operator can actually reconcile.

The [October Network Explorer findings](/network-explorer/research/tokenized-fund-infrastructure-october-2026#october-findings) show how disclosed fund arrangements divide infrastructure roles. Use them to identify questions about custody, administration and distribution; the research sample is not a market census, provider ranking or proof that an integration is available for your offering.

## Run the Same Implementation Pilot with Each Finalist

For a proposed private fund, agree the legal structure and permitted investor cases with counsel first. Use fictional investor records and a sandbox or test network; do not upload real identity documents simply to compare demos. This is a proposed acceptance exercise, not a completed platform test.

| Pilot case | Evidence to retain | Acceptance question |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible subscription and issuance | Approval, signed documents, cash reconciliation, mint authorization and holder record | Can the team trace one subscription to one ownership entry without unexplained differences? |
| Ineligible or expired investor | Rejection reason, claim/rule version and attempted transaction | Do the configured restrictions work through both the portal and direct transaction paths? |
| Administrative exception | Mint, freeze, forced transfer, recovery and rule-change approvals | Can only authorized roles act, with the exceptions and resulting register changes explained? |
| Failed integration or settlement | Duplicate callback, timeout, retry and reconciliation log | Is there one accountable owner, with no duplicate issuance or unexplained cash/token mismatch? |
| Redemption and exit | Request, eligibility check, cash instruction, burn/cancellation and exported register | Can the operator reconcile the final position and hand records to a successor? |

Set expected outcomes and stop conditions before running the cases. Have the administrator reconcile the legal register, the platform's records and onchain balances; have compliance inspect exceptions. Record any manual workaround, who must operate it and whether it is included in the proposal.

Compare total operating scope after the pilot: implementation work, recurring modules, external KYC/custody/administration, network costs, support and exit assistance. Public documentation does not establish negotiated pricing, implementation time, performance or acceptance of your offering. No provider account or production deployment was tested for this guide.
