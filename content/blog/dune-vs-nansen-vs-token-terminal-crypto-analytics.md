## Analytics Tools Answer Different Questions

Crypto analytics is not one category. A founder may need to know whether users are active. A compliance team may need to understand suspicious wallet behavior. A fund may need comparable protocol metrics. A BD team may need ecosystem traction. A tokenization platform may need to monitor wallet flows, investor cohorts and operational activity.

Dune, Nansen and Token Terminal are all strong names, but they are built around different analytical jobs.

## Short Answer

Dune is strongest when teams need customizable onchain dashboards, SQL-based analysis and public or private data views.

Nansen is strongest when teams need wallet labels, token flow intelligence, smart-money monitoring, portfolio context and market signals.

Token Terminal is strongest when teams need protocol fundamentals, financial-style metrics and comparable analysis across crypto networks and applications.

## Dune: Best Fit for Custom Onchain Dashboards

Dune is widely used for onchain analytics, community dashboards and SQL-based exploration. Analysts can build dashboards from blockchain datasets, publish queries and create views that others can reuse.

Dune may fit when the buyer needs:

- custom onchain dashboards
- SQL query workflows
- public market dashboards
- protocol activity tracking
- holder and transfer analysis
- ecosystem data storytelling
- analyst-friendly query access
- shareable research pages

The diligence question is whether the data needed for the buyer's chain, contract and event model is available and fresh enough. Dune can be very powerful when the data is accessible, but buyers still need analysts who can define the right queries.

For tokenization teams, Dune may help with public RWA dashboards, token holder analysis, protocol transparency pages and market education.

## Nansen: Best Fit for Wallet Intelligence and Market Signals

Nansen is known for wallet labels and market intelligence. Instead of only asking what happened onchain, teams use Nansen to ask who may be behind the activity, which wallet cohorts are moving, and how capital flows through a market.

Nansen may fit when the buyer needs:

- wallet labels and entity context
- smart-money monitoring
- token flow analysis
- market intelligence
- portfolio and wallet behavior views
- exchange and fund flow context
- alerts around wallet activity
- ecosystem discovery

The diligence question is label quality and coverage. Wallet attribution is probabilistic and should not be treated as legal identity. Teams should understand how labels are used, what confidence level is appropriate and when a compliance-grade tool is required instead.

For tokenization teams, Nansen may help monitor market behavior, investor segments, ecosystem flows and high-value wallet activity.

## Token Terminal: Best Fit for Protocol Fundamentals

Token Terminal approaches crypto analytics through financial and business metrics. It is useful when a team wants to compare networks and protocols through revenue, fees, active users, incentives, token economics and other fundamentals.

Token Terminal may fit when the buyer needs:

- protocol revenue and fee metrics
- comparable network fundamentals
- market maps and sector views
- valuation-style analysis
- adoption and usage metrics
- dashboards for investors or strategy teams
- API access to standardized metrics
- research into crypto business models

The diligence question is metric definition. Buyers should understand how revenue, fees, token incentives and active users are calculated before relying on comparisons.

For tokenization teams, Token Terminal may help assess ecosystem maturity, chain traction, DeFi market structure and infrastructure-provider context.

## What Buyers Should Compare

Analytics tooling should start with the decision:

- Are we trying to monitor operations?
- Are we trying to understand users?
- Are we trying to compare protocols?
- Are we trying to create public dashboards?
- Are we trying to evaluate market risk?
- Are we trying to support investor reporting?
- Are we trying to generate leads or educate buyers?

Then compare:

- chain coverage
- data freshness
- label methodology
- metric definitions
- dashboard flexibility
- query language requirements
- API access
- export options
- permissioning
- alerting
- support
- pricing at team scale

For regulated products, analytics tools should not be confused with compliance systems. A dashboard may identify useful patterns, but sanctions screening, AML monitoring and case management need dedicated controls.

## Practical Recommendation

Use Dune when the priority is custom onchain dashboards, transparent SQL queries and shareable public analytics.

Use Nansen when the priority is wallet intelligence, labels, smart-money signals and market monitoring.

Use Token Terminal when the priority is comparable protocol fundamentals and financial-style crypto metrics.

For FluidRWA-style vendor research, all three can be useful: Dune for public dashboards, Nansen for wallet flow context and Token Terminal for market maturity analysis.
