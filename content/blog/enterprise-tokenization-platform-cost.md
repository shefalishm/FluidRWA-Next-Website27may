## Tokenization Platform Cost: Short Answer

Public software pricing rarely captures the complete cost of launching and operating a tokenized asset. The platform is one layer in a legal, compliance, custody, payment and administration stack.

Do not publish or approve a budget based on a universal dollar range. Request current proposals for the exact asset and operating model, then compare three-year total cost.

## Complete Cost Model

| Cost layer | What it may include | Main cost driver |
|---|---|---|
| Legal and product design | Structure, offering documents, rights, jurisdictions and contracts | Asset complexity and market coverage |
| Platform | Setup, license, modules, environments and usage | Investors, assets, wallets, transactions or AUM |
| Implementation | Configuration, custom development, migration and integration | Number of systems and custom workflows |
| Smart contracts | Design, deployment, upgrades and audits | Novelty, value at risk and chain count |
| Identity and compliance | KYC, KYB, AML, accreditation, screening and cases | Investor type, countries and review rate |
| Custody and wallets | Safekeeping, MPC, policies, transactions and recovery | Asset type, value, volume and control model |
| Cash and settlement | Banking, stablecoins, ramps, payment processing and reconciliation | Currencies, payment methods and jurisdictions |
| Administration | Register, NAV, statements, distributions and redemptions | Product lifecycle and investor count |
| Distribution | Broker, placement, marketplace, marketing and investor acquisition | Channel and capital target |
| Internal operations | Staff, oversight, reconciliation, support and incident response | Automation quality and exception volume |

## Pricing Models You May Encounter

- fixed implementation fee
- annual platform license
- fee per issuer, asset or vehicle
- fee per investor, wallet or account
- transaction or settlement fee
- basis points on assets or issuance
- API or usage tiers
- premium support and service levels
- custom-development day rates
- required third-party pass-through charges

A low entry price can become expensive if basic lifecycle events, environments, integrations or support are separate modules.

## Three-Year Cost Scenarios

Ask every vendor to price the same assumptions.

### Base case

One asset, one jurisdiction, modest investor count, standard integrations and planned transaction volume.

### Growth case

Multiple vehicles or share classes, additional jurisdictions, more investors, higher transaction volume and one extra chain or distribution channel.

### Stress case

High manual-review rate, heavy support, provider migration, security incident, chain disruption or unplanned regulatory and product change.

Include internal hours and third-party bills, not only invoices from the platform.

## Build vs Buy

### Buying a platform

Can reduce time to launch and provide established workflows, but may introduce license cost, configuration limits, provider dependencies and migration constraints.

### Custom development

Can provide control and product differentiation, but the buyer owns architecture, secure development, testing, audits, upgrades, monitoring, integrations and long-term maintenance.

### Hybrid approach

Many institutions use a platform for commodity workflows and custom components for differentiation. This can work well when boundaries, data ownership and operational responsibility are explicit.

The cheapest first-year option is not necessarily the lowest-risk three-year option.

## Hidden Cost Questions

1. Which features shown in the demonstration cost extra?
2. Which integrations require professional services?
3. Are test and disaster-recovery environments included?
4. Who pays blockchain fees and third-party charges?
5. What usage creates overages?
6. How much manual work remains after implementation?
7. What support is available during a failed lifecycle event?
8. How are renewals and price increases calculated?
9. What costs apply to data export and migration?
10. Which roadmap features are assumed in the proposal?

## Budgeting Mistakes

- comparing license fees without implementation
- omitting legal and regulated intermediaries
- assuming tokenization creates distribution or liquidity
- ignoring investor support and exception handling
- underfunding audits, monitoring and incident response
- failing to model additional jurisdictions or chains
- accepting unpriced partner dependencies
- leaving exit and migration out of the contract
