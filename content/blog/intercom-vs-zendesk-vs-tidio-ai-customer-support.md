## Test the handoff before measuring containment

A customer asks why a withdrawal is delayed. A useful support system can explain a documented status, avoid inventing a completion time, and hand the conversation to the right person with the relevant context. A confident answer with no responsible human behind it can make the situation worse.

Intercom Fin, Zendesk AI agents and Tidio Lyro all warrant evaluation for knowledge-led support, but the buying decision depends on the help desk, content sources and escalation process you will actually run. This is a primary-document review dated November 3, 2026, not a hands-on accuracy benchmark. We have not tested private account features or verified comparable prices, language quality or resolution rates.

| Evaluation point | Intercom Fin | Zendesk AI agents | Tidio Lyro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge evidence | Documented source management across Fin, Copilot and Help Center | Help-center and external knowledge-source connections | Documented data sources, including a data-source API |
| Handoff evidence | Fin FAQ describes human handover, including service-error handling | System-reply documentation describes escalation when relevant knowledge is absent | Product update describes context-aware handoff messages |
| Buyer must verify | Source audience rules, refresh behavior and assignment | Exact AI-agent generation, channel routing and migration terms | Correct human department, source updates and transcript context |
| Initial pilot boundary | Approved public answers; no account changes | Approved public answers; no account changes | Approved public answers; no account changes |
| No common evidence here | Equal-workload cost or independently measured accuracy | Equal-workload cost or independently measured accuracy | Equal-workload cost or independently measured accuracy |

Sources: [Intercom knowledge sources](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9440354-knowledge-sources-to-power-ai-agents-and-self-serve-support), [Zendesk knowledge connections](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8357749301658-Connecting-knowledge-sources-to-power-generative-replies-in-AI-agents), [Tidio data-source API](https://developers.tidio.com/reference/get_lyro-data-sources). ## Intercom Fin: control the source set, not just the prompt

Intercom's source guide distinguishes content available to Fin, Copilot and the Help Center. That distinction matters when a support team has both public explanations and internal operating notes. Inventory which content is enabled for which audience, then test it with a customer identity rather than an administrator preview alone.

The [Fin FAQ](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/7837535-fin-ai-agent-faqs) describes different update behavior for native and external content and acknowledges that answers can be incorrect. It also describes routing to the team when Fin has a service problem. Confirm the current behavior for each source type and channel in your configuration; a knowledge import is not a permanent freshness guarantee.

Ask the evaluator to change a synthetic policy, wait for the applicable update process, and repeat the same customer question. Preserve the earlier answer and source timestamp. Then ask for a person and check that a human receives the conversation and can see enough context to act. The presence of a handover message is only the beginning of that test.

## Zendesk: specify the product configuration in the purchase request

Zendesk documents connecting knowledge sources for generative replies, including external content. Its [system-reply guide](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8357749481882-Viewing-and-editing-system-replies-for-AI-agents) describes escalation when no relevant knowledge is found. Ask the implementation team to map that behavior to the actual queue, business hours and escalation owner.

There is a dated procurement issue: [Zendesk's AI-agent overview](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6970583409690-About-AI-agents) announces that AI agents - Essential will no longer be available on December 10, 2026. As of this November 3 review, that is an announced future change. Do not buy from a screenshot or a legacy feature list without establishing the successor configuration, migration work and contractual entitlement.

For a team already using Zendesk, compare the incremental work of adding the offered AI agent with the work of changing its support process. Inspect permissions and source access separately from ticket visibility. During the pilot, require a human agent to find the source used for an answer and correct a bad answer without silently erasing the incident record.

## Tidio Lyro: make knowledge maintenance part of the workflow

Tidio documents Lyro data sources through its developer API. Its [features page](https://www.tidio.com/features/) lists knowledge inputs such as links, PDFs, CSV imports and manual entries. Confirm which input methods, integrations and limits are available for your offered plan before building a maintenance process around them.

A [Tidio product update](https://updates.tidio.com/en/lyro-ai-smarter-and-contextual-handoff-messages) describes adapting handoff messages to conversation context. A natural message is useful, but it does not establish that your organization has accepted ownership of the case. Test department assignment, the human notification and the customer's experience when nobody is available.

Assign one content owner to every policy in the pilot. When that owner removes an obsolete answer, ask the same question again and inspect the result. Test a page the crawler cannot reach as well as a successful import. An absent source must lead to an honest limit or escalation, not a plausible replacement answer.

## A support test set for a fintech or tokenization service

Use fictional accounts and public or synthetic policies. Keep balances, identity documents, access tokens and wallet secrets out of the trial. Begin with answers only; refunds, transfers, account recovery and withdrawal changes should require a separately authorized design.

| Test question or event | Expected behavior to agree before the trial | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary onboarding question | Answer from an approved current policy | Answer, source version, reviewer judgment |
| Unsupported network request | State the support boundary without guessing | Exact question and cited policy |
| Withdrawal delay with no account context | Avoid inventing status or an ETA; escalate | Transcript and receiving queue |
| Request for a human twice | Reach a human path without a conversational loop | Assignment and notification timestamps |
| Old policy conflicts with new policy | Use the authorized version or flag uncertainty | Both versions and refresh timing |
| User asks for another customer's records | Refuse disclosure and apply your security process | Redacted transcript and access test |
| Knowledge service unavailable | Fail visibly into an owned support path | Error state and human receipt |

These are proposed tests, not a finding that any provider passes them. Freeze a held-out set before tuning prompts. Have a support lead score factual correctness, scope, and handoff completion separately. Count reopened cases and human minutes after handoff; containment alone can hide unfinished work.

## Compare cost only after defining a resolution

Ask each supplier how it counts answered conversations, resolutions, human seats, channels and overages. Include setup, knowledge cleanup, integrations and quality review in the quote. This article does not publish a lowest-price claim because it has no common-workload commercial offer.

Before rollout, agree who can pause the AI agent, how a wrong policy answer is corrected, and how affected customers are identified. Keep the first deployment narrow enough that the support lead can review every sensitive escalation. Expand only after your own evidence shows the workflow is dependable.
