## The short answer

FalconX, B2C2 and GSR are credible starting points for institutional digital-asset execution, but the buyer-fit differs. FalconX presents an integrated prime-brokerage proposition covering spot, derivatives, financing, direct market access and connected custody. B2C2 is a principal liquidity provider and OTC counterparty. GSR combines systematic and high-touch OTC with market making, fiat connectivity and tokenized-asset liquidity initiatives.

Do not select a counterparty using website volume figures or asset counts alone. An institution needs executable evidence for its own ticket sizes, instruments, jurisdictions, settlement assets, custody model and credit requirements.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Decision factor | FalconX | B2C2 | GSR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary orientation | Institutional prime brokerage | Principal OTC liquidity provider | Systematic OTC, market making and capital-markets services |
| Public product breadth | Spot, derivatives, FX, EMS, financing, DMA and custody connectivity | Principal spot and derivatives liquidity, subject to entity and jurisdiction | Systematic and high-touch OTC, market making, derivatives and treasury support |
| Execution access | UI, API, RFQ, streaming and algorithms | Bilateral principal dealing and electronic connectivity | UI, API, systematic RFQ and high-touch execution |
| Distinctive angle | Integrated trading and capital solutions | Focused dealer and principal-counterparty model | Broad crypto-fiat coverage plus issuer and tokenized-asset liquidity work |
| Good fit | Funds needing trading plus financing and portfolio-level services | Institutions prioritizing consistent bilateral liquidity | Funds, fintechs and issuers needing execution or market-making support |
| Main diligence point | Product entity, cross-product margin, custody separation and credit | Counterparty exposure, pricing behavior and settlement | Entity, execution method, conflicts, market-making terms and settlement |

## Provider profiles

### FalconX

FalconX describes a broad institutional platform spanning electronic and high-touch spot, OTC derivatives, FX, an execution-management system, financing and direct market access. Its public materials also describe integrations for qualified custody in selected U.S. workflows.

**Good fit:** Asset managers, hedge funds, ETF participants and large fintechs seeking fewer hand-offs between execution, financing and operational services.

**Verify:** Contracting entity, regulated status by product, asset and fiat coverage, API behavior, principal versus agency role, margin, collateral, custody segregation, settlement, default rights, balance-sheet exposure and business-continuity arrangements.

### B2C2

B2C2 publicly describes itself as a principal liquidity provider: it acts as counterparty and deals on its own account. Its services cover institutional crypto liquidity, and recent materials include tokenized-gold OTC trading with continuous market access and T+0 settlement, subject to jurisdiction.

**Good fit:** Professional counterparties that want direct dealer pricing and a clear principal-execution relationship.

**Verify:** Legal entity, permissions, products, supported pairs, RFQ protocol, quote validity, last-look policy, settlement method, credit line, prefunding, collateral, disclosure, conflicts and default treatment.

### GSR

GSR offers systematic OTC through UI and API, high-touch trading, derivatives and market making. It publicly emphasizes broad asset and fiat coverage and has launched OTC liquidity for selected tokenized real-world assets through regulated partners.

**Good fit:** Institutions and token issuers that need a combination of trading access, systematic execution and market-structure support.

**Verify:** Execution entity, jurisdiction, principal or agency role, fiat settlement network, market-making mandate, inventory obligations, token concentration, reporting, conflicts, termination and treatment of confidential issuer information.

## How to compare execution quality

Create a controlled sample of representative orders. Include liquid and less-liquid assets, normal and volatile sessions, different ticket sizes, fiat and stablecoin settlement, and at least one operational exception. Send comparable RFQs at the same time without creating an artificial information leak.

Measure:

- quoted spread and executable depth;
- fill ratio, reject rate and quote response time;
- slippage against a documented benchmark;
- market impact after execution;
- settlement time, fails and manual interventions;
- credit, collateral and prefunding cost;
- financing rate and margin treatment where relevant;
- quality and timeliness of confirmations and reporting.

A tight quote that requires expensive prefunding or creates repeated reconciliation breaks can be worse than a slightly wider all-in price.

## Counterparty and custody risk

Map where assets sit before, during and after a trade. If the provider offers integrated custody or connects to a qualified custodian, identify the separate legal entities, account title and moment assets leave protected custody. Determine whether netting, margin and flexible settlement create unsecured exposure.

Set counterparty limits independently of expected execution quality. Review audited financial information where available, capital, insurance, client-asset arrangements, default provisions and concentration. Do not assume a license for one activity covers every product listed on a global website.

## Best fit by buyer scenario

| Buyer scenario | Likely starting point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-strategy crypto fund needing finance and derivatives | FalconX | Prime-services breadth and cross-product workflows are central. |
| Institution seeking a direct principal liquidity counterparty | B2C2 | The dealer model is explicit and can be evaluated cleanly. |
| Token issuer needing market making plus OTC distribution | GSR | Market making and capital-markets services sit beside execution. |
| RWA venue seeking secondary token liquidity | GSR, then B2C2 depending asset coverage | GSR has public tokenized-asset liquidity initiatives; B2C2 documents tokenized-gold support. |
| Large buyer minimizing single-counterparty risk | Qualify at least two | Redundancy and competitive RFQs can be more important than platform consolidation. |

## RFP questions

1. Which legal entity provides each product in each jurisdiction?
2. When do you act as principal, agent, lender, custodian or technology provider?
3. Which assets, fiat currencies, order types and settlement networks are production-ready?
4. What quote, reject, last-look and error-trade policies apply?
5. How are collateral, margin and close-out amounts calculated?
6. What client assets or cash are held, and under what legal title?
7. Which records are available through API and for audit?
8. What happened during the provider's most material service incident in the last 24 months?
9. How can positions, collateral and history be migrated on termination?
10. Which services depend on another group company, custodian, exchange or bank?

## Final recommendation

FalconX is the strongest starting point when the brief is genuinely prime brokerage rather than spot execution alone. B2C2 offers a focused principal-liquidity model. GSR is particularly relevant when systematic OTC, broad crypto-fiat access, market making or tokenized-asset liquidity are part of one mandate.
