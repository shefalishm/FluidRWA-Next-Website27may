## Short Answer

RWA tokenization platforms should be compared by asset type, not by generic popularity. A platform designed for regulated securities may not fit private credit. A platform that works for real estate may not solve tokenized treasury operations. A product issuer may not be available as infrastructure for your own asset.

Common platforms and infrastructure providers to compare include Securitize, Tokeny, Taurus, Zoniqx, Brickken, Polymath, DigiShares, Centrifuge, Maple, Figure and Provenance. The right shortlist depends on issuance model, compliance controls, custody, investor onboarding, distribution and asset servicing.

## What An RWA Tokenization Platform Actually Does

An RWA tokenization platform may support:

- Asset setup
- Token issuance
- Investor onboarding
- KYC and KYB integration
- Transfer restrictions
- Cap table or holder registry
- Custody integration
- Payment and settlement workflows
- Investor reporting
- Redemption workflows
- Corporate actions or distributions
- Secondary transfer controls

Not every platform handles every part. Some are issuance software. Some are regulated marketplaces. Some are custody infrastructure. Some are protocols for credit or liquidity. Some are full-service tokenization providers.

## Platform Comparison By Use Case

| Use case | Vendors to evaluate | What matters most |
|---|---|---|
| Regulated securities | Securitize, Tokeny, Polymath, ADDX, Archax | Investor eligibility, transfer controls, regulatory workflow |
| Institutional custody plus tokenization | Taurus, Fireblocks, Copper | Custody, wallet controls, bank-grade operations |
| Issuer-led tokenization | Brickken, Zoniqx, DigiShares | Issuer dashboard, investor workflows, asset servicing |
| Private credit | Centrifuge, Maple, Figure, Provenance | Borrower onboarding, credit data, repayments, investor reporting |
| Tokenized treasuries | Securitize, Ondo, Superstate, Hashnote, Backed | Product structure, redemption, custody, eligible investors |
| Real estate | DigiShares, Brickken, Securitize, Tokeny | SPV workflows, investor records, distributions, compliance |

## How To Compare Tokenization Platforms

### 1. Asset Class Fit

Ask whether the platform has already supported assets like yours. Tokenized real estate, credit, treasuries, funds, commodities and securities have different operational requirements.

### 2. Investor Workflow

Review onboarding, KYC, KYB, investor eligibility, accreditation, wallet setup, subscriptions, redemptions and reporting.

### 3. Transfer Control

If tokens are regulated or restricted, the platform must support allowlists, jurisdiction controls, transfer-agent workflows, lockups or other policy logic.

### 4. Custody And Wallet Integrations

Confirm whether investors use self-custody, embedded wallets, institutional custody or qualified custodians. Custody affects both UX and compliance.

### 5. Lifecycle Servicing

The platform should support what happens after issuance: distributions, statements, tax reports, ownership changes, redemption windows and investor communications.

### 6. Distribution Path

Many tokenization projects fail because they issue tokens without a route to investors. Ask whether the platform supports marketplace access, broker-dealer partners, issuer distribution or private investor workflows.

## Buyer Questions

- Which asset classes are supported in production?
- Which jurisdictions and investor types are supported?
- Can the platform enforce transfer restrictions?
- Which KYC, KYB and custody providers integrate?
- Does the platform support lifecycle servicing?
- Does it support secondary transfers or marketplace access?
- What is the implementation timeline?
- What are setup, platform, transaction and servicing costs?
- Who owns the investor data and token contracts?
- What happens if the issuer migrates platforms?

## RWA Tokenization Platform Scoring Model

| Criterion | Weight | What to evaluate |
|---|---:|---|
| Asset-class fit | 20% | Real experience with the asset type, structure and investor workflow |
| Compliance workflow | 15% | KYC, KYB, eligibility checks, transfer controls and jurisdiction rules |
| Custody integration | 15% | Qualified custody, wallet support, issuer treasury and investor holdings |
| Lifecycle servicing | 15% | Distributions, redemptions, statements, reporting and corporate actions |
| Investor experience | 10% | Onboarding, wallet setup, subscription flow and support workflows |
| Distribution options | 10% | Marketplace access, issuer distribution, partner networks or private placement flows |
| Technical flexibility | 10% | APIs, chain support, data exports, token standards and migration options |
| Commercial fit | 5% | Setup fees, platform fees, transaction fees and long-term servicing costs |

For a regulated asset, the compliance workflow and custody integration can matter more than the token standard. For a crypto-native RWA protocol, liquidity, oracle design and DeFi composability may carry more weight.

## Best Fit By Asset Class

| Asset class | What the platform must handle |
|---|---|
| Tokenized treasuries | Eligible investors, custody, fund or note structure, redemption and yield reporting |
| Real estate | SPV structure, investor registry, distributions, transfer restrictions and reporting |
| Private credit | Borrower onboarding, credit data, payment schedules, collateral and reporting |
| Fund interests | Subscriptions, transfer agency, NAV, statements, redemptions and investor eligibility |
| Carbon credits | Registry connection, retirement, MRV data, provenance and double-counting controls |
| Commodities | Warehouse receipts, proof of reserves, custody, delivery rights and oracle data |

This is why "asset tokenization platform" is not specific enough as a buying category. The platform has to match the asset's legal and operational life.

## Implementation Timeline

A realistic tokenization platform project often moves through these stages:

1. **Asset readiness:** confirm legal structure, asset ownership, investor rules and target jurisdictions
2. **Vendor shortlist:** compare tokenization, custody, KYC, legal, audit and payment providers
3. **Workflow design:** map subscriptions, transfers, redemptions, reporting and admin permissions
4. **Platform setup:** configure assets, investor flows, token contracts and dashboards
5. **Security and compliance review:** review contracts, data flows, permissions and operating controls
6. **Pilot issuance:** run limited users, test reporting, validate support workflows
7. **Production launch:** open distribution, monitor operations and maintain servicing rhythm

Skipping the workflow design stage is one of the fastest ways to create rework.

## Red Flags

Avoid platforms that:

- Cannot explain who legally issues or controls the asset
- Treat custody as an afterthought
- Do not support investor eligibility or transfer restrictions
- Cannot export investor or transaction records
- Have unclear pricing for servicing, reporting or redemptions
- Do not support the asset lifecycle after issuance
- Promise liquidity without explaining distribution mechanics
- Cannot identify which responsibilities remain with the issuer

## Platform Versus Full Vendor Stack

A tokenization platform rarely replaces the full vendor stack. Buyers may still need:

- Legal counsel
- Fund administrator or transfer agent
- KYC and KYB provider
- Custody or wallet provider
- Smart contract auditor
- Oracle or reserve data provider
- Payments or stablecoin settlement provider
- Tax and reporting support

The strongest tokenization strategy starts with the operating model, then picks vendors that fit it.
