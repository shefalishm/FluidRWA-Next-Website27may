A tokenization demo can look finished long before the business is ready. The token appears in a wallet, the investor portal looks polished and the dashboard reports a successful issue. Then an investor changes wallets, a payment arrives with the wrong reference, or the first redemption needs approval from someone outside the platform. That is where the purchasing decision becomes real.

This guide is for the team that has to run those events, not just announce a launch. Start with the shortlist, identify the role you need, and use the operating tests below to turn attractive features into evidence you can compare.

## Why Tokenization Vendor Comparison Is Harder in 2026

Minting a token is usually the easy part. An issuer still needs to establish the investor's rights, control who can hold or transfer the asset, keep records current and process income or redemptions. A platform demo may show issuance without showing how those jobs will be done for your product.

Citi argued in its tokenization research that blockchain adoption is moving toward an inflection point where tokenized financial and real-world assets could become a major use case for mainstream blockchain infrastructure. McKinsey has also framed tokenization as a shift from experimentation toward practical deployments where adoption depends on operational value, regulatory clarity and market infrastructure. The Financial Stability Board has separately warned that tokenization can introduce financial stability considerations when scale, leverage, interconnectedness or liquidity transformation become meaningful.

Those points matter for vendor selection. A vendor that can mint tokens may still be weak at investor onboarding. A platform with strong compliance controls may not solve custody. A product issuer may not be a platform you can use for your own asset. A custody provider may be essential to the project but not responsible for legal structuring, investor eligibility or secondary-market rules.

Before comparing vendors, decide which problem you are solving:

- Are you issuing a fund, security, note, real estate interest, credit asset, commodity product or treasury product?
- Do you need software, a regulated issuer, a transfer agent, a broker-dealer, a marketplace, a custodian or a blockchain network?
- Are investors retail, accredited, professional, institutional, DeFi-native or existing clients?
- Will tokens be transferable, locked, allowlisted, marketplace-traded or redeemable only with the issuer?
- Which jurisdiction controls offering rules, investor eligibility, custody and reporting?

Those questions determine whether a company belongs on your shortlist.

## How to Evaluate Tokenization Companies

Use this framework before choosing any provider.

### 1. Asset Class Fit

Tokenized real estate, private credit, fund interests, tokenized Treasuries, carbon credits, commodities and private company equity have different requirements.

For example:

- A tokenized Treasury product needs fund administration, custody, redemption, yield handling and investor restrictions.
- A real estate tokenization project may need SPV structuring, property cash-flow reporting, investor documents and transfer limitations.
- A private credit product needs borrower data, collateral reporting, servicing workflows and default handling.
- A digital securities product may need transfer agent workflows, broker-dealer support and securities-law compliance.

Do not choose a vendor only because it says "RWA tokenization" on its website. Ask what asset classes it has actually supported.

For example, [**Tokenizer.Estate**](/fluidrwa/tokenizer-estate) is a specialist to evaluate if you own or manage a property and want to launch an offering under your own brand. It provides white-label software for offering pages, token issuance, investor onboarding and payment integrations. The company also describes coordinating with outside legal, banking and payment providers. That coordination is distinct from providing those services itself.

*Disclosure: Tokenizer.Estate is a paying FluidRWA partner.*

### 2. Compliance and Transfer Controls

Institutional tokenization is not only about creating a token. The system must know who can buy, hold, redeem and transfer the asset.

Evaluate:

- KYC, KYB, AML, sanctions and PEP screening
- Investor accreditation, suitability or professional-investor workflows
- Jurisdiction-based restrictions
- Transfer restrictions and allowlists
- Wallet screening and transaction monitoring
- Audit trails and regulator-ready records
- Data privacy and evidence retention

If compliance is still unclear, use FluidRWA's [KYC provider comparison guide](/blog/best-kyc-providers-tokenization-projects-2026/) and [Web3 compliance guide](/blog/web3-blockchain-compliance-guide/) before selecting a platform.

### 3. Custody and Wallet Model

Custody is one of the most important decisions in tokenization. Some projects use qualified custody. Others use MPC wallet infrastructure, embedded wallets, issuer-controlled wallets or investor self-custody with allowlisted addresses.

Evaluate whether the tokenization company integrates with custody providers such as Fireblocks, Taurus, Anchorage Digital, BitGo or other regulated custody and wallet infrastructure providers. For financial institutions, custody and authorization controls may matter as much as token issuance.

### 4. Distribution and Liquidity

Issuing a token does not establish investor demand or a functioning secondary market.

Ask:

- Who will actually buy the asset?
- Can the platform connect issuers with broker-dealers, wealth managers, institutions or marketplaces?
- Is there a secondary market?
- Are transfers legally permitted?
- Are buyers eligible in the target jurisdictions?
- What happens after issuance if there is no liquidity?

Recent research on tokenized RWA markets has emphasized that tokenization and liquidity are separate outcomes. A token can exist on-chain and still trade rarely, remain concentrated among a small number of holders or be restricted by compliance rules.

### 5. Lifecycle Management

A tokenized asset has a life after issuance. It may need distributions, redemptions, reporting, corporate actions, NAV updates, investor communications, tax documents, proof of reserves, governance actions or servicing workflows.

Strong vendors support the full asset lifecycle. Weaker fits may only support initial token creation.

### 6. Interoperability

Institutions do not want trapped assets. They need infrastructure that can work across custodians, wallets, blockchains, reporting systems, fund administrators and market venues.

That is why providers such as Chainlink, Canton Network, Ripple, R3, Hedera and Provenance matter even when they are not traditional tokenization platforms. They support connectivity, settlement, messaging, data or blockchain infrastructure that can make tokenized assets more usable.

## Wider Tokenization Ecosystem: Platforms and Supporting Providers

The ten-platform shortlist above compares issuer-facing starting points. The companies below also include product issuers, networks, custody and connectivity providers. They serve different roles and are not an expanded platform ranking.

### 1. Securitize

Securitize describes issuance, investor onboarding, transfer management and asset servicing for digital securities and funds. Confirm which technology and regulated-service entities would support your offering.

**Best for:** large asset managers, regulated issuers, alternative investment firms and enterprise fund tokenization.

**Strengths:** fund issuance, shareholder records and configured transfer workflows described in its product materials.

**Watchouts:** smaller issuers may find the operating model more enterprise-oriented than they need.

**Useful next step:** compare Securitize with other [tokenization platforms](/vendors/tokenization-platforms/) and confirm whether you need issuer technology, regulated services, transfer agent support or marketplace access.

### 2. Tokeny

Tokeny is a Luxembourg-based provider focused on regulated digital securities and enterprise tokenization infrastructure. It is often evaluated by banks, asset managers and financial institutions that need compliant issuance, transfer restrictions and lifecycle tools.

**Best for:** banks, fund administrators, asset managers and regulated issuers that want modular tokenization infrastructure.

**Strengths:** permissioned token controls, issuer servicing and investor applications; jurisdiction fit depends on the offering and operating partners.

**Watchouts:** buyers should evaluate distribution options and the broader operating stack around the software.

**Useful next step:** read the FluidRWA comparison of [Tokeny vs Securitize](/blog/tokeny-vs-securitize/) for a deeper decision framework.

### 3. Zoniqx

Zoniqx positions itself as an end-to-end tokenization and institutional digital asset infrastructure platform. Its model focuses on issuance, identity, compliance, lifecycle management and institutional distribution through a more complete operating environment.

**Best for:** asset managers, banks, private equity firms, real estate issuers, fund managers and institutional issuers that need lifecycle and distribution support.

**Strengths:** broad asset class support, integrated identity and compliance workflows, lifecycle management and institutional distribution focus.

**Watchouts:** enterprise deployments usually require planning, implementation work and clearer operating requirements.

**Useful next step:** run the [Tokenization Readiness Assessment](/tokenization-readiness-assessment-tool) before comparing Zoniqx with other enterprise platforms, because fit depends heavily on asset class and launch model.

### 4. Ondo Finance

Ondo Finance is one of the most visible companies in tokenized U.S. Treasuries and institutional yield products. Its strength is not generalized tokenization software for issuers; it is productization of familiar financial exposures for eligible investors.

**Best for:** institutional investors, treasury managers, digital asset funds and platforms seeking tokenized Treasury exposure.

**Strengths:** clear product positioning, strong market awareness, tokenized Treasury focus and distribution momentum.

**Watchouts:** Ondo is more relevant as a tokenized product provider than as a general-purpose tokenization platform for arbitrary assets.

### 5. Brickken

Brickken describes issuance, investor management and lifecycle tools for asset issuers. Compare the implementation and operating scope against your project rather than assuming it is cheaper or simpler than an institutional deployment.

**Best for:** SMEs, private companies, real estate sponsors and issuers exploring digital securities or asset tokenization.

**Strengths:** issuer tools, KYC/KYB workflow configuration and lifecycle management described on its product site.

**Watchouts:** buyers should validate jurisdictional compliance, transfer rules, custody and distribution partners for their specific asset.

### 6. Centrifuge

Centrifuge is a major name in tokenized private credit and asset-backed financing. It connects real-world credit assets with on-chain capital markets and has long been associated with the RWA lending category.

**Best for:** credit funds, asset originators, trade finance providers and institutional lending workflows.

**Strengths:** private credit specialization, early category leadership and on-chain credit infrastructure.

**Watchouts:** it is more specialized than a general issuance platform.

### 7. Superstate

Superstate focuses on tokenized funds and investment products that bring regulated asset management workflows on-chain. It is relevant for institutions evaluating tokenized fund access and blockchain-native fund administration models.

**Best for:** institutional investors and digital asset allocators seeking regulated tokenized funds.

**Strengths:** asset management focus, tokenized fund positioning and institutional product design.

**Watchouts:** it should be compared as a product issuer and fund platform, not simply as issuer software.

### 8. Hashnote

For the USYC route associated with Hashnote, consult Circle's USYC product information. It describes Circle International Bermuda Limited as token administrator for the Cayman Islands fund and restricts access to non-US persons, with additional eligibility conditions. This is a fund-access decision, not a purchase of general-purpose issuance software. Redemption capacity and fund terms must be confirmed; no settlement or return is guaranteed here.

**Best for:** institutional treasury, cash management and yield-oriented tokenized products.

**Strengths:** focused product strategy and institutional Treasury relevance.

**Watchouts:** product availability and investor eligibility depend on structure and jurisdiction.

### 9. Fireblocks

Fireblocks is not a tokenization platform in the narrow sense. It is a major custody, wallet and digital asset operations infrastructure provider used by institutions that need secure transaction authorization and wallet operations.

**Best for:** banks, fintechs, exchanges, payment companies and institutions that need secure wallet and custody operations.

**Strengths:** MPC wallet infrastructure, policy controls, operational security and institutional integrations.

**Watchouts:** Fireblocks is usually part of the tokenization stack, not the entire stack.

### 10. Taurus

Taurus provides digital asset infrastructure for banks and financial institutions, including custody and tokenization-related capabilities. It is especially relevant where bank-grade infrastructure and regulated financial operations are central.

**Best for:** banks, broker-dealers, financial institutions and regulated infrastructure buyers.

**Strengths:** institutional custody, bank focus and digital asset infrastructure depth.

**Watchouts:** fit depends on whether the buyer needs custody, issuance, marketplace connectivity or all three.

### 11. Chainlink

Chainlink provides oracle and cross-chain infrastructure that can support tokenized assets through data, proof-of-reserve, interoperability and connectivity layers.

**Best for:** projects that need asset data, reserve verification, cross-chain messaging or connectivity between tokenized assets and external systems.

**Strengths:** oracle infrastructure, proof-of-reserve tooling and broad ecosystem connectivity.

**Watchouts:** Chainlink is an infrastructure layer, not an issuer or full tokenization platform.

### 12. Canton Network

Canton Network is designed for institutional financial markets that need privacy, synchronization and interoperability across regulated participants.

**Best for:** banks, market infrastructure providers and institutional networks.

**Strengths:** financial-market design, privacy-aware workflows and institutional interoperability.

**Watchouts:** it is more relevant to institutional market infrastructure than smaller issuer launches.

### 13. Ripple

Ripple is relevant for institutional payments, settlement and tokenization infrastructure, particularly where cross-border value movement and financial institution connectivity are important.

**Best for:** banks, payment companies and institutions evaluating tokenized settlement or asset mobility.

**Strengths:** financial institution relationships, payments infrastructure and enterprise blockchain positioning.

**Watchouts:** tokenization use cases should be evaluated separately from payments and settlement use cases.

### 14. R3

R3 and Corda remain relevant for enterprise blockchain and regulated financial workflows. R3 is often considered where privacy, permissioned networks and financial institution integration matter.

**Best for:** banks, capital markets infrastructure, trade finance and regulated enterprise workflows.

**Watchouts:** buyers should compare whether a permissioned model or public-chain model better fits the use case.

### 15. Hedera

Hedera is an enterprise-grade public network used for digital assets, identity, provenance and institutional applications. It can be relevant for tokenization projects that want predictable fees, governance and enterprise ecosystem support.

**Best for:** enterprises, financial institutions and projects needing scalable public-network infrastructure.

**Strengths:** enterprise governance, performance and practical tokenization tooling.

**Watchouts:** the network choice still needs to match custody, compliance and distribution requirements.

### 16. Provenance Blockchain

Provenance Blockchain is associated with financial asset workflows, lending, private markets and institutional financial services infrastructure.

**Best for:** lending institutions, private market workflows and financial asset settlement.

**Strengths:** financial-services focus and asset-backed transaction relevance.

**Watchouts:** buyers should validate ecosystem depth and integrations for their specific asset type.

### 17. Figure

Figure belongs in a lending or private-credit shortlist when the project involves loan origination, trading or related financial-asset infrastructure. Buyers should confirm which part of that workflow Figure would actually provide.

**Best for:** financial institutions, lenders and credit platforms.

**Strengths:** credit-market experience and institutional financial workflow focus.

**Watchouts:** it is not a generic tokenization platform for every asset class.

### 18. Maple Finance

Maple Finance is a major on-chain credit marketplace that supports institutional lending and credit strategies. It is relevant in tokenized private credit comparisons because credit is one of the most important RWA categories.

**Best for:** institutional credit, digital asset lenders and on-chain private credit strategies.

**Strengths:** credit specialization and lending-market infrastructure.

**Watchouts:** credit risk, borrower quality, liquidity and investor eligibility need close diligence.

### 19. DigiShares

DigiShares is focused on real estate tokenization and investment management workflows. It is often relevant for property sponsors, real estate funds and developers exploring fractionalized real estate investment products.

**Best for:** real estate developers, property investment platforms and real estate fund sponsors.

**Strengths:** real estate specialization, investor management workflows and asset-specific tooling.

**Watchouts:** real estate tokenization requires local legal structuring, investor documentation and realistic liquidity planning.

### 20. ADDX

ADDX is a private markets platform focused on access to alternative investments for eligible investors. It is relevant where tokenization intersects with regulated private market distribution.

**Best for:** accredited investors, private market products and alternative investment access.

**Strengths:** regulated private markets positioning and investor access model.

**Watchouts:** issuers should evaluate market access, eligibility and jurisdictional constraints.

### 21. Polymath

Polymath's Capital Platform describes white-label token issuance, investor portals, corporate actions and configured restrictions on Polymesh. Confirm the current deployment and operating partners rather than treating historical security-token activity as evidence of present product fit.

**Best for:** regulated issuers and security-token workflows.

**Strengths:** security-token focus and category history.

**Watchouts:** buyers should evaluate current product maturity, ecosystem activity and implementation fit.

### 22. KAIO (formerly Libre)

KAIO, previously known as Libre, presents tokenized access to institutional funds and alternative investments. Ask which fund, investor type and jurisdiction are supported for the proposed route; the current site notes distribution restrictions.

**Best for:** wealth managers, private banks and alternative investment platforms.

**Strengths:** alternative investment focus and distribution-oriented model.

**Watchouts:** product availability and investor eligibility are central diligence items.

### 23. Backed Finance

Backed Finance offers tokenized securities linked to traditional financial assets. It is relevant for investors and platforms looking at regulated tokenized exposure to familiar instruments.

**Best for:** eligible investors, DeFi integrations and tokenized listed-asset exposure.

**Watchouts:** geographic availability and investor restrictions matter.

Archax is a regulated digital asset exchange, broker and custodian based in the UK. It is relevant for institutional trading, custody and tokenized asset market infrastructure.

**Best for:** institutions that need regulated trading, custody or market access.

### 25. Midas

Midas offers tokenized investment products and is relevant for buyers evaluating regulated, blockchain-based investment exposure, particularly in Europe.

## Best Tokenization Companies by Use Case

## Common Mistakes When Choosing a Tokenization Provider

### Mistake 1: Treating tokenization as minting

Minting is only one step. The real work includes rights, compliance, custody, onboarding, transfers, servicing and investor trust.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring distribution

An asset can be tokenized and still fail commercially if the issuer has no qualified buyer channel.

### Mistake 3: Buying a platform before confirming legal structure

Technology cannot fix an unclear legal claim, weak investor documentation or unresolved jurisdictional issues.

### Mistake 4: Assuming liquidity is automatic

Tokenization may improve transferability, but liquidity depends on market access, eligible buyers, pricing, disclosure, trading venues and compliance rules.

### Mistake 5: Choosing one vendor for every layer

Some projects need one integrated partner. Others need a modular stack. The right answer depends on the asset and operating model.

## Before you shortlist: issuance software, fund access or a market?

Two companies can both advertise tokenization while selling different things. One supplies software to an issuer; another admits investors to a managed fund; another provides a regulated service or market connection. Comparing them as interchangeable "best platforms" can send a project to the wrong sales team.

Start with the role that is missing in your own operating model:

| Your immediate job | Category to evaluate | Evidence to bring to the first meeting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issue and service your own asset | Issuer software plus appointed operating partners | Asset structure, administrator, investor population and lifecycle events |
| Invest in an existing tokenized fund | Eligible product access and distribution | Investor status, subscription terms, redemption requirements and geography |
| Launch tokenized private-company shares | Register, issuance and permitted transfer workflow | Share class, authoritative register, consent rules and venue requirements |
| Add a secondary market | Market operator, brokerage and settlement arrangement | Actual instrument admission and transfer restrictions, not a token standard alone |

For issuer software, Tokeny's platform documentation, revisited November 19, 2026 for this distinction, describes issuance and lifecycle tooling. That product description does not appoint every legal, identity, custody or distribution role for a new offering. The other entries retain their existing source review dates and scope.

### Run a lifecycle demonstration instead of another minting demo

Use one fictional investor and asset. Demonstrate onboarding, subscription, issuance, a rejected transfer, an eligibility change, a distribution, a corrected record and redemption. For each step, identify the operator, authoritative record and escalation route.

Request pricing on that complete workload. Separate software subscription, implementation, per-investor or transaction charges, chain costs and third-party services. Confirm whether jurisdiction-specific legal work, investor verification, custody, administration and distribution are in the quote or separate purchases.

The answer may be a combination of providers rather than one "end-to-end" vendor. What matters is that the seams between their responsibilities are explicit and testable.

## A practical platform evaluation: from feature list to working evidence

Imagine a property fund with an existing administrator, investors in several countries and a plan to accept both bank payments and stablecoins. A white-label portal may be useful, but it does not answer who approves an investor, when an order becomes binding, which register determines ownership or who can authorize a redemption. Use that same fictional offering in every demo. The comparisons become clearer when providers have to solve the same sequence rather than choose their easiest screen to show.

The following are proposed buyer acceptance tests, not claims that every shortlisted platform supports every function. Agree the required outcome, responsible operator and retained evidence before deciding whether a feature is included.

### 1. Choose the deployment model before judging the interface

**Hosted software** can reduce the work of assembling a portal, but ask who owns the account, domain, investor relationship and production configuration. **White-label deployment** should mean more than changing a logo: check document templates, language support, accessibility, investor communications, permitted customization and responsibility for maintaining those changes. **API-led integration** puts more of the workflow in your own application, so your engineers need versioning, authentication, error handling and change notices. **Custom development** can address unusual requirements while adding responsibility for testing, maintenance and handover.

Ask whether the demonstration uses the same product tier, permissions and integrations proposed for production. A sales sandbox can be informative without establishing live payment access, current investor eligibility or availability in your jurisdiction. Have the supplier mark each required function as available now, requiring configuration, dependent on a separate partner, or planned. Keep roadmap items out of the launch acceptance criteria unless you deliberately accept that dependency.

For an API evaluation, use a sequence with a repeated request, a delayed notification and an unavailable downstream system. Check whether an order can be duplicated, how status changes are reconciled and whether your team can retrieve earlier records after pagination. Brickken's current API documentation provides one concrete architectural example: it prepares unsigned transactions for review and signing by the customer's application. That makes transaction inspection and signer control relevant tests; it does not mean every integration or complete data export is included.

### 2. Trace investor onboarding through to a permitted holding

Follow a person and a company investor separately. Determine who collects identity documents, verifies beneficial owners, checks eligibility, approves exceptions and retains the supporting evidence. Ask which party is the data controller for each dataset, where information is processed and how a correction reaches all relevant systems. The investor should not have to guess whether a rejected application belongs with the issuer, the platform or a separate verification provider.

Then change something. Let a document expire, revoke a permission, replace a wallet and change the investor's contact details. Check whether the portal, identity service, ownership register and token controls remain consistent. A working onboarding form is not evidence that ongoing eligibility changes work. Also distinguish the ability to view an offering from permission to subscribe, receive tokens, transfer them or redeem.

The legal question comes before the compliance toggle. ESMA's MiCA Article 2 scope text excludes crypto-assets that qualify as financial instruments. A general claim that a platform is MiCA-ready therefore does not establish the regime for a tokenized share or fund interest. Ask qualified counsel to classify the actual instrument and identify the entities and activities involved. Do not substitute a jurisdiction list, headquarters address or token standard for that assessment. [Read the primary scope text](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-2-scope).

### 3. Separate payment support, settlement and redemption

A provider may display bank transfer, card and stablecoin options while different partners perform the underlying services. For each proposed route, record the receiving entity, account or wallet, supported currency and network, order reference and condition for releasing tokens. Define what happens with an incorrect amount, an unsupported asset, a late payment, a duplicate notification or a failed investor approval. Confirm who communicates with the customer and who can return funds.

Do not infer delivery-versus-payment from the presence of a payment button. Ask the team to identify the finality point for the money leg and the asset leg and explain how one-sided delivery is prevented or resolved. A token transfer, a bank settlement and an investor's legal entitlement can have different completion conditions. The [October 2024 BIS/CPMI tokenisation report](https://www.bis.org/publications/tokenisation-context-money-and-other-assets-concepts-and-implications-central-banks.pdf) distinguishes linked settlement from atomic execution; the practical buyer question is what your proposed arrangement actually guarantees and what remains a counterparty or operational dependency.

Redemption deserves its own demonstration. Submit a request outside the dealing window, follow it while pending, apply the approved valuation, and trace payment and token cancellation through the records. Ask about partial fulfillment, suspensions, rejected requests, cancellation rights and the handling of a failed payout. Do not promise instant exits because a blockchain operates continuously. The underlying asset, fund terms, banking arrangements and authorized operator may not.

### 4. Test the operating record, not only the dashboard

Ask which record is authoritative for investor ownership and who is allowed to correct it. Compare that record with token supply, investor balances, accepted subscriptions and outstanding redemption requests at one specified time or block. Document the treatment of treasury holdings, frozen balances and pending transactions so the totals are intelligible. A difference should produce an assigned exception, not disappear into a spreadsheet that no one owns.

Next, run an income distribution and a corporate action. Identify the record date, entitlement calculation, withholding inputs, approval, payment result and correction process. If the platform supports governance or voting, check how eligibility and voting power are determined and how the result is retained. If electronic signatures are needed, confirm document version, signer authority, evidence retention and the way amended terms are communicated. A checkbox labelled governance or digital signatures does not describe a complete process.

DigiShares' current capabilities describe investor records, distributions and fund dealing, including administrator-approved NAV. Its stated software role remains distinct from payment processing and investment intermediation. This is a useful illustration of the wider procurement rule: a platform can coordinate a workflow without being the entity that legally performs every service. Put the issuer, administrator, custodian, payment provider, market operator and technology supplier into a written responsibility matrix.

### 5. Inspect token standards and administrative powers separately

Request the actual deployed interface, implementation version and configuration rather than accepting a list of standards in a presentation. ERC-3643 specifies identity and compliance components for permissioned tokens. Its privileged operations are important: ordinary transfers and forced transfers do not follow identical checks. Test who can mint, burn, freeze, recover, upgrade or move balances, what approvals apply and how the action is recorded. Standard compatibility is not an assurance that all privileged actions are constrained in the way your policy requires. [ERC-3643 specification](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-3643).

ERC-1400 is an umbrella proposal associated with partitions, transfer checks, document references and controller operations. Ask which of its component interfaces the implementation actually uses; a broad compatibility label is not a substitute for an interoperability test. ERC-4626 instead addresses tokenized vault shares against a single underlying ERC-20 asset. It does not establish title to offchain property, independent NAV verification or available redemption liquidity. These are different design tools, not competing regulatory licenses.

For asynchronous vaults, ERC-7540 separates a request from the later claim. Ask how pending, claimable and claimed amounts appear to the investor and administrator. The specification leaves cancellation outside its scope, so a promised cancellation function needs its own explanation and tests. Operators can receive substantial authority; inspect approval and revocation rather than assuming that a vault is permissionless or instantly redeemable. [ERC-7540 specification](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7540).

### 6. Make the chain and wallet decision operational

Choose networks against custody support, investor access, settlement dependencies, data requirements and the team's ability to operate them. A platform advertising several chains does not establish that the same asset, permissions, payment route or custodian works on every chain. Request a product-specific compatibility record and a deployment reference for the combination you intend to use. Distinguish a live implementation from an integration announcement or a pilot.

A wallet being able to display a token does not establish that its user may purchase, receive, transfer or redeem that instrument. Test account creation, signing, gas funding, recovery and support with the intended investor population. Self-custody, embedded wallets, enterprise key infrastructure and asset custody divide responsibilities differently. Ask who controls keys and who safeguards the underlying asset; they may be entirely separate parties.

For a multichain model, decide which supply record is authoritative, how minting is coordinated and what happens when a bridge or messaging dependency stops. Do not count copies or wrapped positions as new underlying assets without understanding the accounting. Network Explorer can help map disclosed product and infrastructure relationships, but a named relationship is not proof that a new issuer will receive the same integration or commercial access.

### 7. Require an exit and continuity exercise before signing

Request a machine-readable export of investor identifiers, wallet mappings, eligibility history, ownership movements, signed document references, distributions and administrative events. Keep sensitive personal information under agreed access controls. Reconcile the export against the designated register and chain snapshot, then ask your own team to restore a sample into an independent system. An API that returns current balances is not necessarily a complete migration path.

Separate software access from production authority. Identify who holds upgrade and administration permissions, how keys are rotated, how emergency action is approved and which supplier accounts remain after handover. Confirm source and license access, hosting obligations, backup restoration, support escalation and notice of discontinued integrations. A non-custodial label does not answer all these questions, particularly if a vendor still controls an application, identity registry or privileged function.

Use three release gates: the legal and operating structure is approved by accountable professionals; the lifecycle pilot produces accepted evidence; and the continuity exercise succeeds without depending on one supplier employee. If a gate fails, record the missing artifact and owner. That gives the buyer a concrete reason to proceed, narrow the scope or stop, rather than a vague impression from another polished demo.

## Read market scale without comparing unlike numbers

Market dashboards and platform announcements can describe different things: distributed token value, assets represented on a ledger, fund assets under management, loan balances, cumulative issuance or transaction volume over a period. These figures are not interchangeable. A large represented asset balance does not mean that the same amount is freely available to onchain investors; cumulative issuance does not describe the current outstanding supply.

For any scale comparison, retain the metric definition, date, product perimeter, valuation method, stablecoin treatment and handling of bridged or wrapped positions. Do not add the value of an underlying fund and its tokenized access vehicle as if they were independent assets. Likewise, wallet addresses are not necessarily unique investors, and transfer activity is not necessarily economic trading.

RWA.xyz's platform view usefully distinguishes distributed assets from represented assets and stablecoins. Use those distinctions when reading market research, not as a provider quality score. This guide deliberately does not turn live dashboard balances into a universal ranking. A buyer needs evidence that a provider can support the proposed instrument and workload, not the largest number assembled from incompatible definitions.

## A purchase-ready platform scorecard

A founder can leave a tokenization demo impressed and still not know who will answer an investor's redemption request. Resolve that uncertainty before scoring features. Start with a single offering, a named operating team and the records that must agree when money and ownership move.

The following is a buyer evaluation framework, not a scored benchmark or a claim that every shortlisted platform supplies every function.

| Criterion | Evidence the buyer should obtain | Acceptance exercise |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership record | Offering documents and appointed register owner | Reconcile an issued interest to the investor record |
| Investor eligibility | Product-specific rules and accountable reviewer | Reject an ineligible applicant without accepting funds |
| Issuance authority | Defined administrator and signing permissions | Require independent approval for minting |
| Funding | Account, payment reference and allocation rules | Hold an unmatched payment outside the ownership register |
| Transfer restrictions | Configured rules and exception authority | Attempt a transfer to an unapproved investor |
| Servicing | Distribution, voting and corporate-action records | Reproduce one investor statement from source events |
| Redemption | Cutoffs, liquidity owner and cancellation evidence | Reconcile interest cancellation to final payment |
| Integrations | Versioned API documentation and support ownership | Recover from a missed event without duplicating an action |
| Security | Service-specific assessment and recovery procedure | Remove an administrator and test continuity |
| Exit | Export format, contractual assistance and retained records | Reconstruct holdings without the incumbent dashboard |

### Match the software role before choosing a company

Tokeny's platform documentation describes issuer servicing and investor applications. DigiShares describes configurable tokenization software. Zoniqx distinguishes z360 from zConnect. Those product descriptions help identify the questions to ask; they do not assign your offering's legal responsibilities or promise investor demand.

For a property sponsor, start with a branded investor journey and ongoing distributions. For a private company, start with ownership changes and corporate actions. For a fund manager, begin with the administrator and transfer-agent model. Do not use the same weighted scorecard for all three.

### Ten questions to take into the selection meeting

1. Which legal entity contracts for each service?
2. Who maintains the authoritative ownership record?
3. Who approves investor eligibility for this offering?
4. What happens to money received before approval?
5. Which actions need independent authorization?
6. How are blocked transfers investigated and released?
7. Who reconciles distributions, fees and redemptions?
8. What history can we export, including corrections?
9. Which implementation and third-party charges are outside the quote?
10. Can we complete an orderly migration without losing investor access?
