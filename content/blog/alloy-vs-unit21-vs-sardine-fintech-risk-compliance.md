Fintech and Web3 risk teams do not buy "compliance software" in the abstract. They buy specific capabilities: approve a good user faster, block a bad actor before money moves, screen payments, monitor suspicious transactions, manage cases, produce audit evidence and satisfy internal policy.

Alloy, Unit21 and Sardine all operate in the risk and compliance stack, but they solve different parts of the workflow.

## Short Answer

Start with Alloy if the main job is connecting identity data sources and onboarding decisions. Confirm the data sources and policy controls available in your proposed setup.

Look at Unit21 if the team spends most of its time monitoring transactions, reviewing alerts and managing investigations.

Consider Sardine where payment fraud and crypto funding are part of the same workflow. Check the responsibilities its on-ramp product actually assumes in each market.

## Quick Comparison

| Documented workflow | Alloy | Unit21 | Sardine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product focus | Identity and risk orchestration | AML monitoring and case operations | Fraud prevention and AML |
| Starting workflow | Data sources and decision policies | Rules, alerts and investigations | Risk signals and monitoring |
| Confirm before shortlisting | Data coverage and decision ownership | Alert tuning and filing scope | Product scope and payment liability |

These observations come from the linked company documentation, checked 10 October 2026. They are not feature-completeness scores. Unlisted capabilities have not been assessed, and buyer questions are FluidRWA's procurement recommendations.

## Test who owns each decision

For a vendor pilot, replay one synthetic customer journey: a failed identity check, a retried payment and a later alert that needs manual review. Ask each provider to show which decision it owns, which events it hands off, and how a reviewer can reproduce the decision after a rule changes. If your product includes fiat funding, the [ramp onboarding evidence map](/network-explorer/research/ramp-onboarding) helps distinguish provider-hosted checks from checks your team must run.

## What Alloy Is Good For

Alloy's official materials describe identity orchestration and fraud-risk decisioning, including data-source connections and decision workflows. Confirm the required data providers, markets, policy controls and operating responsibilities in the proposed product. This comparison does not independently benchmark speed or coverage.

Alloy is especially useful for:

- Fintech onboarding
- KYC orchestration
- Identity data-source management
- Fraud and compliance checks at signup
- Teams that want to swap or combine vendors without rebuilding workflows
- Risk teams that want policy changes without engineering queues

Alloy is worth testing at the first decision: should this customer, merchant or business be approved, denied or reviewed, and who owns that decision?

## Where Alloy May Not Be Ideal

Alloy is not primarily a blockchain analytics tool or a crypto transaction monitoring platform. Web3 businesses may still need on-chain analytics, wallet screening and KYT tooling in addition to Alloy.

## What Unit21 Is Good For

Unit21 is built for fraud and AML operations. Its public materials describe real-time monitoring, AML transaction monitoring, sanctions screening, case management, regulatory filing, customer risk rating, payment screening, device intelligence and AI agents for detection and investigation. Its transaction monitoring page emphasizes ingesting any data type, configurable rules, backtesting, shadow mode, graph-based rules and SAR filing.

Unit21 is especially useful for:

- AML transaction monitoring
- Fraud operations
- Case management
- Regulatory filing workflows
- Payment screening
- Customer risk ratings
- Teams that want fraud and AML in one operating layer

Unit21 is worth testing after onboarding, when the team has transactions to monitor and alerts to investigate. Ask how a case moves from signal to decision and evidence.

## Where Unit21 May Not Be Ideal

Unit21 is not the first tool most teams would choose for pure identity orchestration at signup. It can ingest entity data and support risk workflows, but Alloy is more directly focused on onboarding decision orchestration.

## What Sardine Is Good For

Sardine is strong where payments, fraud and crypto overlap. Its on-ramp documentation describes a Merchant of Record on-ramp for consumer and enterprise crypto purchases, with built-in risk and compliance. It says Sardine handles merchant of record, KYC, compliance, payments and fraud liability, and supports consumer wallets, fintechs, stablecoin issuers and institutions for funding treasuries and payout flows.

Sardine is especially useful for:

- Fiat-to-crypto on-ramps
- Wallets that need higher approval rates and lower fraud
- Stablecoin funding workflows
- Fintechs moving money into crypto products
- Payment-risk teams that need fraud and compliance together

Sardine's payment-risk focus may matter when teams need to control fraud without turning away legitimate users. Test approval and fraud outcomes on your own traffic; neither can be inferred from product materials.

## Where Sardine May Not Be Ideal

Sardine may not replace a full AML transaction monitoring and case management platform. If the buyer needs SAR workflows, transaction monitoring programs and broad lifecycle compliance operations, Unit21 may be the better primary platform.

## How to Choose

Use Alloy at the front door.

Use Unit21 across the lifecycle.

Use Sardine when the front door involves payment risk, crypto purchases or stablecoin funding.

Most mature fintech and Web3 stacks will combine these categories rather than pick only one vendor.

## A 2026 risk-platform comparison should include the case your policy gets wrong

A clean onboarding demo is the easiest case. The revealing case is a legitimate customer rejected by a rule, or an accepted customer whose later activity needs investigation. Give Alloy, Unit21 and Sardine the same permissioned examples and ask how the proposed configuration reaches and revises its decisions.

The [Alloy](https://www.alloy.com/), [Unit21](https://www.unit21.ai/) and [Sardine](https://www.sardine.ai/) product sites, revisited November 19, 2026, describe overlapping identity, fraud and financial-crime workflows. Overlap makes exact scope more important: a broad platform description does not establish that all data sources, monitoring features or investigation tools are included in one proposal.

| Case | What to examine | Outcome to measure separately |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Similar-name screening result | Source evidence and analyst resolution | False-positive effort |
| Returning customer with changed behavior | Event history, rules and escalation | Relevant cases surfaced for review |
| Changed business ownership | Updated entity evidence and decision record | Completeness of the recheck |
| Provider or data-source outage | Explicit unavailable state and approved fallback | Cases incorrectly accepted or unnecessarily blocked |
| Reversed analyst decision | Attribution, reason and downstream update | Reproducibility of the changed outcome |

Agree the expected treatment with your compliance owners before the demo. Do not use a vendor's demonstration approval rate as a universal measure of accuracy.

### Review AI assistance as a separate control

Where the proposal includes an AI investigator or case summary, ask the reviewer to open the exact evidence behind each material statement. Introduce a contradictory record and a missing record. The summary should not fill either gap with a plausible story.

Confirm who may close a case, change a rule or override an escalation. Request the rule/model version, decision history, access permissions and retention settings. A fluent explanation is not proof that the decision is correct.

### Procurement questions that change the operating cost

Price the same customer population, verification mix, transaction events, screening frequency, analyst seats and retention period. Identify separately billed datasets or partners. Include review effort and correction work, rather than comparing only a per-check charge.

Ask for data-processing terms and the contracting entity for the proposed jurisdictions. Assign ownership for complaints, adverse decisions, incident escalation and vendor exit. The organization using the system still needs an approved policy and responsible decision makers.
