## Short answer

The EU DLT Pilot Regime is [Regulation (EU) 2022/858](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-laws/eu-dlt-pilot-regime-regulation-2022-858/). It lets authorised firms run trading and settlement infrastructure on distributed ledger technology, with specific permissions and some exemptions from existing market rules. It has applied since 23 March 2023.

## What it allows

The regulation recognises three types of DLT market infrastructure:

- **DLT multilateral trading facility (DLT MTF):** authorised investment firms and market operators can apply to run one.
- **DLT settlement system (DLT SS):** authorised central securities depositories can apply to run one.
- **DLT trading and settlement system (DLT TSS):** a combined model that both groups can apply for.

Each operator needs a specific permission from its national competent authority.

## What it limits

The regime covers only eligible shares, bonds, money-market instruments and UCITS, and it applies thresholds to the value of instruments admitted to trading or recorded on a DLT market infrastructure. Check the thresholds in the Regulation text before you plan around them. This is why many tokenized real-world assets sit outside the regime and need a different legal route.

## Why it is under review

ESMA published its [review report on 25 June 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/07/esma-suggests-amendments-to-the-dlt-pilot-regime-to-make-it-permanent-and-attractive). It found limited uptake, with only three authorised infrastructures, and recommended making the regime permanent, making thresholds flexible and extending eligible assets.

On 4 December 2025 the European Commission proposed amendments as part of its Market Integration and Supervision Package. According to a [Council presidency discussion paper](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-2365-2026-REV-1/en/pdf), the proposal would remove product-specific thresholds and significantly raise the aggregate market value thresholds. Those sources describe a proposal under negotiation, so confirm the current status before relying on any change.

## What an issuer should do

If you are tokenizing a security for EU investors, ask counsel which framework applies to your instrument and your venue: the DLT Pilot Regime, MiCA, national securities law, or a combination. Then shortlist advisors who have worked on comparable structures.
