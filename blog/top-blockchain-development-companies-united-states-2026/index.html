What makes these firms different?

LeewayHertz: enterprise application scope

LeewayHertz's published case study identifies a San Francisco headquarters and describes blockchain-platform development. That makes it a candidate when a buyer needs an architecture discussion spanning on-chain logic and enterprise systems. Ask for a current, relevant reference and identify which components the proposed team will build versus integrate. The case study alone does not establish suitability for a regulated securities workflow.

PixelPlex: Web3 product and tokenization scope

PixelPlex describes New York delivery work involving smart contracts, Web3 applications and tokenization. A buyer should distinguish a demonstration or proof of concept from a production product with monitoring, account recovery and upgrade controls. Request named technical leads, code samples where disclosure permits, and a production-support plan.

Intellectsoft: financial-software integration scope

Intellectsoft states a New York headquarters and describes smart contracts and blockchain ledgers within a broader financial-software practice. This may suit a project where the ledger is one component of a larger application. The buyer should ask which engineers write the contract code, where that work is delivered, and how security review is separated from implementation.

What should a US buyer put in the RFP?

  1. Define the system of record. Specify which rights and balances exist on-chain and which stay in internal databases or custodial systems.
  2. Map regulated steps. For tokenization, show who owns investor eligibility, identity checks, transfer restrictions and redemptions. Engineering claims are not legal opinions.
  3. Demand a security boundary diagram. Identify signing keys, admin roles, upgrade authority, incident response and independent review.
  4. Specify proof, not adjectives. Ask for a relevant production reference, architecture excerpt, test strategy and post-launch service levels.
  5. Clarify the US relationship. Confirm contracting entity, engineering locations, subcontractors, data access and time-zone coverage.
  6. Protect the handoff. Set repository ownership, documentation, deployment credentials, acceptance tests and exit assistance in the contract.

FAQ

Which US blockchain development company is best for an RWA project?

There is no universal best. Ask each candidate to demonstrate the exact tokenization, custody, transfer-control and investor-onboarding work your project requires, and verify those claims independently.

Does a US office mean the development team is in the United States?

No. Confirm the contracting entity, engineering locations, time-zone coverage and subcontracting arrangements in the proposal.

What should a blockchain development RFP request?

Request architecture, deliverables, repository ownership, audit scope, key-management responsibilities, testing evidence, incident support and a milestone-based commercial proposal.

Are these companies ranked by revenue or project quality?

No. This is a non-ranked editorial sample based on visible location and service documentation, checked October 16, 2026.
