Best Fund Tokenization Platforms for Asset Managers in 2026

Compare fund tokenization platforms by regulated roles, investor onboarding, transfer controls, administration, custody, distribution and redemptions.

Short answer

Shortlist fund tokenization platforms by the operating roles you need, not a feature count. Compare Securitize, Tokeny, Zoniqx, Taurus and KAIO against the proposed fund structure. Identify who owns investor eligibility, the register, administration, custody, distribution and redemptions before asking for a complete implementation proposal.

Where Each Platform May Fit

Securitize

Securitize presents tokenization alongside fund-administration and investor-service offerings. Its Fund Services page describes NAV, accounting, investor onboarding, capital calls, distributions and reporting. It is a starting point for managers considering coordinated services, not only issuer software. Identify each appointed entity and agreement; an available service is not proof that it is included for your fund.

Tokeny

Tokeny centers its infrastructure on ERC-3643 and identity-linked permissioned transfers. Its platform documentation separates the issuer Servicing App from the Investor App, including lifecycle actions and investor holdings/NAV views. It may fit managers building modular EVM-based infrastructure with appointed operators. Confirm who operates identity registries, rules, recovery, servicing and investor support.

Zoniqx

Zoniqx separates z360 issuer orchestration and lifecycle operations from zConnect access and distribution workflows. Its z360 materials describe eligibility, subscriptions, records, payouts and redemptions. Its website identifies Zoniqx Inc. as a technology provider rather than a broker-dealer, investment adviser or transfer agent. Confirm appointed intermediaries, actual deployment evidence and available modules for the proposed fund.

Taurus

Taurus-CAPITAL describes issuance and servicing integrated with Taurus-PROTECT custody technology. Its developer documentation distinguishes EVM, Solana and Hedera integration paths rather than one universal token interface. It is relevant to institutions prioritizing contract governance and custody integration. Confirm deployed versions, signing controls, fund-administrator connections and which custody responsibilities belong to the institution or a separate provider.

KAIO (formerly Libre)

KAIO (formerly Libre) presents infrastructure for institutional fund access and lists products with different liquidity schedules and eligibility restrictions. It is relevant to managers considering fund-access and distribution arrangements. Distinguish direct fund interests from feeder or access structures, then confirm issuer, administrator, custody, transfer restrictions and redemption mechanics for the specific offering. A listed underlying manager is not evidence that every token holder invests directly in that manager's fund.

Proof-of-Concept Tests

  1. Onboard one eligible and one ineligible investor.
  2. Process a subscription through final reconciled ownership records.
  3. Apply a lockup and reject an unauthorized transfer.
  4. Update NAV and generate investor reporting.
  5. Process a distribution with withholding and failed-payment handling.
  6. Complete a redemption and reconcile token, cash and administrator records.
  7. Recover an investor wallet without weakening identity controls.
  8. Export the complete register, documents, rules and audit history.

Make redemption the deciding exercise

Use one fictional investor whose wallet balance, register holding and pending redemption can be traced together. Ask the team to show cutoffs, applicable fees, approval, token treatment, cash payment and the final register update.

Then delay the cash leg and change the investor's receiving account under the approved verification procedure. The system should distinguish a request, an approved obligation and a completed payment. Ask who investigates each discrepancy and who communicates with the investor.

Agree a complete launch and operating scope

Separate platform access, implementation, chain operations, administration, transfer agency, custody, onboarding, distribution and reporting responsibilities. Confirm which services are supplied by the platform group and which require separate contracts and approvals.

For jurisdiction, define the fund domicile, manager, target investors, distribution locations and instrument. A platform available in a country is not an authorization for your fund or offering.

The tokenization company shortlist helps separate software, access and market roles. Submit a fund-launch brief with the intended structure and operating appointments rather than requesting an undefined "end-to-end" package.

Build the operating stack around the fund

Do not issue an RFP titled simply "tokenize our fund." Define the fund, investors, regulated roles, records, cash flows and lifecycle events first. Then compare RWA tokenization platforms, institutional custody providers and fund administration and transfer agent providers as one operating stack.

An asset manager's launch gate: subscriptions must work in both directions

A fund launch can look complete when the first investor subscribes. The more revealing test is a rejected subscription followed by a redemption, a wallet change and a revised investor statement. The manager needs a coordinated operating model for all four, not simply a token that transfers successfully.

Treat alternatives as different operating models

A team comparing Securitize, Tokeny, Zoniqx, Taurus and KAIO should explain why each candidate belongs in the same request for proposal. A manager seeking software for its existing appointed operators has a different purchase from a manager seeking access to a coordinated service stack. A distribution relationship is another distinct decision.

For a smaller private-asset offering, include DigiShares or another relevant issuer-software option from the platform directory only when the proposed scope fits. Do not add every provider to create a longer list. Compare a narrower set against the same obligations and operating records.

First decide what the investor will actually own

The investor sees a token in a wallet. The manager sees a fund interest, an administrator's record and a set of dealing rules. Those descriptions need to refer to the same entitlement. Before discussing a network or portal, write one sentence explaining the claim represented by the token and identify the document that establishes it.

Structure to investigateWhat needs to be establishedOperating dependency
Direct tokenized fund interestThe token's relationship to the fund's issued shares or unitsAuthoritative register, investor rights and appointed record keeper
Feeder or access vehicleThe investor's interest in the vehicle and the vehicle's holding in the underlying fundTwo sets of dealing, reporting and service-provider arrangements
Tokenized share classRights and restrictions of the class relative to other classesClass-level NAV, allocations, distributions and transfer rules
Onchain strategy vaultWhat the vault share represents and how strategy assets are controlledContract permissions, valuation, withdrawals and strategy risk

These are questions for the proposed structure, not legal classifications for a named product. A tokenized feeder should not be described as a direct holding merely because the underlying manager's logo appears on an access page. A vault interface should not be treated as a complete legal fund structure without examining the actual arrangement.

The manager should identify rights to income, redemption proceeds, information and any voting or consent process. Record how those rights survive a wallet change, provider migration or interruption of the chain. The investor portal can explain the process, but it does not establish rights independently of the governing documents.

Match the system to the fund's dealing model

An open-ended fund with recurring dealing windows, a closed-ended private fund with capital calls and an evergreen private-credit vehicle have different servicing needs. Start with the actual policy rather than asking a platform to make them all behave like a continuously traded token.

For capital-call structures, define commitments, paid-in amounts, unfunded obligations, notices and default handling. A holder's token balance may not describe every outstanding obligation. For recurring subscriptions and redemptions, define cutoffs, valuation points, notice, settlement and any applicable restrictions. Ask how a request received outside the dealing window is represented and communicated.

For a feeder, map both the access vehicle and underlying fund calendars. A portal request may be accepted while the next underlying dealing window is still pending. Establish the owner of that interval, the record of outstanding requests and the communication investors receive. Avoid promising immediate access or exit based on the speed of a blockchain transaction.

Separate four meanings of liquidity

Tokenization can change how an interest is recorded and transferred. It does not, by itself, produce buyers or change the assets held by the fund. Ask the proposer to identify which of the following mechanisms is actually available for your offering.

  1. Permitted transfer: an eligible holder can transfer to another eligible holder under the applicable rules. This does not establish a willing buyer.
  2. Secondary trading: a permitted venue or bilateral process can support transactions, subject to its participants, instruments, permissions and actual demand.
  3. Fund redemption: the investor submits a request under the fund's dealing terms. Payment depends on those terms and the operating process, not only on burning a token.
  4. Separate liquidity arrangement: a third party may provide a facility or purchase mechanism under its own conditions. Determine who commits resources and when that commitment can change.

Request the relevant agreements and actual eligibility for each route. A supported exchange integration is not a listing, and a listing is not guaranteed execution. A displayed redemption button is not proof that a fund can meet every request immediately. Keep these distinctions visible in investor communications as well as the RFP.

Where assets are used as collateral, establish permitted use, custody, control and the path back to an unencumbered position. Test whether a pledged or restricted holding can accidentally enter a redemption or transfer workflow. Do not infer that a token usable in one protocol is acceptable collateral for every investor or counterparty.

NAV is a controlled input, not a blockchain price feed

Identify who calculates NAV, who approves it, which class and dealing period it covers, and which system publishes the approved version. A platform displaying NAV is not necessarily the administrator calculating it. A continuously changing market quote is not automatically the fund's approved dealing NAV.

Record valuation time, approval time, publication time and the investor requests assigned to that valuation. Make stale or missing NAV visible. Demonstrate that the system does not silently use an earlier figure for a new dealing period. If an estimate is permitted in the operating model, distinguish it from the final approved record and state what later reconciliation is required.

When NAV is corrected, retain the original version, reason, approver and affected subscriptions, redemptions or statements. Ask how the administrator and portal identify which investors are affected. The technical ability to edit a value should not let an operator bypass the fund's correction process.

For several share classes or currencies, verify identifiers and allocation rules separately. Do not rely on a fund-level total to prove that each investor's class-level record is correct. Include rounding differences, reinvested distributions and rejected payments in reconciliation. Assign responsibility for any difference instead of hiding it in an unexplained adjustment.

Worked example: reconcile 100 units through the full lifecycle

Use fictional records with a one-to-one token/unit mapping, a single class, no treasury-held units and no fees or rounding. This is a quantity-control exercise, not an investment or valuation example. Your real structure may require a different reconciliation policy.

EventRequired recordTotal outstanding units after the event
Opening holdingApproved register and corresponding token supply show 100 units100
New subscription allocates 20 unitsAccepted instruction, confirmed funding and authorized issuance agree120
Existing holder transfers 30 units to another eligible holderTransfer and holder records agree; no new units are issued120
Recipient submits a request to redeem 15 unitsRequest is pending; distinguish restricted/reserved units from completed cancellation120 pending the agreed completion event
Redemption completes and 15 units are canceled under the agreed policyPayment evidence, cancellation and administrator register reconcile105

After the transfer, the original holder has 70 units, the new subscriber 20 and the recipient 30. After the recipient's completed 15-unit redemption, those holdings are 70, 20 and 15, totaling 105. The identity of holders changes during the transfer; total supply does not.

Now deliberately break the sequence. Remove funding confirmation before issuance, delay the redemption payment, import a duplicated subscription instruction and interrupt the chain-event feed. Ask what is blocked, what remains pending and which team owns the exception. Do not make the records agree by treating an unconfirmed payment as final.

Reconcile at an agreed snapshot, including timing differences. An onchain transaction can be visible while an administrator import is pending. Preserve the discrepancy with its owner and expected resolution rather than overwriting one system to match the other. For omnibus custody or multiple wallets per investor, reconcile beneficial-holder records through the relevant account mapping rather than comparing addresses directly with investor counts.

Token standards, governance and chain selection

For an ERC-3643 implementation, the published standard includes identity and compliance interfaces, privileged roles, recovery, freezing and mint/burn functions. That creates specific governance questions: who controls each role, how agents are changed and what actions require independent approval? A standard name is not evidence that every configured rule or operator process is correct.

Ask which controls are enforced by the contract and which depend on the portal, administrator or external identity provider. Test the same prohibited action through every supported path. Record the version of the deployed contract and its configuration, not merely the protocol named in the sales material. Have qualified advisers approve the legal effect of the resulting records.

Choose a network around custody support, investor wallet requirements, operational monitoring, governance and lifecycle functions. Verify the actual production environment. Availability on a test network or for a simple transfer does not prove that issuance, restrictions, recovery and reporting work together on your target network.

If several networks represent the same economic interest, identify the supply-control policy and authoritative record. Test interrupted migration, duplicate event delivery and temporary unavailability. Do not assume that adding a bridge preserves restrictions or that identical token names establish one coordinated register. The fund needs an operating plan for inconsistent cross-network state.

Build, license or coordinate services?

A custom build can be appropriate when the manager needs distinctive workflows or deep integration with existing operators. It also requires ownership of testing, upgrades, monitoring and continuity. Packaged software can shorten some implementation work, but it still needs approved documents, integrations and appointed service providers. Coordinated services may reduce handoffs while introducing several agreements under one brand.

Write the responsibility map before choosing among these routes. Include who owns contracts and administrative keys, who approves releases, how the administrator receives events and what happens when a provider stops supporting a component. A white-label interface should not conceal who can actually resolve an investor's problem.

Keep sensitive investor information outside public records unless the approved design specifically requires disclosure. Review what personal data is collected, where it is retained, who can access it and how identity attestations are revoked or replaced. A privacy policy is not a substitute for testing what the deployed application exposes.

Finally, rehearse transition. Export holder identities and account mappings, holdings, documents, eligibility decisions, contract addresses, administrative permissions, historical events and pending obligations. Agree how the appointed operators continue servicing investors while software changes. The fund's continuity should not depend on one person retaining access to one dashboard.

FAQ

Which providers offer end-to-end tokenized fund launch for asset managers?

Compare the full operating arrangement rather than a single brand. This guide considers Securitize, Tokeny, Zoniqx, Taurus and KAIO as different starting points. Require named responsibility for issuance, administration, investor records, custody, distribution and redemptions.

How do tokenization platforms differ on transfer agency, compliance and distribution?

Some provide software used by appointed operators; others connect separate service entities or investor-access networks. Identify who maintains the authoritative register, approves eligibility and distributes the offering. Do not infer these mandates from a token contract or integration list.

Does tokenizing a fund make it liquid?

No. Transfer permissions, a secondary venue, eligible buyers and the fund's redemption terms are separate matters. Confirm actual access, dealing windows, notice periods, restrictions and payment responsibilities in the offering documents and operating agreements.

Which chains do fund tokenization platforms support?

Confirm the exact network, token standard, deployment environment and lifecycle functions for the proposed product. A supported chain does not establish availability of investor eligibility, custody, redemptions or administrator integration on that chain.

How long does it take to launch a tokenized fund?

The critical path includes legal structure, service appointments, investor eligibility, data integration, testing and operational approval. Ask for milestone dependencies and acceptance criteria rather than relying on an issuer-software deployment estimate.

What should an asset manager ask a tokenization platform before signing?

Ask who owns each record and operating responsibility, what is excluded, how subscriptions and redemptions reconcile, how exceptions and wallet recovery work, and what can be exported or migrated. Obtain written scope from every required service provider.

Is a tokenized feeder the same as a direct interest in the underlying fund?

Not necessarily. Identify the security or contractual interest the token represents, its issuer, the vehicle's underlying holding and the investor's rights. A feeder or access vehicle can add its own dealing, reporting and service-provider dependencies.

How should fund token supply reconcile to the administrator's register?

Define the authoritative ownership record and reconcile opening units, approved issuances, transfers and cancellations to closing holdings. Track pending requests and treasury-held units separately under the agreed model. Investigate breaks instead of assuming an onchain balance alone establishes legal ownership.
