Vendor Comparisons

KYC and AML Providers Compared for Crypto and Tokenization

Compare identity, KYB, wallet screening, monitoring and Travel Rule providers for crypto and tokenization, with product boundaries and practical buyer tests.

Reviewed and updated by FluidRWA · August 15, 2026 · Editorial update November 26, 2026

Short answer

Compare KYC and AML providers by the decisions they support: identity and business verification, name screening, wallet exposure, transaction monitoring and Travel Rule data exchange. These are complementary services, not interchangeable products. Confirm the proposed modules, source integrations, market coverage and review responsibilities before testing the complete customer journey.

The investor is approved. Why is the payment still waiting?

A new investor has completed identity checks. Their company appears in a registry. The deposit arrives, yet the operations team cannot release it: the wallet alert has not been reviewed and the transfer information does not match the beneficiary record. Every dashboard shows part of the story. Nobody has the complete decision.

That is the problem a useful KYC and AML comparison should solve. Buying a bigger collection of checks does not automatically connect the customer, company, wallet and payment. The right stack gives each decision an owner, makes uncertainty visible and lets a legitimate customer recover when something goes wrong.

This guide compares the service boundaries and evaluation questions behind twenty established providers. It is not a ranking based on a controlled performance test. For a broader starting shortlist, use our crypto KYC and AML buyer guide. Here, the focus is what each component does, what it does not establish and how the components work together.

KYC, KYB, fraud prevention and AML overlap, but they are not synonyms. Identity proofing concerns the applicant and evidence; monitoring concerns events and changes over time. A Travel Rule connection concerns transfer information and counterparties. Your responsible institution determines the policy and applicable obligations. A software product does not take over those responsibilities merely by including a compliance module.

How the provider proposals differ

Alloy

Alloy describes a decisioning and orchestration engine connecting identity, fraud and other data sources, with configurable customer journeys and policy testing. Its materials include versioning, audit records and rollback. Evaluate the selected sources and the decision strategy together rather than treating the platform as a single verification database. Ask who maintains each connector, how open cases behave after a policy change and which evidence reaches your own systems. For deeper identity-workflow detail, see Persona vs Alloy vs Socure.

AU10TIX

AU10TIX's Identity Verification Suite describes document capture and extraction, biometric matching, liveness and identity-data checks. Its AML Screening materials describe sanctions, PEP and adverse-media screening with rescreening. Database rescreening should not be confused with tracing activity on a blockchain. Ask which identity and screening components are enabled for your applicants, what triggers review and which separate wallet-risk or transfer-messaging services your operating model still requires.

Chainalysis

Chainalysis KYT describes transaction monitoring, configurable behavioral and exposure alerts, and review workflows. Address screening and deeper investigations are distinct product considerations. Ask the proposed team to show direct and indirect exposure evidence for your actual assets, then demonstrate how an alert becomes a documented disposition. Confirm which monitoring, screening and investigation tools your analysts can access. A blockchain risk assessment is not a substitute for customer document verification or permission to accept a transaction.

ComplyAdvantage

ComplyAdvantage Mesh documentation separates person/company screening, rescreening, payment screening, transaction monitoring, customer risk scoring and case management. That is useful when comparing financial-crime workflows, but it should not be presented as proof of document authenticity or biometric identity. Ask which applications and datasets are enabled, which customer and transaction attributes you must supply and how the reviewer distinguishes a possible match from a resolved concern. Confirm how updates trigger reviews of existing customers.

Crystal Intelligence

Crystal Expert describes wallet screening, monitoring, investigations and evidence exports. Its product material distinguishes APIs with different history-retention behavior, an important operational detail when you need to reconstruct an earlier check. Crystal also distinguishes its risk-assessment role from Travel Rule information exchange performed through partners. Ask which API preserves the required evidence, which messaging service is involved and how the combined workflow links a transfer decision to the underlying assessment.

Elliptic

Elliptic Lens is described as a workspace combining wallet screening, transaction screening, monitoring and case handling with configurable risk rules. Evaluate the proposed rules, asset coverage and logged evidence rather than assuming every event can be assessed in the same way. Ask how analysts move from an alert to the relevant transaction and exposure context, how changed intelligence triggers another review and which export lets your team explain the earlier decision outside the workspace.

Entrust / Onfido

Entrust's identity-verification documentation describes Studio workflows that combine document, biometric, data and fraud tasks, using SDK capture or a hosted link. Available tasks depend on account enablement, workflow activation and SDK compatibility. Ask the provider to demonstrate your actual capture route and supported task versions. Identity-workflow orchestration alone does not establish blockchain analytics or transfer messaging. Pin down how retry, step-up and review outcomes reach the application before comparing this with a broader monitoring platform.

Jumio

Jumio describes identity verification and separate AML Screening capabilities. Screening can use extracted identity details or supplied data; its result is not the same as document verification. Ask how the proposed journey joins those steps, which search configuration applies and how incomplete screening is represented. Test a supported identity followed by a screening alert and a failed screening request. Those outcomes should not all become the same rejection message for the customer or the support team.

Middesk

Middesk's business-verification documentation covers entity information and associated-person due diligence. Its documented KYC implementation uses Socure, with capabilities depending on the configured modules and required inputs. That makes the business-to-person handoff a useful demonstration case. Ask which business jurisdictions and entity types are supported, which person checks are enabled and how an unresolved individual result affects the overall company review. Do not assume that a business-information match completes every ownership and identity check.

Notabene

Notabene describes transfer-authorization and Travel Rule workflows involving counterparty identification, due diligence, data exchange and policy checks. Its integration documentation also accommodates internal checks and external screening services. Ask which services are included in your setup, which API version applies and how beneficiary information is compared with your existing customer records. Demonstrate a transfer waiting on a separate check, then reconcile the result without accidentally releasing the payment before the responsible system authorizes it.

Persona

Persona's documentation distinguishes an Inquiry from individual Verifications, Accounts, Cases and event-driven Workflows. That model helps frame configurable onboarding and review, but the buyer still needs to define the final product decision. Ask how repeated attempts connect to one applicant, how cases are corrected and which records can be exported. Confirm your integration route and downstream status handling. Do not treat a completed capture step as proof that every screening or eligibility requirement has been resolved.

Sardine

Sardine's transaction-monitoring materials describe configurable rules, historical testing, customer-risk context and linked-entity investigations. Its broader fraud context is relevant when evaluating payment and onboarding behavior together. Ask which identity, device, payment and counterparty inputs your proposed deployment actually uses. A relationship graph does not automatically establish proprietary cross-chain attribution. Confirm the upstream intelligence sources, permitted automated actions and human-review responsibilities instead of assuming that every risk signal comes from the same underlying system.

Scorechain

Scorechain distinguishes point-in-time wallet screening from ongoing transaction monitoring, with configurable scenarios and case handling. Actions such as blocking or freezing depend on integrations with custody or internal systems; an analytics response does not itself control the assets. Ask which connectors support your intended action, who authorizes it and how a failed action is reconciled. Demonstrate the path from an exposure alert through review to the receiving system, preserving the rule and evidence behind the outcome.

Sumsub

Sumsub documents identity and business verification, transaction monitoring, Travel Rule and case-management areas. Its blockchain-intelligence documentation distinguishes managed connections from bring-your-own-key arrangements, depending on the underlying provider. One interface therefore does not mean one native intelligence source or one identical support boundary. Ask which provider, taxonomy, chain coverage and integration model the proposal uses, and how source failures appear in the workflow. Compare the enabled configuration rather than the broad platform feature list.

Sygna

Sygna Bridge describes API-based originator and beneficiary information exchange. Hub and Gate present different operating approaches, while official materials identify external blockchain-analytics and sanctions integrations. Ask which current services your proposal relies on, who operates each connection and how protocol compatibility is demonstrated for your counterparties. Compare an API-driven journey with a browser-operated process in terms of review responsibility, reconciliation and evidence retention. Neither approach should be assumed to inherit every capability of every listed integration.

TRM Labs

TRM Wallet Screening describes pre-transaction address-risk assessment, configurable risk settings and attribution context. Its product page distinguishes that service from ongoing Transaction Monitoring. Ask the team to demonstrate your asset and exposure scenarios in both stages, including a new alert after an initially accepted check. Confirm what source and confidence information analysts can inspect, how notes and investigations connect to the customer record and which actions remain your institution's responsibility.

Trulioo

Trulioo describes person verification, business verification and AML screening as related but distinct services. Identity-data matching, document checks, ownership mapping and screening should be scoped separately in the proposal. Ask which sources and verification routes cover your actual markets, how missing or conflicting ownership information is handled and what match evidence is available to reviewers. A connected owner record does not automatically establish that person's eligibility for your offering or resolve every screening question.

Unit21

Unit21's monitoring and case-management materials describe data ingestion, configurable detection rules, testing, linked-entity analysis and investigation records. Its case workflow leaves final decisions and approvals with the compliance team. Ask which transaction, customer, device and external signals you must supply, how rule changes are tested and how analysts inspect and export evidence. Monitoring and investigation capabilities should not be mistaken for a standalone document-and-selfie onboarding service or assumed proprietary blockchain attribution.

Veriff

Veriff's session documentation distinguishes approved, declined, resubmission, expired and abandoned outcomes, with review available under the relevant arrangement. Those distinctions matter for applicant recovery and support. Ask which outcomes your integration receives, how the backend retrieves the authoritative result and when a fresh session is required. Test an interrupted journey and a correctable capture issue separately from a policy rejection. A completed verification session does not automatically finish the rest of your monitoring or eligibility workflow.

VerifyVASP

VerifyVASP presents TravelRule information exchange alongside products for identity matching and self-hosted wallet control checks. Those are separate questions: whether transfer information matches, whether an address is controlled and whether the transfer meets policy. Ask which checks are native, partner-supplied or operated by your own team. Test mismatched names, unsupported wallets, unavailable counterparties and incomplete exchanges. Do not infer that wallet-control evidence alone provides a complete customer identity or blockchain-risk assessment.

Verification methods: ask what is actually being proved

An extracted name is not the same as an authenticated document. A database match is not proof that the person using the device controls the matched identity. A selfie match is not a complete financial-crime investigation. Keep evidence collection, evidence validation and applicant verification distinct when reviewing a proposed workflow.

MethodUseful questionImportant limitation to examine
Document capture and OCRCan the applicant submit a readable supported document?Extraction alone does not establish authenticity
Document authenticity checksWhich manipulation and consistency checks apply to this version?Coverage can differ across document versions and capture routes
NFC or chip readingIs the exact document and device combination supported?Not every applicant has a compatible chip or phone
Database verificationWhich source and attributes are matched?Thin files, old addresses and matching ambiguity need recovery
Government eID or bank identityWhat attributes and assurance does the method convey?Availability and acceptance are market- and product-dependent
Biometric and liveness checksWhich person-to-evidence and attack checks are performed?Presentation-attack testing is not blanket injection protection
Reusable credential or attestationWho issued it, what does it prove and who accepts it?Expiry, revocation and binding still need evaluation

For fraud-defense testing, distinguish a physical presentation to a camera from digital content injected into a capture path. Ask for the scope of the relevant evaluation: product version, capture method, attack classes and exclusions. A logo or a single accuracy percentage cannot explain all of those boundaries. NIST SP 800-63A-4 is a useful identity-proofing reference, not a certification of any vendor listed here or a universal crypto compliance rule.

Use authorized synthetic attacks and test environments, not borrowed identity documents or unauthorized attempts against a production service. Also test legitimate applicants using an older phone, denied camera permission, low connectivity or a non-Latin name. A secure workflow should have a defined path for correction and proportionate review, not endless resubmission.

Coverage: build a country and method sheet

Give every finalist the same launch-population sheet. Include applicant residence, document issuer and version, language, device, permitted method and required output. For businesses, add incorporation country, legal form, registry source and ownership depth. Country totals cannot substitute for that information. Neither can a map showing where a vendor has customers.

For a launch involving India, for example, ask which proposed local identity-data methods are supported, what access and consent arrangements apply and which alternatives exist for your applicant population. Have the responsible advisers confirm applicability to your activity. Do not assume a vendor's ability to retrieve a local identifier means your business is entitled to use that route or that it satisfies every obligation.

Apply the same discipline elsewhere: an identity-wallet integration, a local registry connection and a document-checking SDK answer different questions. Separate technical availability from the accepting institution's policy. Keep future or roadmap support out of the launch baseline until it is actually available and accepted.

KYB and enhanced review: follow the ownership gaps

Start with a business whose trading name differs from its legal name. Add an intermediate holding company and an owner in another jurisdiction. Ask the vendor to show the retrieved entity record, evidence date, declared owners and the unresolved links. Incorporation status does not establish the complete ownership picture or authority to act for the company.

Record whether ownership information was supplied by the applicant, retrieved from a source or assessed by an analyst. A visually complete diagram can still contain unsupported links. Require the decision record to preserve missing information, reviewer actions and the reason the case could proceed or remain on hold under your policy.

Where enhanced review is applicable, define the source-of-funds or source-of-wealth information required, who assesses it and what triggers refresh. Uploading a document is not the same as reviewing its relevance and provenance. Keep those responsibilities separate from ordinary entity matching and avoid requesting sensitive information without an approved purpose and handling process.

Wallet screening: compare evidence, not colored risk badges

A wallet-risk result becomes useful when an analyst can understand the relevant exposure. Ask for the exact chain and asset, address format, attribution source, assessment time, direct or indirect relationship and rule that produced the alert. Unsupported assets, missing data and no detected exposure should not collapse into the same green status.

Two vendors may use different entity labels, risk categories and exposure methods. Do not average their scores or declare one wrong just because the colors differ. Reconstruct a sample path and compare the evidence, supported scope and your own decision policy. Retain the response and relevant source references so a later attribution change does not make an earlier decision impossible to explain.

A node or RPC service helps your application detect blockchain activity. That is different from attributing counterparties or assessing financial-crime exposure. Test the complete deposit sequence: event detection, confirmation handling, risk request, review assignment and release decision. A detected deposit is not automatically a screened deposit. Repeated event delivery must not create duplicate cases or release the same balance twice.

Ongoing monitoring also needs an owner. Decide what happens when a previously accepted address receives new intelligence or changes behavior. Specify which customer and transaction records are affected, whether a new review is required and how corrections reach downstream systems. A one-time screening result should not quietly become a permanent statement about an address.

Travel Rule: demonstrate an exception, not just a connection

Wallet risk assessment, counterparty due diligence and exchanging transfer information are separate tasks. A vendor may help with one or several. Ask which platform discovers the counterparty, which transmits the information, which validates the response and which system decides whether the transfer can proceed.

Use a synthetic transfer with a counterparty outside the preferred connection path. Then try missing beneficiary information, a rejected message, corrected data and a delayed acknowledgment. Observe whether the application distinguishes a message awaiting action from a rejected transfer. Confirm how each state reconciles with the payment record and who can authorize the next step.

Ask about supported message schemas and protocol versions, including IVMS101 where relevant to the proposed route. A common data model does not, by itself, establish connectivity to every counterparty. Likewise, a partner logo does not prove the configured service handles a particular corridor, asset or exception. Keep the sending institution's obligations and acceptance policy separate from the messaging product's capabilities.

For self-hosted wallets, document the applicable process and permitted evidence with the responsible compliance team. Do not apply a universal transfer threshold or assume a signature, wallet-risk response and identity credential are interchangeable proofs. Preserve only the information required by the approved process and protect transfer data in transit and at rest.

Three operating models to compare

ModelPotential operational benefitResponsibility to make explicit
Integrated platformOne workflow and fewer interface handoffsWhich functions still depend on external sources and separate entitlements
Orchestrated specialist stackChoice of sources and independent decision policyConnector maintenance, incompatible taxonomies and support escalation
Verification embedded in a payment serviceIdentity linked to that provider's payment journeyWhether evidence and outcomes are portable outside that service

Do not assume an identity check embedded in a payment provider can serve every investor, wallet or asset workflow. Similarly, do not assume that connecting a specialist through an orchestrator transfers ownership of every incident to the orchestrator. Require a named operator, support boundary and evidence handoff for each component.

Confirm the actual deployment arrangement: vendor-hosted SaaS, a dedicated environment or customer-managed infrastructure if offered. Ask who patches software, manages keys, scales the service and investigates incidents. A particular deployment option must be confirmed for the selected product, not inferred from a vendor's enterprise positioning.

Do not mark an exercise complete because a vendor says implementation can handle it. Retain the demonstrated outcome, the written commitment or the unresolved dependency. A useful procurement record distinguishes demonstrated behavior from work still required before launch. Set stop criteria and an owner for remediation before moving live traffic.

Measure outcomes without rewarding the wrong denominator

Consider a constructed monitoring example: 1,000 transfers enter the workflow, 100 produce alerts, 60 alerts are closed without escalation, 20 are escalated and 20 remain open. The alert rate is 10%. It would be wrong to call all 60 closed alerts false positives without reliable labels, or to treat all 20 escalations as confirmed wrongdoing. These are workflow dispositions, not ground truth.

Compare alert volume, reviewed cases, supported outcome labels, unresolved backlog and time to a usable decision separately. Report the observation window and exclusions. A lower alert rate may reflect improved rules, missing input data or unsupported activity. A faster review may simply defer work to customer support. Inspect the cases behind the headline.

For onboarding, separate starts, completed journeys, approvals and later labelled outcomes. Compare by market, document, device and customer type where appropriate and lawfully assessed. For monitoring, include known relevant scenarios and review effort, not just automatic approvals. Use the same inputs, rule scope and evaluation window across finalists.

Evidence, privacy and assurance before signing off

Ask for the exact assurance document and its scope rather than collecting trust logos. Record the service, legal entity, reporting period, exclusions and relevant subservice organizations. An information-security assessment, a presentation-attack test and a privacy obligation answer different questions. None should be relabelled as blanket regulatory approval or proof that every workflow is secure.

Map the information each party receives: original documents, extracted attributes, biometrics, risk signals, review notes or status references. Specify role-based access, subprocessor responsibilities, retention, deletion and permitted uses. Test an export containing an open investigation and a corrected decision, not only an approved customer row. Ensure migration preserves the history needed to understand pending cases.

Reusable identity deserves the same discipline. Check the issuer, relying-party acceptance, evidence scope, expiry, revocation and binding to the person or account. An identity wallet is not the same as a crypto wallet. A credential proving an attribute does not necessarily establish offering eligibility or permission for a later transfer. Decide what happens when the credential expires or the customer changes wallets.

Tokenization: connect the decision to the asset workflow

A fund subscription, private-credit investment and real-estate offering can require different eligibility and operating controls. Agree the applicable rules with the responsible institution and advisers. Then map which system controls onboarding, investor records, wallet permissions, custody handoffs and transfer restrictions. Do not leave an approval trapped in a verification dashboard.

Test an investor with multiple wallets, a company whose owner changes and a previously accepted investor whose review expires. The receiving system should distinguish an expired review from a new adverse finding. Preserve the earlier decision and identify the permitted next action. Keep personal identity documents off public-chain records and out of general analytics URLs and logs.

Pass only the minimum necessary status and reference into the asset workflow. Confirm how status changes propagate, who reconciles failures and how the team proves which policy governed a transfer. Our ongoing investor recheck guide covers that lifecycle; the screening comparison examines matching and review in more detail.

A practical shortlist brief you can use

Write one brief before requesting demonstrations. State the customer types, launch markets, legal entities, asset networks and permitted verification methods. Name the systems that receive decisions and the people who own policy, integrations and review. List mandatory requirements separately from preferences; a failure on a mandatory control should not disappear inside an attractive average score.

Ask every finalist to return the same five things: a module-and-source map, a completed coverage sheet, a proposed decision flow, responses to the acceptance exercises and a support/data-responsibility plan. Mark each response as documented, demonstrated or still requiring agreement. Those labels describe the procurement evidence, not a public trust rating of the company.

Use your own priorities if you apply weights. A wallet platform may emphasize exposure assessment and transfer handling; an issuer onboarding corporate investors may emphasize ownership and asset eligibility. There is no universal percentage allocation that proves the right choice. Record why each criterion matters and why the evidence supports the final decision.

Product scope in this comparison was checked against current vendor documentation on November 26, 2026. Described capabilities are starting points for a scoped proposal, not measured superiority on your customers. The objective is a stack your team can operate, explain and recover, including on the difficult days.

FAQ

Which KYC and AML provider is best for a crypto business?

There is no universal winner. Compare the specific identity, business, screening, monitoring and transfer-data services your product needs, then test the proposed configuration on the same approved scenarios.

Does wallet screening replace KYC?

No. Wallet screening assesses blockchain exposure. It does not by itself verify the person or business controlling an address, establish eligibility for an offering, or complete every required customer check.

Can one platform cover identity checks and blockchain monitoring?

A platform may present several services in one workflow while using external data providers for some checks. Confirm which modules are native, partner-delivered or separately enabled, and who supports each dependency.

What is the difference between Travel Rule screening and messaging?

Counterparty or wallet risk assessment is different from exchanging originator and beneficiary information. Test discovery, supported counterparties, data validation, rejected messages and reconciliation for the actual transfer route.

What documents should a KYC or KYB provider support?

Specify the actual applicant country, document issuer, document version and permitted verification method. Business checks also need the relevant entity and ownership evidence. There is no single document checklist suitable for every product and jurisdiction.

How should buyers assess liveness and deepfake protection?

Request evidence for the exact product and integration, distinguishing presentation attacks from digital injection. Use authorized synthetic test scenarios and evaluate recovery for legitimate applicants, not just a headline accuracy figure.

Can verified identity be reused for tokenized assets?

Reuse depends on who accepts the evidence, its scope, expiry and revocation, and the current product policy. Verified identity does not automatically establish eligibility for every asset or permission for every transfer.

What should a compliance vendor pilot demonstrate?

Demonstrate normal and interrupted onboarding, ownership gaps, screening matches, wallet risk, transfer-data exceptions, changed decisions and evidence export. Keep technical failures, policy rejections, unknown outcomes and confirmed fraud separate.
