CLARITY Act Timeline: What Passed, What Stalled, and What It Means for RWA

The CLARITY Act's verified timeline, current status and potential effects on exchanges, custody, tokenized funds, credit, stablecoins and blockchain infrastructure.

Reviewed by FluidRWA Research Team · October 25, 2026

Short answer

As of October 25, 2026, the US CLARITY Act is not law. The House passed H.R. 3633 on July 17, 2025, by 294 to 134. Senate committees then worked on different market-structure proposals, but on September 15, 2026, the Senate rejected a procedural vote to begin floor consideration, 49 to 50. Tokenized-asset businesses should follow the bill without treating its proposed rules as effective today.

The status in one minute

The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act of 2025, usually called the CLARITY Act, is a US proposal for the regulation of digital commodities and their intermediaries. The House passed H.R. 3633 on July 17, 2025. It did not become law then. On September 15, 2026, the Senate rejected cloture on the motion to proceed to H.R. 3633, with 49 in favor, 50 against and one senator not voting. That vote concerned whether to advance consideration, not final passage.

The distinction matters. A platform cannot rely on proposed registration routes, exemptions or definitions as if they were in force. The House-passed text is the best source for what the House approved; Senate committee material records what senators subsequently proposed. Neither is a substitute for current law or advice on a particular product.

What is in the House-passed bill?

The 258-page House-passed text is the reference version for this summary. Its table of contents separates the bill into six titles:

Part of the House-passed textWhat a reader will find
Title I: definitions and implementationProposed definitions, rulemaking, expedited intermediary registration and savings provisions.
Title II: offers and salesProposed treatment of certain investment-contract-related digital commodity transactions, disclosures and blockchain maturity requirements.
Title III: SEC intermediariesProposed rules touching securities intermediaries, alternative trading systems, recordkeeping and custody-related activities.
Title IV: CFTC intermediariesProposed regulation of digital commodity exchanges, brokers, dealers and qualified custodians.
Title V: innovationProposed innovation offices and studies, including on decentralized finance and market infrastructure.
Title VI: anti-CBDC provisionsThe House-passed version also contains the Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act.

This is an outline of the proposal, not a statement that any of these provisions now governs a business. The precise definition, exception and effective-date language matters; do not base an implementation decision on a headline or this table.

The definition that matters most for RWA

In section 101 of the House-passed bill, a digital commodity is linked to a blockchain system and derives, or is reasonably expected to derive, value from that system's use. The definition then has exclusions. Among them are certain securities and instruments representing an interest in an issuer's revenues, profits, obligations, debts or assets; creditor rights; and rights to interest or repayment of principal. The details and exceptions in the statutory wording matter, so this is a reading aid, not a classification opinion.

That is why a network token and a token recording a loan claim should not be put in the same regulatory bucket merely because both move onchain. A tokenized Treasury fund share is not a digital commodity simply because its transfer record uses a blockchain. Conversely, the bill's exclusion language should not be treated as a ready-made legal answer for every structure. The actual instrument and transaction still require review.

Why the Senate versions require separate reading

The House bill is one legislative text. Senate Banking and Senate Agriculture worked on related but distinct material. The November 2025 Agriculture draft discussed a CFTC spot-market framework, customer protections and coordination with the SEC, while acknowledging unresolved language. Senate Banking then released revised text in May 2026 for its own process.

When a commentary says "the CLARITY Act would require X," check which version and section it means. A proposal can change in committee, on the floor or between chambers. The September 2026 Senate vote does not itself tell us which provisions would have survived into any future law.

What does this mean for tokenized real-world assets?

For RWA teams, the first question remains what legal claim the token represents. A token may record a fund interest, equity, debt, a beneficial interest or another right. Calling it an RWA or placing it on a blockchain does not answer whether an offer, sale or secondary transaction involves a security. The House text's treatment of digital commodities and its securities-law provisions are reasons to examine the actual rights and transaction, not a shortcut around that examination.

This is FluidRWA's practical reading of the bill's scope, not a legal classification of any asset. Before choosing issuance, transfer-agent, custody, trading or distribution vendors, prepare a short product map for counsel:

  1. Identify the legal instrument, issuer and rights held by token owners.
  2. Map primary issuance, redemption and secondary transfers separately.
  3. Name each intermediary and who controls assets and customer records at each step.
  4. Record investor location, marketing geography and any transfer restrictions.
  5. Note which assumptions depend on a *proposed* CLARITY provision rather than current law.

Exchanges, broker-dealers and trading venues

The House bill proposes a CFTC framework for digital commodity exchanges, brokers and dealers (Title IV). It also contains provisions for SEC-registered intermediaries dealing in digital commodities and permitted payment stablecoins (Title III). That is not a simple SEC-to-CFTC handover for every token. A venue listing a tokenized security alongside a network token would need to determine the nature of *each* instrument and the activity being performed. Any future dual-registration or alternative-trading-system path would depend on final legislation and implementing rules.

For a buyer evaluating a trading provider today, request an asset-by-asset listing policy, the identity of the licensed operating entity, custody arrangements, surveillance responsibilities and the treatment of assets that cannot be listed on the same venue. Those questions remain useful whatever happens to CLARITY.

Banks, custodians and investors' assets

Section 405 of the House version proposes conditions for a “qualified digital asset custodian” in the contemplated digital-commodity system, including supervisory routes. Section 402 addresses the use of qualified custodians by futures commission merchants. These provisions should not be advertised as a current certification for a vendor. Nor do they say that any wallet with good key management is a qualified custodian.

For institutions, separate three layers in the diligence pack: custody of underlying fund assets, custody of tokenized securities or commodities, and control of an investor's wallet. They may involve different firms and legal duties. Ask for the exact entity, supervisor, account structure, asset segregation and insolvency treatment for each layer.

Funds, private credit and real estate

This is where headlines about “regulatory clarity for all RWAs” are most misleading. The House-passed definition's exclusions speak directly to interests in assets or debts, creditor rights and repayment of interest or principal. A tokenized private-credit note, fund share or property interest may therefore raise securities and property-law questions that CLARITY's proposed digital-commodity framework does not resolve. This is an inference from the definition in section 101, not a ruling on any named product.

An asset manager should still document the issuer and legal vehicle, the investor's enforceable claim, cash-flow and redemption rights, administrator and transfer-agent responsibilities, and whether the token record is the legal register or a technical mirror. For real estate, also identify the land or entity interest and the jurisdiction of the underlying property. Those decisions are more immediate than speculative changes to digital-commodity registration.

Stablecoin payments and settlement

The House text expressly distinguishes permitted payment stablecoins from digital commodities and contains provisions for certain transactions through brokers, exchanges or alternative trading systems. It should not be read as a complete rulebook for stablecoin issuance, redemption, reserves or cross-border payments. A payment company must examine its actual fiat and stablecoin flows and the separate laws applying to them. The proposed market-structure bill does not, by itself, confer permission to operate a ramp or payment service.

DeFi, wallets and infrastructure providers

Section 109 proposes limited treatment for certain non-controlling developers and providers of blockchain services. Importantly, the same section preserves the possibility of other money-transmission or Bank Secrecy Act treatment based on conduct outside its narrow scope. Titles III and IV also contain DeFi-related exclusions. None should be paraphrased as “DeFi is exempt.”

For a protocol or wallet provider, the fact pattern matters: who can change smart-contract rules, freeze transfers, hold keys, custody assets or operate a user-facing intermediary? A genuinely non-controlling software publisher and a firm exercising discretionary control are not the same case. Final statutory language and agency implementation would matter if a bill passes.

International firms with US customers

CLARITY is a US legislative proposal. An overseas issuer, custodian or platform cannot assume it is irrelevant just because its headquarters is elsewhere, nor that every overseas activity would be covered. The relevant questions are where investors are solicited, where transactions occur, which US-facing intermediaries participate and what existing US law already applies. That is a jurisdictional assessment for counsel, not a conclusion supplied by the bill's title.

What changes now, and what waits?

Now: nothing in H.R. 3633 has taken effect merely because the House passed it or a Senate committee published draft text. Keep operating against current applicable law and contracts. Map the rights and responsibilities above so future changes can be assessed quickly.

If a bill eventually passes: compare the *enacted* version against the House version, read its effective-date and rulemaking provisions, and update each product classification and vendor contract. For example, the House-passed Title II proposes a 360-day effective-date rule with a later date for some provisions requiring final rules (section 206). That clock has not started for this unenacted bill.

What should readers watch next?

Watch for a new Senate floor action, revised committee text, an amendment to H.R. 3633, or a compromise version approved by both chambers. Congress.gov's page may lag a fresh procedural vote; cross-check the Senate's own roll call before repeating a status claim.

FAQ

Has the CLARITY Act passed?

It passed the US House on July 17, 2025, but has not passed both chambers or become law as of October 25, 2026. A September 15, 2026 Senate cloture vote on the motion to proceed failed 49 to 50.

Did the Senate reject the CLARITY Act itself?

The September 15 vote rejected cloture on the motion to proceed to H.R. 3633. That was a procedural vote about taking up the bill, not a final vote on its passage.

Are tokenized securities automatically digital commodities under CLARITY?

No such automatic conclusion follows from putting an asset onchain. The House-passed bill addresses digital commodities and also preserves securities-law concepts. Classification of a tokenized share, fund interest or debt claim depends on its rights and transaction structure; seek qualified US legal advice.

Is the Senate draft the same as the House-passed bill?

No. Senate committees published discussion drafts and later text during negotiations. Those proposals should not be quoted as if they were the House-passed text or enacted law.

What should a tokenization team do now?

Document the legal rights represented by each token, the offering and trading arrangements, custody and customer protections, and the jurisdictions involved. Ask counsel to assess current law. Monitor Congress for any new text and votes rather than planning against a proposal as if it were effective.

Would CLARITY make tokenized real estate or private credit a digital commodity?

Not automatically. The House-passed definition excludes certain securities and claims tied to an issuer's assets, debts, interest or principal. Real-estate and private-credit structures require an instrument-by-instrument legal assessment; the bill is not law.
