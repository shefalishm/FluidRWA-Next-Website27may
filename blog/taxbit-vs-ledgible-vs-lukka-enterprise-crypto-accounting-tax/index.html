TaxBit: separate the accounting package from the tax package

TaxBit's Accounting Suite help page describes transaction standardization, lot-level calculations and journal mappings to ERP systems. Its current reporting platform separately describes form generation and filing after customer approval. Do not assume the quote for one includes the other, or that the older Accounting Suite documentation establishes today's commercial availability for your entity.

The cost-basis product describes acquisition lots, account-level methods and flags for incomplete history. Ask the demo team to trace a disposal back to its acquisition and fee records. Your adviser should approve the applicable method; a list of software methods does not establish which one you may use.

For information reporting, name the required forms, jurisdiction, recipient delivery, approval and correction steps. Confirm who submits each output and who owns rejected filings. No accuracy or audit-outcome guarantee follows from these product descriptions.

Pricing, contracted coverage and implementation effort were not established in this review.

Ledgible: put reconciliation at the centre of the demo

Ledgible's accounting product describes a digital-asset subledger, gain/loss reporting and OFX exports for ledger import. Its accounting help guide also describes ledger connections and mappings; confirm the current connector and sync direction rather than treating a file export as a live integration.

Its separate Tax Information Reporting solution describes aggregation, taxpayer mapping and form generation, including connection to existing reporting systems. That differs from a firm's own accounting close and from the professional tax product. Ask where cost-basis calculation ends and form preparation, filing, delivery and corrections begin.

The reconciliation guide compares calculated and source balances by asset. Bring a history with a known difference and ask the team to explain it. Check whether corrections remain visible to the reviewer. This is a proposed buyer test, not a result FluidRWA obtained.

Confirm the exact source and history supported, what happens when credentials expire and what export your accountant receives. A named integration does not establish completeness for every transaction type.

Lukka: decide whether you need a broader data foundation

Lukka's enterprise materials describe data management alongside tax and business reporting. Its reporting page separately lists financial statements, tax forms, NAV reporting and cash/crypto reconciliation services. Confirm which are software outputs and which require a service engagement.

For example, a fund administrator may need consistent inputs for accounting, valuation review and reporting rather than a standalone tax output. This is a buyer scenario, not a claim that Lukka has implemented your particular workflow.

Ask which fields arrive from each source, how they are normalized, how changes are recorded and how outputs reach your systems. Verify which valuation data and licences are included; public descriptions do not settle commercial entitlements.

Cost basis and reporting need different evidence

A complete balance is not proof that acquisition history is complete. Include a transfer with missing basis, a fee paid in another asset and a later correction in the trial. Ask the provider to retain the source identifier, acquisition lot, selected method, valuation timestamp and reviewer decision behind the result.

For regulatory reporting, add a sample reportable account and a rejected or corrected output. Have your advisers define the expected form, jurisdiction and treatment. Ask who handles onboarding documentation, taxpayer matching, approval, filing acknowledgements and recipient delivery. Do not assume every listed regime is included or that software determines your legal obligations.

Which cost-basis software fits your buyer type in 2026?

Imagine that your closing balance matches the custodian, but the gain on one disposal looks wrong. The missing fact may be a purchase made before a wallet migration. A faster calculation engine will not recreate that evidence. The useful purchase is a system that shows where the history ends, preserves the correction and carries the reviewed result into the next report.

That is why cost-basis automation should be evaluated by buyer and output, rather than by the largest advertised integration count.

Its pricing page distinguishes professional tax pricing from custom enterprise accounting pricing; the latter varies with users, wallets, exchange connections, volume and ledger integrations. Do not apply a professional-tax starting price to an enterprise implementation.

GainsKeeper for Digital Assets is aimed at digital-asset reporting workflows, including tax lots and Form 1099-DA. It must not be confused with GainsKeeper for Individual Investors: the official discontinuation notice says that individual product's final supported tax year was 2024 and expressly separates it from other Wolters Kluwer products.

CoinTracker's pricing page offers personal tax plans differentiated by transaction allowance and services. Its free portfolio tier does not include downloadable tax forms. A portfolio tracker is therefore not a free substitute for every reporting output.

A reproducible cost-basis acceptance test

Use a fictional, permissioned test dataset before exposing customer records. For an illustrative arithmetic check, suppose a company acquires two units for a total of $200, transfers both to another controlled wallet, and later sells one for $150. Ignore fees for this simplified calculation. If the approved policy carries the original $100 unit basis through that internal transfer, the gain on the sale is $50, not $150. This is a software test assumption, not a determination of tax treatment.

Now remove the acquisition record and run the same import. A useful exception workflow shows the missing evidence rather than presenting a confident zero-basis result. Restore the acquisition, approve the correction, rerun the disposal and compare the old and new export. A reviewer should be able to explain the change without reconstructing it from screenshots.

Form 1099-DA: ask the right period-specific question

The IRS's 2026 Form 1099-DA instructions distinguish covered from noncovered digital assets and describe different basis-reporting treatment, including optional reporting methods for certain transactions. Do not assume that every broker statement supplies complete acquisition history or that one rule applies to every token and account. Have a qualified adviser define your entity's requirements, then ask the vendor to demonstrate those requirements using the correct reporting year.

Keep calculation, filing and recipient delivery as separate acceptance gates. A correct lot calculation does not prove that a form was submitted, accepted, delivered or corrected. Retain the relevant account, approval, submission and correction identifiers alongside the reported amount.

Security evidence belongs in the proposal, not a logo comparison

Ask for the actual security report or trust-center materials covering the purchased service, examination period, relevant exceptions, subprocessors and customer responsibilities. Request read-only source permissions where feasible, retention controls, a deletion process and a recovery exercise. A SOC badge alone does not establish that your connector, entity or implementation is within scope.

Separate calculation approval from filing approval

A calculation can be internally consistent while still being the wrong report for the entity or period. Have one acceptance step for source and lot reconstruction, then another for the reporting output. Name the reviewer responsible for taxpayer information, exceptions, recipient delivery and corrections.

The IRS instructions for Form 1099-DA for 2026 distinguish covered and noncovered digital assets. They are a specific US reporting reference, not a global tax rule or a reason to assume every source statement contains acquisition history. A qualified adviser should decide the requirements for the relevant entity and activity.

FAQ

How do you automate cost basis tracking for digital assets at scale?

Inventory the sources, preserve raw records, map ownership, match transfers and apply an approved lot-selection policy. Route missing acquisitions and ambiguous events to review. Automation should reproduce the calculation and its evidence, not silently replace missing history with an assumption.

What should enterprise crypto tax reporting solutions be evaluated on?

Evaluate source completeness, lot provenance, approved methods, corrections, entity and year scope, reporting outputs, access controls and exportability. Accounting close and customer information reporting are different deliverables and should have separate acceptance tests.

How do enterprise crypto tax systems calculate cost basis?

They associate disposals with acquisition lots using configured methods and transaction classifications, fees and valuations. Correct results depend on complete evidence and applicable policy. Have the responsible tax adviser approve methods and treatment.

How do you automate reporting for internal audit and digital asset tax compliance in a repeatable process?

Freeze a source snapshot, version classifications and calculation rules, assign reviewers, preserve approvals and generate traceable outputs. Rerun the same input and explain every change when a correction or rule update creates a different result.

Which enterprise crypto tax reporting vendors integrate with ERP and accounting systems?

TaxBit, Ledgible and Lukka offer different accounting, data and reporting scopes. Request the exact ERP connector or file format, supported version, posting direction, reconciliation controls and owner of failed imports rather than treating integration as a yes-or-no feature.

What data does a crypto tax tool need from custodians and exchanges?

It needs account ownership, transactions, opening positions and acquisition history, asset and network identifiers, timestamps, quantities, fees and valuations. Reporting workflows may also need taxpayer and account classifications. A closing balance or blockchain transaction alone is not complete basis evidence.
