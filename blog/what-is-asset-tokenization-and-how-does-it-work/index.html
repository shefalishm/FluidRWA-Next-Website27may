What Is Asset Tokenization and How Does It Work?

A plain-English look at what an asset token represents, how issuance and transfers work, and which records and rights matter beyond the blockchain.

Reviewed by FluidRWA Research Team · October 24, 2026

Short answer

Asset tokenization puts a digital record of an asset or a claim on a blockchain. A token can represent a fund share, a security or another defined interest, but the token alone does not tell you what you legally own. The product documents and ownership records do that.

FluidRWA research brief

Asset-tokenization operating model

Asset tokenization creates a digital representation of rights, but the legal and operational system determines what those rights mean. A token without enforceable structure and servicing is incomplete.

Think about a fund share

Suppose a fund issues shares to approved investors and records those shares as tokens. An investor may see a token in a wallet and transfer it under the fund's rules. The fund still needs offering documents, investor checks, an ownership record and a way to process subscriptions and redemptions. The token does not replace those jobs.

The Bank for International Settlements describes tokenization as representing assets on a digital platform. For tokenized securities, the US Securities and Exchange Commission distinguishes securities tokenized by or for an issuer from tokens created by an unrelated third party. Holder rights can differ. Ask what the token represents before asking which blockchain it uses.

How the process works

  1. Define the asset and rights. The issuer records what a holder is entitled to in the governing documents.
  2. Set up the product. The team decides who may buy, hold, transfer or redeem it.
  3. Issue the token. The technology creates a digital record tied to the ownership or entitlement model.
  4. Run the lifecycle. Investors are onboarded, transfers are checked, and income, reporting and redemptions are handled over time.
  5. Keep records aligned. Token balances, issuer records and payment records must agree where the structure requires them to.

The exact sequence varies by asset and jurisdiction. A tokenized fund share, a warehouse receipt and a token linked to a third party's security do not give holders identical rights.

Where it may help

Shared digital records can make ownership and transfer events easier to follow. Rules in the system may also automate parts of a workflow. The BIS review of tokenization describes possible efficiency and transparency gains, alongside new dependencies and risks. Those gains need to be demonstrated for the actual product; a token by itself does not create liquidity or reduce every cost.

FAQ

Does a token automatically give me ownership of the underlying asset?

No. Read the documents that define the holder's rights and the issuer's obligations. Different token structures can give holders different claims.

Does every blockchain transfer become a valid legal transfer?

That depends on the asset, governing documents and product rules. The issuer may restrict eligible holders and transfers.

What should I check first?

Identify the asset, issuer, holder rights, official ownership record and how investors enter and exit.
